\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Electronic markets are feedback systems in which adaptive trading algorithms respond to prices that their own trades move. This thesis asks whether controlling their risk requires identifying the algorithms. In the models studied here, observable dynamics reveal agents only through a merged measure of their memories and weights. Recovering individual cohorts can be statistically expensive, whereas safety depends on a small number of aggregate functionals. With a declared memory bound, a summed-response certificate controls worst-case loss under bounded shocks and supports an uncertainty-aware pre-trade guard. Without an appropriate memory bound, finite observations cannot certify that loss uniformly.

The third paper develops a common functional framework. It gives a coefficient-energy upper bound, a finite-depth obstruction based on best polynomial approximation, and a long-memory impossibility result. A proposed uniform matching lower bound fails as stated; a counterexample and a weaker fixed-atom bound delimit what remains open. Safety extends to sector-bounded nonlinear agents with signed, time-varying weights and memories. A weight-drift bound gives a guaranteed certificate lifetime, rather than an exact prediction of when the loss budget will first be exceeded. A positive-system comparison yields a sharp multi-asset bound, with registered numerical validation and its remaining formalization boundary stated separately. A threshold-game counterexample shows that convex costs and an exact sensitivity certificate alone do not ensure strategic equilibrium existence. Lean proofs cover the finite-horizon safety induction, deterministic lifetime calculations and certificate-radius arithmetic.

The existing empirical papers find that class disclosures reveal memories that aggregate data leave ambiguous, and that amplification statistics forecast adverse excursions in some thin markets. This is conditional, reduced-form evidence; liquidation-mechanism tests fail. The thesis-level ground-truth, prospective-mechanism and learning-agent studies remain pending. Co-authorship permissions and submission decisions remain human tasks.
