\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Electronic markets are feedback systems: adaptive trading agents, now increasingly AI agents, respond to prices that their own trades move. Supervisors respond by asking to see inside the agents. This thesis asks whether that knowledge is needed for safety and answers that, in the closed-loop markets studied here, it is not: certify, don't identify. Prices reveal the agents only through a merged measure of their memories and weights. Separating two agents whose memory rates differ by $\delta$ costs trading capital of order $\delta^{-4}$, separating $K$ agents costs experiments of order $(\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)}$, and with general memory accuracy improves only as $1/\log N$. Safety depends on the agents only through one number, the summed price response, which is certified with $\sigma^2\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon)$ experiments whatever their number and spacing, from passive data at a cost of order one over the stability margin, and not at all without a bound on memory; a pre-trade guard built on the certificate is the largest safe admission rule. A general theory explains the pattern: every question is a functional of the merged measure, and its exact cost is the value of a polynomial approximation problem; the safety question sits on the boundary of the parametric regime and pays one logarithm. Applied to AI agents, the theory shows that a shared model lowers the stability margin in proportion to its adoption share, and it gives an instrument that needs from deployers only a memory bound and a sensitivity bound: certify the margin daily from public data and trigger on its trend. In a registered simulation the trend warned about two weeks before instability in 96\% of markets with no false alarm, while thresholds computed from a model of the agents erred by nearly a factor of two, in opposite directions for learning rules and for language-model desks. In data, prices leave the agents' memories unrestricted while disclosed trader-class positions restrict them; with enough days public data decide the safety question in new markets whose agents they do not identify; and in thin books the safety statistic forecasts the size of adverse excursions beyond a full volatility baseline. The evidence is narrower than the theory: decided margins rest mainly on price reversion in markets far from the boundary, the margin does not forecast adverse moves, and a guard estimated from public data does no better than a volatility limit. Of the 23 primary hypotheses of the thesis's registered tests, 11 pass; all failures are reported.

\chapter*{Use of AI tools}

Every number in the thesis is produced by code that was executed and archived, and an automated check compares the printed numbers with the files that produced them. The candidate reviewed all content and takes responsibility for it.
