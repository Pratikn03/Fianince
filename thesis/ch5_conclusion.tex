\chapter{Conclusion and open problems}\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis asked whether safety in a market with adaptive agents requires knowing who the agents are. For the markets studied here, it does not. Prices reveal the agents only through a merged measure of their memories and weights, and recovering the agents from that measure costs trading capital of order $\delta^{-4}$, experiments of order $(\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)}$, and with general memory an accuracy that improves only logarithmically (Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}). Safety depends on the agents only through their total feedback weight, which is certified at the parametric rate under a memory bound and cannot be certified without one; a guard built on that certificate is the largest safe admission rule (Chapter~\ref{ch:p2}). The theory of Chapter~\ref{ch:p3} places these results in one picture, in which the cost of a question is set by its behaviour at long memory, and shows that the safety bound needs only the agents' total sensitivity, even when their rules are nonlinear and their memories and weights change, and that learning makes certificates perishable at a computable rate. In data, disclosed trader classes reveal what prices do not, and registered tests replicate this on new days. The safety statistic forecasts adverse excursions in thin Binance books but not on spot venues, and the registered tests find that, with enough days, public data decide the safety question in new markets whose agents they do not identify, but that this decision rests mainly on price reversion, that the summed response itself is no more precise than the separation of memories, and that a guard estimated from public data does not reduce realized tail losses.

The policy message is direct. Supervisors who want to control transient risk should ask for the information that certifies safety, a bound on memory and on total sensitivity, renewed as often as the agents learn, rather than for the identities of the agents. Identity-based regimes may serve other purposes, such as enforcement and attribution, but in the model of this thesis they are neither necessary nor efficient for safety. The evidence adds a practical point: passive observation of prices delivers neither kind of knowledge reliably, and disclosure is what makes either attainable.

\subsection{What the earlier evidence establishes}\label{sec:ev_existing}

\paragraph{Prices do not reveal the agents.} In 30 Binance perpetuals, trader-class positions restrict a persistence that prices and flow leave essentially unrestricted (with a one-hour price-only kernel the intervals are 28 to 124 times wider; at a matched horizon the gap is smaller but present in every symbol, on the original and on 30 new symbols, Section~\ref{sec:evidence}), and in a pre-registered test the CFTC's trader-class reports do the same in all eight regulated futures markets examined (Sections~\ref{p1:sec:disc_data} and~\ref{p1:sec:cot}). This is the identification half of the thesis in data: the merged measure leaves class memories essentially unrestricted.

\paragraph{The safety statistic carries information.} In thin Binance books, six-hour estimates of the amplification margin forecast the size of the next hour's adverse excursion out of sample in every pre-registered forecasting test, beyond a full volatility-forecasting baseline and VPIN; the effect is about a fifth as large in thin US stocks, negligible on Bybit and absent on Coinbase (Section~\ref{p2:sec:thin} and Table~\ref{p2:tab:alltests}).

\paragraph{What it does not establish.} The forecasting result is reduced form. Tests of a leverage or liquidation mechanism fail (Section~\ref{p2:sec:p22}), and the follow-up that points to slow reversion is close to a check of how the statistic is built (Section~\ref{p2:sec:p25}). As a risk control, the certified guard improves on a static limit but not, at matched exposure, on a volatility cap (Section~\ref{p2:sec:p24}). And from passive data the certificate itself is not always precise: in Paper~1's registered test the summed response was significantly different from zero in only 5 of 30 thin markets (Section~\ref{p1:sec:disc_data}). Test~E1 checks the thesis's central claim directly.

\subsection{Test E1: certify versus identify on fresh data}\label{sec:ev_e1}

\paragraph{Design.} The summed response is a linear functional of the merged measure and the theory says it is cheap to certify, while the agents' memories are expensive to identify. In passive data both come from one regression of five-minute order flow on twelve lagged returns: the sum of its coefficients is $\hat\Phi$, and two exponentials fitted to the coefficients give the price-only memory rates. Published trader-class positions give class-tagged rates. Thirty Binance USD-M perpetuals on 105 fresh dates (the 4th, 5th, 18th, 19th and 23rd of each month, January 2025 to September 2026); each symbol gives $I_{\rm cert}$, the width of the day-bootstrap interval for $\hat\Phi$ relative to $|\hat\Phi|$, and $I_{\rm id}$, the same for the separation of the price-only rates. Primary~1: $I_{\rm id}\ge10\,I_{\rm cert}$; Primary~2: price-only rate intervals at least ten times wider than class-tagged ones; each in at least 75\% of symbols with a Holm-corrected binomial test. The specification (hash \texttt{0b6c31c0\ldots}) called Primary~1 a risky prediction.

\paragraph{Result.} Primary~2 passed: price-only intervals were at least ten times wider than class-tagged ones in all \EonePtwo{} symbols, with a median ratio of \EoneMedianRatio{}, a replication of Paper~1 on 105 new days. Primary~1 failed: it held in \EonePone{} of \EoneSymbols{} symbols (Holm-adjusted $p=\EonePholmOne$). The median $I_{\rm cert}$ was \EoneMedianIcert{} and the median $I_{\rm id}$ \EoneMedianIid{}: relative to its size, the certificate was no more precise than the separation of memories, and the summed response was significantly different from zero in only \EonePhiIdentified{} symbols.

\paragraph{Post hoc reading.} There are two reasons, one of design and one of substance. The price-only rates were fitted on $[0,0.99]$, so the interval for their separation cannot be wider than $0.99$. In \EoneSaturated{} of the 30 symbols at least one rate interval reaches a bound of the fit, which means the data do not restrict that rate, yet $I_{\rm id}$ is then capped near one: \EoneSmallIid{} symbols have $I_{\rm id}<2.5$. The relative-width measure therefore cannot register the failure of identification that Primary~2 shows directly. And $\hat\Phi$ is small relative to its sampling error in passive five-minute data, as Paper~1's earlier test had warned. Neither reason contradicts the theorems, which concern controlled experiments; both limit what passive data can show. The test failed as registered.

\subsection{Test E2: a prospective test of the mechanism}\label{sec:ev_e2}

\paragraph{Idea.} Every pre-registered test so far used archived data. A prospective test removes any doubt about the order of registration and data: the data do not exist when the specification is written.

\paragraph{Design.} The original specification (pushed on 23 October 2026) asks, for 20 thin perpetuals over at least three months after registration, whether six-hour $\hat C_3$ forecasts next-hour liquidation volume beyond volatility, impact and lagged liquidations (Primary~1), and whether adding realized next-hour liquidations to the excursion model removes at least half of $\hat C_3$'s forecasting gain (Primary~2). Binance's forced-liquidation stream, which it named as the source, cannot be recorded from the United States, and the public archive contains no liquidation files. An addendum, pushed on its own before any post-registration file was downloaded, keeps the hypotheses and replaces liquidation volume by forced deleveraging measured from open interest: the open interest destroyed during five-minute moves larger than twice their recent standard deviation. It also fixes the window (24 October 2026 to 31 January 2027), the split into 50 training and 50 test dates, and the estimators, which are those of the thin-book tests of Section~\ref{p2:sec:thin}. The price of the substitution is stated in advance: E2 can no longer test the liquidation channel with direct liquidation data, which was its advantage over the open-interest proxy of Section~\ref{p2:sec:p22}.

\paragraph{Status.} The analysis code and its notebook are committed with the addendum and refuse to run before 2 February 2027.

\subsection{Tests D1, G1, V1, C2, O1 and C3}\label{sec:ev_more}

\paragraph{D1: does tagging restore the parametric rate as data grow?} Theorem~\ref{p1:thm:discvalue} predicts that class-tagged data make memory an ordinary estimation problem, while prices and flow alone leave it far harder. On 58 untouched dates for the same 30 perpetuals, with nested subsamples of 14, 28 and all days, Primary~1 asked that the class-tagged interval width fall with a slope between $-0.75$ and $-0.25$ in $\log n$, and Primary~2 that the advantage of tagging grow with slope at least $0.2$, each in at least 75\% of symbols. \emph{Result.} Primary~2 passed: the advantage grew in \DonePtwo{} of \DoneSymbols{} symbols, exactly the number required (median slope \DoneSlopeRatio{}, Holm-adjusted $p=\DonePholmTwo$). Primary~1 failed: \DonePone{} of \DoneSymbols{} symbols were in range, two short of the \DoneNeed{} required, although its binomial $p$-value was $\DonePholmOne$; class-tagged intervals shrank more slowly than the parametric rate, with a median slope of \DoneSlopeTag{} against $-1/2$. \emph{Post hoc reading.} The 58 dates span 21 months. If class memory drifts, days from new periods do not shrink an interval at the parametric rate, and the specification named this as the informative failure. The exploration of Section~\ref{sec:ev_explore} finds such drift.

\paragraph{G1: does the guard beat a volatility limit in stress hours?} Chapter~\ref{ch:p2} finds no advantage over a volatility limit at matched exposure over all hours. The theory concerns worst-case loss, so G1 asked the question in stress hours, those whose six-hour volatility is in the top decile of the symbol's training distribution, for 20 thin perpetuals. \emph{Result.} Both primaries failed. The training data chose $\theta=\GoneTheta$: the training tail-loss ratio rose steadily with the weight on amplification, from \GoneThetaTLRzero{} at $\theta=0$ to \GoneThetaTLRone{} at $\theta=1$, so the amplification-adjusted limit coincided with the volatility limit and the difference was exactly zero. In \GoneStressHours{} stress hours on \GoneStressDates{} test dates the guard's tail-loss ratio was \GoneGuardPct{} higher than the volatility limit's (log difference \GoneStressGuard{}, 95\% interval \GoneStressGuardCI{}, $p=\GoneStressP$). Over all \GoneAllHours{} test hours, a secondary comparison registered as a replication of Chapter~\ref{ch:p2}, it was \GoneAllRatio{} times higher (log difference \GoneAllGuard{}, interval \GoneAllGuardCI{}). \emph{Reading.} The guard's theorems bound worst-case loss under a certified operator; G1 shows that, estimated from six hours of public data and used to size positions, it does not reduce realized tail losses, in stress hours or otherwise. Post hoc: sizing divides by the product of two noisy six-hour estimates, which enlarges positions whenever the estimate is spuriously low.

\paragraph{V1: the thin-book effect on a new venue.} The thin-book forecasting result is large on Binance, small in US stocks and absent on Coinbase. A thin-book explanation predicts an effect in the thinnest pairs of any venue; an explanation specific to leveraged perpetuals predicts none on spot. V1 tested the depth-free statistic on Kraken spot, a venue never used in this project. \emph{Result.} Primary~1 failed. Of the \VonePairs{} thin pairs fixed by the universe rule, only \VoneIncluded{} met the inclusion rule of 12 valid training and 20 valid test days; on their \VoneTestHours{} test hours the statistic added nothing out of sample ($\Delta R^2=\VoneDeltaRtwo$ points, 95\% interval \VoneDRtwoCI{}, Clark--West $p=\VoneCWp$), and Primary~2 was not tested. With three pairs the test had little power. Its registered reading holds: the result is consistent with the Coinbase result and with an explanation specific to perpetual futures, and it identifies no mechanism.

\paragraph{C2: certify the margin, identify the memories, on new symbols.} Designed after the exploration of Section~\ref{sec:ev_explore}, C2 drew 30 Binance perpetuals that appear nowhere else in this project (seeded random order, written down before any data were downloaded) and used 105 dates each. The margin $M=1-a-\kappa\Phi$ was estimated with the displacement kernel; it counted as decided if its day-bootstrap interval excluded zero, and the memories counted as identified under the corrected criterion. A third primary repeated the horizon-matched comparison of Pilot~3: the width of the interval of log half-life for class-tagged persistence against the price-only slow memory from a 96-lag (eight-hour) kernel. \emph{Result.} Primary~1 failed by one symbol: the margin was decided in \CtwoDecided{} of \CtwoSymbols{}, all on the safe side, against \CtwoNeed{} required (Holm-adjusted $p=\CtwoPholmOne$). Primary~2 passed: \CtwoB{} symbols had a decided margin and unidentified memories, \CtwoC{} the reverse ($p=\CtwoPtwoP$), and the memories were identified in \CtwoIdentified{} symbol. Primary~3 passed: the tagged interval was narrower in \CtwoPthreeTighter{} of \CtwoPthreeSymbols{} evaluable symbols ($p=\CtwoPthreeP$), by a median factor of \CtwoRatio{} and at least \CtwoMinRatio{}, and the price-only interval reached a bound of the fit in \CtwoBound{}; the pilot's 7.5 was replicated. \emph{What the secondaries show.} As in the pilot, the decision rests mainly on reversion: without the feedback term it was the same in \CtwoSameNoFb{} of \CtwoSymbols{}, and the feedback was contrarian in \CtwoFbNeg{}. The registered jackknife correction changes the reading of Primary~3. The uncorrected tagged persistence is \CtwoTagR{} per five minutes (half-life \CtwoTagHL{} hours); corrected for the within-day bias, the median is \CtwoJackR{}, and the corrected interval lies below one in only \CtwoJackBelow{} of \CtwoClassFits{} class fits. Within a day, class positioning ratios are close to a random walk, and the hours-long half-lives of the uncorrected estimator are largely produced by removing each day's mean. Primary~3 stands as registered, but what it shows is that positioning data restrict a persistence parameter that prices cannot bound, not that they measure a learning memory: slow turnover of positions produces the same persistence.

\paragraph{O1: do small perpetuals overshoot after scheduled releases?} Pilot~2b found that, after the 2025--2026 releases, the point estimates of the 120-second area were negative, an overshoot, in most symbols, and Proposition~\ref{prop:overshoot}, added before O1's result was known, says that a genuine overshoot certifies contrarian feedback. O1 asked the question once, pooled across the pilot's 30 perpetuals with the releases resampled jointly, on the \OoneReleases{} CPI, Employment Situation and FOMC releases of 2022--2024, which the pilot had not used. \emph{Validity.} Both registered conditions held: the pooled pre-release drift was \OonePre{} of the long-run response (interval \OonePreCI{}), and bitcoin's moves at pseudo-events twenty minutes earlier were \OonePseudo\% as large as at the releases. \emph{Result.} The primary failed: the pooled area was $\bar A_{120}=\OoneAbar$ seconds (interval \OoneCI{}, one-sided $p=\OoneP$), positive, not negative. Only \OoneNeg{} of \OoneSymbols{} symbols had a negative point estimate, none significantly. \emph{What the secondaries show.} In 2022--2024 the response had the shape the natural-experiment design assumes: a median \OoneShare\% of the long-run response (an altcoin move of \OoneBeta{} times bitcoin's) was in place within ten seconds, against 94\% in the pilot's 2025--2026 releases, and the rest followed gradually. The two-sided horizon-limited bound $|A_{120}|<60$ seconds held in \OoneTwoSided{} of \OoneSymbols{} symbols, and the memories were identified in \OoneIdentified{}. \emph{Reading.} The overshoot of the pilot is not a stable feature of these markets: it does not appear in the earlier releases, so it is either specific to 2025--2026 or noise, and these data give no evidence of contrarian feedback. Post hoc, and not tested here: the share absorbed within ten seconds rose from about a third to almost all between the two periods, consistent with faster algorithmic arbitrage between bitcoin and small perpetuals.

\paragraph{C3: is the margin decided once the sample is long enough?} C2's first primary failed by one symbol with 105 days per symbol, while the theory says the certificate's precision improves at the parametric rate. C3 asked the same question once, on \CthreeDrawn{} further symbols never used in this project and 430 days each, a sample chosen in advance by a power calculation from C2's own per-symbol results. The estimators were C2's; one rule was added after C2 drew a symbol whose price did not move: a day is valid only if at least half of its five-minute returns are non-zero, and a symbol is evaluable with at least 200 valid days. \emph{Result.} Both primaries passed. \CthreeExcluded{} of the \CthreeDrawn{} symbols had fewer than 200 valid days and were excluded under that rule. In the remaining \CthreeSymbols{}, with a median of \CthreeDays{} valid days, the margin was decided in \CthreeDecided{}, all on the safe side, against \CthreeNeed{} required (Holm-adjusted $p=\CthreePone$); \CthreeB{} symbols had a decided margin and unidentified memories and none the reverse ($p=\CthreePtwo$), and the memories were identified in \CthreeIdentified{}. Counting the excluded symbols as undecided, \CthreeDecided{} of \CthreeDrawn{} would still meet the 75\% threshold. \emph{What the secondaries show.} On each symbol's first 105 valid days the margin was decided in \CthreeFirst{} of \CthreeSymbols{}, so the gain came with the sample, as the theory predicts. The decision still rests mainly on reversion: without the feedback term it was the same in \CthreeSameNoFb{} of \CthreeSymbols{}, and the feedback was contrarian in \CthreeFbNeg{}. C3 shows that the safety question is decidable from public data in these markets while the agents are not identified; it does not show that the feedback term is measured precisely.

\subsection{What the tests change}\label{sec:ev_change}

The tests confirm the identification half of the thesis on new data: prices and order flow leave memories unrestricted, also at a matched horizon and on symbols never used before, while class positioning data restrict a persistence parameter that prices cannot bound, with an advantage that grows with the sample. Whether that persistence is a learning memory is not established: corrected for its within-day bias it is close to a random walk (C2). On two sets of new symbols the decision a guard needs was reached where the memories were not identified (\CtwoB{} symbols against \CtwoC{} in C2, \CthreeB{} against \CthreeC{} in C3), and with 430 days the margin was decided in \CthreeDecided{} of \CthreeSymbols{} symbols (C3), after falling one symbol short with 105 days (C2). This is the empirical form of the safety half, within limits: the decision rests mainly on reversion, so the data certify that these markets are far from the boundary without measuring the feedback precisely; the summed response itself was no more precise than the separation of memories (E1); and a guard estimated from public data did not reduce realized tail losses (G1). The theorems of Chapters~\ref{ch:p2} and~\ref{ch:p3} are untouched, because they concern declared contracts (reset experiments, exact windows, audited disclosure) and passive records are none of these. What changes is the claim the thesis makes about public data. Identification from prices fails, as predicted. Certification from prices works, with enough days, where the market is far from the boundary (C2, C3); near the boundary the feedback term would have to be measured, which passive data have not done. The information that makes either attainable is disclosure, or an outside shock that plays the role of an experiment (Proposition~\ref{prop:natural}).

\subsection{Exploration after the tests}\label{sec:ev_explore}

After the four tests, their downloaded days, which are spent for confirmation, were explored to find a design that public data can decide, so that the next confirmatory test can be fixed before its data are seen. Nothing in this subsection is confirmatory evidence; it is reported because it shapes what comes next.

\paragraph{The decision a guard needs is decidable; the separation of memories is not.} The theory's safety question is the sign of the margin $M=1-a-\kappa\Phi$ (equation~\eqref{eq:fw_S}). Estimated with the feedback kernel acting on the price displacement, as in Definition~\ref{def:cohort}, rather than on returns as in E1, the day-bootstrap interval for the margin excluded zero in \PilotMarginX{} of \PilotSymbols{} symbols, all on the safe side; with E1's return kernel it did in \PilotMarginRet{}. With the identification criterion corrected, so that a separation counts only if its interval excludes zero and neither rate interval touches a bound of the fit, memories were identified in \PilotIdentified{} of \PilotSymbols{}. On random subsamples the margin was decided in \PilotCurveTen{}, \PilotCurveTwenty{} and \PilotCurveForty{} symbols with 10, 20 and 40 days, and the memories in no more than \PilotIdMax{}.

\paragraph{The decision rests mostly on reversion.} The feedback term $\kappa\Phi$ was typically about a quarter of the size of the reversion term (median ratio \PilotFbRatio{}) and negative, that is contrarian, in \PilotFbNeg{} of \PilotSymbols{} symbols; with it removed the decision was the same in \PilotSame{} of \PilotSymbols{}. These markets are certifiably far from the boundary, and the data do not measure the feedback itself precisely. The sign matters: the non-negativity of Definition~\ref{def:cohort} excludes contrarian agents, a limitation Mansi Bhavsar pointed out in comments on an earlier draft, and here contrarian feedback is the common case.

\paragraph{Class memory drifts.} The top-position memory rate differed across calendar quarters (heterogeneity test at 5\%) in \PilotDrift{} of \PilotSymbols{} symbols, against 1.5 expected by chance. Within quarters, \PilotWithinIn{} of the tagged-width slopes fall in D1's range, against \PilotAcrossIn{} across the whole period, although the median slope within quarters (\PilotWithinSlope{}) is still flatter than $-1/2$.

\paragraph{Natural experiments: bitcoin moves are not outside shocks.} Proposition~\ref{prop:natural} turns a displacement that adds no order flow into a reset experiment. A first pilot used every one-minute bitcoin move as such a displacement of 30 small perpetuals. The altcoins absorbed a median \PtwoPassThrough\% of a move within the minute, but both placebos failed: the pre-move drift was consistent with zero in only \PtwoPreOK{} of 30 symbols and the reverse regression in \PtwoReverseOK{}. The altcoins slightly lead bitcoin, so an ordinary bitcoin move is not an outside shock to them, and the design was dropped.

\paragraph{Scheduled releases are outside shocks, and the response overshoots.} A second pilot used the \PtwobEvents{} US releases whose timing is fixed in advance and whose content is unknown until the release second: every CPI and Employment Situation release (8:30 ET) and every FOMC statement (2:00 ET) from January 2025 to October 2026, at one-second resolution. Here the design holds. Before the release the altcoins did not drift towards the coming news (the interval covered zero in \PtwobPreOK{} of \PtwobSymbols{} symbols, median size \PtwobPreSize\% of the full response), and at pseudo-events twenty minutes earlier bitcoin's moves were \PtwobPseudo\% as large as at the release. The response was not the gradual catch-up the margin estimator assumes. A median \PtwobShare\% of the long-run response (an altcoin move of \PtwobBeta{} times bitcoin's) was in place within ten seconds, and the point estimates then overshoot the long-run level and revert over minutes: the 120-second area $A_{120}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:horizon} is negative in \PtwobNegArea{} of \PtwobSymbols{} symbols, though its interval excludes zero in only \PtwobNegAreaSig{}. Three consequences follow. The registered-style reading ``upper bound of $A_{120}$ below 60 seconds'' holds in all \PtwobOneSided{} symbols, but only because the area is negative; the two-sided bound $|A_{120}|<60$ seconds holds in \PtwobTwoSided{}. The finite-horizon margin of Proposition~\ref{prop:natural}(iv) needs a positive area and can be computed in only \PtwobPosArea{} symbols. And the memories are identified in \PtwobIdentified{} of \PtwobSymbols{}, as the theory predicts. Interval widths shrink with the number of releases at slope \PtwobSlope{} in $\log n$, close to the parametric $-1/2$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:overshoot}, a genuine overshoot requires contrarian feedback, and the area up to the plateau should then turn positive; it is negative in \PtwobNegTotal{} of \PtwobSymbols{} symbols (interval below zero in \PtwobNegTotalSig{}), which, if confirmed, would mean that adjustment continues beyond the fifteen-minute window. Because all symbols share the same releases, the overshoot in \PtwobNegArea{} of \PtwobSymbols{} symbols is not \PtwobSymbols{} independent findings. The registered test of it, O1, on the 2022--2024 releases, found no overshoot (Section~\ref{sec:ev_more}).

\paragraph{The disclosure result at a matched horizon.} Paper~1's price-only route regresses five-minute flow on twelve lagged returns, a one-hour window, while class-tagged half-lives are several hours. A third pilot refits the price-only kernel with 12, 48 and 96 lags (one, four and eight hours) on the E1v2 data and compares both routes in one unit, the half-life. With twelve lags the price-only slow half-life is a median \PthreeHLtwelve{} minutes and below half an hour in \PthreeShortTwelve{} of 30 symbols: the one-hour kernel cannot see memories of hours. With 96 lags it is a median \PthreeHLninetysix{} hours, against a tagged median of \PthreeTagHL{} hours, and within a factor of two of the tagged value in \PthreeNearninetysix{} symbols. The tagged intervals stay narrower in all \PthreeTighter{} symbols at every horizon (smallest ratio \PthreeMinRatio{} at eight hours), but the median ratio of interval widths in log half-life falls from \PthreeRatioTwelve{} to \PthreeRatioFortyeight{} to \PthreeRatioNinetysix{} as the kernel lengthens, and the price-only interval still reaches a bound of the fit in \PthreeBoundNinetysix{} of 30 symbols at eight hours. The robust statement is therefore not a precision ratio but a failure of identification: at a matched horizon prices do not bound the slow memory in most symbols. Within-day demeaning biases the tagged rates down; the registered correction in C2 shows how much, and kernels longer than a day do not fit this design.

\paragraph{The learning curve and the value of the certificate.} A fourth pilot used C3's spent data to design a flagship test. Theorem~\ref{thm:passivecert} predicts that, with a longer record, more markets have a decided margin while their memories stay unidentified. On the first 30, 60, 120, 240 and all valid days of the \PfourSymbols{} evaluable symbols, the margin was decided in \PfourDecThirty{}, \PfourDecSixty{}, \PfourDecOneTwenty{}, \PfourDecTwoForty{} and \PfourDecAll{} (one symbol has fewer than 240 days), and the memories were identified in \PfourIdThirty{}, \PfourIdSixty{}, \PfourIdOneTwenty{}, \PfourIdTwoForty{} and \PfourIdAll{}. Across symbols this is the predicted pattern. Symbol by symbol it is not: the standardized margin, which the theorem says should grow like the square root of the sample, grew in only \PfourSlopePos{} of \PfourSymbols{} symbols (median slope \PfourSlope{} in $\log n$), because the estimated margin moves between 2025 and 2026; the stationarity behind the theorem fails at this horizon, as D1's drift already suggested. The pilot also asked whether the certificate carries information about risk. In rolling 20-day windows, a lower estimated margin went with a higher next-day realized variance (panel $t=\PfourTrv$; $t=\PfourTrvSixty$ with 60-day windows) and, more weakly, a larger adverse move ($t=\PfourTmae$), the effect coming from the reversion part of the margin rather than from feedback; but with a separate forecasting model per symbol, adding the margin lowered out-of-sample accuracy in every specification (change in $R^2$ from \PfourOosLo{} to \PfourOosHi{} points).

\paragraph{Next.} Tests C2, O1 and C3 were registered and run; their results are reported with the registered tests above. What remains open is the feedback term itself: every decision so far rests mainly on reversion, and a market near the boundary would need the feedback measured, from disclosure or from outside shocks.

\subsection{Withdrawn results}\label{sec:ev_withdrawn}

Two results reported in an earlier draft of this thesis are withdrawn. The first was presented as a run of Test~E1 in 38 markets. It did not estimate anything from fresh data: it read results archived from earlier tests and used hard-coded and invented intervals, and its design compared quantities in different units. The specification it ran under is superseded by the one above, and none of its numbers appears in this thesis. The second was a simulation of learning agents whose weights, by construction, could only increase; its ``realized lifetimes'' measured that construction and could not have fallen below the bound they were said to test. Section~\ref{sec:bl_ai} replaces it with a registered simulation in which weights follow the agents' trading profits. The code and output of both are kept in the repository, marked as withdrawn, so that the record is complete.

\subsection{Rules for every new test}\label{sec:ev_rules}

\begin{enumerate}
\item The specification, its hash and an unlock counter are pushed to the public repository in a commit of their own, so the platform records when they became public, before any test data are downloaded or recorded.
\item Each test has at most two primaries with a pre-stated pass rule and a pre-stated correction across them.
\item A failed primary is reported as failed. An explanation found afterwards is labelled post hoc, and any test that follows from it is labelled as registered after the failure.
\item Every number in the thesis is produced by code that writes a result file, and an automated check compares the printed numbers with those files.
\item A result found to be wrong after it was reported is withdrawn in the text, with the reason, and its files are kept and marked rather than deleted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Nonlinear questions.} Theorem~\ref{thm:dich}(iv) gives the exact cost of every linear question, and Proposition~\ref{prop:nonlinear} settles questions that depend on finitely many taps, such as the peak. Extend the exact theory to questions that depend nonlinearly on infinitely many taps, and make the link to the $1/\log N$ rate of identification (Corollary~\ref{cor:cases}(e)) a theorem rather than a reading of Chapter~\ref{ch:p1}'s proof.
\item \emph{Constants.} Theorem~\ref{thm:philog} gives explicit constants, but its lower constant is far below its upper one ($\PhilogLowB$ against $\PhilogUpB$ at $\bar r=0.9$). Find the limit of $\omega_L(\delta)/(\delta\sqrt{\log(1/\delta)})$; the computed brackets sit near the upper constant.
\item \emph{Minimal disclosure for safety.} Characterize the least informative disclosure under which a given safety question becomes certifiable from finite data. Theorem~\ref{thm:dich}(iii) says a memory bound is necessary for the summed response; is it sufficient in a sense that can be made operational?
\item \emph{Strategic agents.} Remark~\ref{rem:strategic_counterexample} refutes existence under a discontinuous admission rule. Determine sufficient assumptions for existence under a regularized guard and for the claimed comparison of equilibrium sensitivity.
\item \emph{Observational certification.} Develop finite-sample certificates from observational order-book data with dependence and non-stationarity, replacing the bootstrap diagnostics of Chapter~\ref{ch:p2}. Test~E1 found passive five-minute data far from the reset contract of Theorem~\ref{thm:dich}; natural experiments (Proposition~\ref{prop:natural}) are the nearest substitute, and their sampling theory under dependent shocks is open. Theorem~\ref{thm:passivecert} settles the cost of passive certification for economies with one closed-loop root; extending its upper bound to several cohorts with one dominant root (Remark~\ref{rem:passivecert}) would make C2 and C3 tests of a theorem in full.
\item \emph{The evidence.} Run Test~E2 after 2 February 2027; measure the feedback term itself, not only the margin, from disclosure or outside shocks; and extend the cohort model to contrarian agents, which the exploration finds to be common.
\end{enumerate}
