## 1. Executive Summary: What Is Novel, What Is Known, and What Is Applied

To ensure that the research contributions of Paper 1 and Paper 2 are defensible before top mathematical finance, econometrics, and financial economics referees, this audit rigorously demarcates:
1. **Prior Theoretical Foundations**: Known results in system identification, finite-mixture minimax estimation, $\ell_1$-optimal control, and market microstructure.
2. **Genuinely Original Contributions**: Exact mathematical theorems and financial-economic mechanisms established for the first time in this research program.
3. **Applied Control & Statistical Theory**: Standard mathematical principles correctly applied to financial limit order books.

The unifying thesis:
$$\boxed{ \text{Information and financial cost of strategy identification} \;\gg\; \text{Cost of certain safe financial decisions} }$$
is an **exact, quantifiable separation theorem** established under specified market and observation contracts, not an assertion of universal market truth.

## 3. Detailed Novelty Decomposition: What Is NOT Claimed

To protect academic credibility, the manuscripts must explicitly avoid claiming novelty for:
1. **The existence of transient amplification in non-normal linear systems**: Kreiss (1962) and Trefethen & Embree (2005) established pseudospectral transient growth decades ago. The contribution is identifying the specific economic order book mechanism ($C_2 > 0, C_3 > 0$) that creates non-normality in limit order books.
2. **The algebraic ill-conditioning of polynomial moment recovery**: Prony (1795) and Donoho (1992) established the difficulty of resolving near-colliding point sources. The contribution is deriving the conversion to executed market volume, committed margin capital, and closed-loop transfer function zeros.
3. **Control without identification as a generic mathematical philosophy**: van Waarde et al. (2020) formalized "data informativity" for linear system stabilization. The contribution is proving the exact scalar sufficiency of $\Phi$ for pre-trade financial admission in limit order books, characterizing the finite-window Markov–Krein geometry, and proving the quantitative sample separation $O(\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon))$ vs. $\Omega((\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)})$.
4. **Finite-sample guarantees from observational moving-block bootstraps**: Observational resampling on crypto perpetual futures does not provide a mathematical safety certificate; it is an empirical stress-testing heuristic.
