\begin{abstract}
\noindent When algorithmic execution replaces human market making, the closed-loop stability of limit order books depends on the coupling between order flow, price formation, and adaptive dealer inventory control. We prove that conventional eigenvalue criteria ($\rho(J) < 1$) are insufficient to guarantee solvency: non-normal closed-loop operators generate massive transient liquidity amplification that can force exchange liquidation long before asymptotic decay takes effect. We formulate the \textbf{Separation Principle between Strategy Identification and Financial Safety Certification}: while identifying hidden trading strategies requires resources scaling as $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$ in memory resolution $\delta$, certifying whether an admitted order flow is safe for clearing requires only evaluating an exact operator resolvent bound. We derive an exact, non-asymptotic depth condition based on the Kreiss resolvent constant ($\mathcal{K}(J)$) and synthesize the \textbf{Maximal Pre-Trade Resolvent Guard ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$)}, an optimal convex admission filter that maximizes cleared volume subject to provable forward solvency. In empirical evaluations across four canonical historical market crashes (May 2021 Flash Crash, May 2022 Terra/LUNA Collapse, November 2022 FTX Crisis, and March 2023 SVB Depeg) and an 8-asset cross-margined portfolio, the guard completely eliminates dealer liquidations ($0.0\%$ vs.\ $22.0\%$--$36.0\%$ unguarded) while preserving $91.6\%$ baseline depth. All 28 mathematical environments (445 named declarations) are formalized in Lean~4 with Mathlib, compiling with zero \texttt{sorry} and zero unapproved axioms.
\end{abstract}
