\paragraph{Research Question and Contribution.}
Financial markets are increasingly dominated by autonomous, learning algorithmic traders whose private internal architectures (memory kernels, recurrent states, and adaptive gains) are unobservable to market operators. A foundational regulatory and exchange-design dilemma has consequently emerged: \emph{Must an exchange identify the hidden trading algorithms operating on its order book in order to safeguard financial stability, or can it certify the solvency and safety of the market using coarse, aggregate observations alone?}

This paper establishes what we term the \textbf{Separation Principle between Strategy Identification and Financial Safety Certification}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{A Sharp Analytical Distinction:} We prove that while uniquely identifying hidden trading strategies requires astronomical observation windows, executed quantities scaling as $\Theta(\delta^{-2})$, and capital scaling as $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$, certifying whether an exchange or dealer can safely clear an admitted order flow requires only testing whether the aggregate operator transient amplification violates an exact scalar bound.
    \item \textbf{The Exact Depth Condition:} We establish an exact, non-asymptotic depth condition based on the Kreiss resolvent constant ($\mathcal{K}(J)$) of the closed-loop order-book system. We show that traditional eigenvalue-based stability criteria (spectral radius $\rho(J) < 1$) fail catastrophically in machine markets because non-normal feedback loops can amplify bounded order flow by orders of magnitude prior to asymptotic decay.
    \item \textbf{The Maximal Pre-Trade Resolvent Guard ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$):} We formulate the optimal pre-trade admission policy as a convex operator constraint that maximizes market liquidity subject to provable forward solvency. In empirical stress tests across four canonical historical market crashes (May 2021 Flash Crash, May 2022 Terra/LUNA, November 2022 FTX, and March 2023 SVB depeg) and an 8-asset cross-margined portfolio, the guard completely eliminates dealer liquidations ($0.0\%$ vs.\ $22.0\%$--$36.0\%$ unguarded) while preserving $91.6\%$ baseline depth.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why This Manuscript Fits \textit{The Journal of Finance}.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Original Market-Design Theory:} To our knowledge, this is the first financial economics paper to provide a formal mathematical bridge between non-normal dynamical systems (Kreiss resolvent theory) and exchange risk management.
    \item \textbf{Fair and Honest Empirical Benchmarking:} We benchmark our guard against five alternative policies (including volume caps, quadratic inventory penalties, and linear shrinkage) under matched training notional and matched risk budgets ($\mathrm{ES}_{95}$), demonstrating that the guard's true value lies in a $28.5\%$ reduction in peak catastrophic drawdowns.
