We are pleased to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Auditable Memory and Financial Identification Limits in Electronic Limit Order Books,''} for consideration for publication in \textit{Quantitative Economics}.

\paragraph{Research Question and Contribution.}
Econometric analysis of financial markets increasingly confronts autonomous trading systems that update their quotes based on private, stateful histories. This paper develops a rigorous econometric and information-theoretic framework for determining the fundamental limits of identifying hidden adaptive memory in electronic limit order books:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Minimax Sample Complexity of Mixture Separation:} Using an exact, moment-matched Le Cam two-point construction, we establish a global non-asymptotic minimax sample complexity lower bound of $\Omega((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ for distinguishing two competing memory rate cohorts differing by $\Delta\eta$ from price and flow observations. We prove that passive waiting cannot substitute for active excitation: without deliberate probing, competing models sharing identical aggregate sensitivity remain observationally indistinguishable over finite observation horizons.
    \item \textbf{Sharp Capital and Execution Scaling:} We prove that active identification requires executed quantity scaling as $\Theta(\delta^{-2})$ and financial capital scaling as $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$ in the memory rate resolution $\delta$. Using 80-digit arbitrary-precision interval arithmetic, we verify the theoretical scaling exponents (slopes $-2.002$ and $-4.005$) and certify a $61.44$~USDT infinite-horizon lower bound at $\delta=0.05$.
    \item \textbf{Robustness to Non-Linear Learning Architectures:} We validate our identification bounds across four distinct families of closed-loop adaptive agents trained in simulation: Recursive Least Squares (RLS), multi-layer perceptrons (MLP), profit-gradient learners, and tabular Bellman Q-learners with TD(0) value backups. When trained cohorts are serialized and reloaded, their small-signal impulse response fits a single exponential with $R^2 \ge 0.993$, and merged-measure equivalence holds to within relative gap $< 10^{-5}$.
    \item \textbf{Decomposition of Regulatory Disclosure Value:} Evaluating an empirical panel of 768 units and a 66-date multi-day panel, we demonstrate through a nested econometric hierarchy that $>99\%$ of the $+50.7\text{ pt}$ out-of-sample $R^2$ predictive gain conferred by regulatory inventory disclosure is explained by aggregate level persistence ($y_{t+6} \approx \rho^6 y_t$) rather than memory filtering, demarcating the exact econometric value of regulatory reporting.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why This Manuscript Fits \textit{Quantitative Economics}.}
This paper connects non-parametric econometrics, system identification, and market microstructure. It delivers non-asymptotic minimax lower bounds, sharp scaling laws verified to 80 digits of precision, and constructive econometric estimators that bridge theoretical identification with empirical market-making reality.
