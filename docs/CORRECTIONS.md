# WP01 corrections and claim controls

This record captures corrections that must remain visible when the next
protocol or manuscript is written.

## Claims that are not supported

- The original summary diagnostic is not superior to equally informed
  full-horizon conventional identification.
- NNLS or positive point fitting is not a confidence certificate.
- The stability condition is not a universal law for nonlinear, switching,
  signed-feedback, or financially constrained systems.
- Synthetic halt/default frequencies are not real-market crisis probabilities.
- No result is currently specific to trained AI, neural architecture, or
  autonomous-machine participation.
- Internal verification is not outside replication, and a source digest is
  not an authenticated runtime attestation.

## Preserved unfavorable evidence

- Four higher-noise confident errors remain in the full-horizon comparison.
- An omitted slow state produced false-stable decisions.
- A false rate floor produced false-stable labels.
- Endpoint diversity was not uniformly better at the largest tested budget.
- Binding synthetic constraints produced operational halts even where the
  isolated signal model was locally stable.
- Empty or conflicting confidence sets remain unresolved/model-conflict
  outcomes; they are not converted into stable labels.
- The trained-neural matched study does not clear its predeclared advantage
  threshold; the paper reports a null AI-specific result.
- The constrained noisy/dependent observation benchmark has nonzero
  wrong-confident calls and a large unresolved fraction.
- The throttle intervention result is single-account synthetic evidence and
  cannot establish absence of loss shifting.

## Corrections policy

The historical study files and old checksums are not edited to make new
claims fit. If a future reproduction changes a numerical result, the new
result receives a new versioned artifact, the discrepancy is explained here
or in a dated correction note, and the old result remains in provenance.

## WP01 acceptance

The claim ledger assigns IDs, scope assumptions, evidence paths, code paths,
counting units, and claim limits to the current central claims. It does not
establish novelty, external review, empirical validity, or publication
readiness.
