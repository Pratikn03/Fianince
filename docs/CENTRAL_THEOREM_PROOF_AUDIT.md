## 1. Executive Summary of Proof Audit

1. **What is unconditionally proved (`PROVED`)**:
   - Within the linear positive-cohort dynamic recursion $x_t = a x_{t-1} + \kappa(u_t + \sum_{i=1}^K q_i y_{i,t-1})$ with $y_{i,t} = (1-\eta_i)y_{i,t-1} + \eta_i x_t$, the closed-loop impulse response $h_t$ is completely monotone (`lem:cm`), observational equivalence is characterized by the merged measure $M = \sum_i w_i \delta_{r_i}$ (`thm:merged`), and the summed closed-loop gain satisfies the exact bridge $S = \sum_{t\ge 0} h_t = \|h\|_1 = \frac{\kappa}{1 - a - \kappa\Phi}$ where $\Phi = \sum_i q_i = \int (1-r)^{-1}\,dM(r)$ (`prop:invariance`, `thm:sum`).
   - For the known one-versus-split cohort testing problem ($1$ cohort at $\bar\eta$ vs. $2$ symmetric cohorts at $\bar\eta\pm\delta$), the closed-loop transfer difference is $\Theta(\delta^2)$, with exact third-tap separation $h^\delta_2 - h^0_2 = -\kappa^2 q \delta^2$ (`thm:quantity`). Under a single unsplit pulse of size $V = \Theta(\delta^{-2})$, both committed margin capital and quadratic impact cost scale as $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$ (`thm:cap`). Under arbitrary multi-period or adaptive round-trip probing with capital reuse across flat cycles, peak committed margin at any single instant can be bounded by $O(v_{\max}^2)$, while **irrevocable cumulative round-trip execution dissipation** remains $\Omega(\delta^{-4})$ (`thm:capital`, `thm:spectral`, `thm:adaptive`).
   - For the $K$-cohort minimax lower bound (`thm:kcohort`), the $K$-point Gauss and Gauss–Radau quadrature measures on $[r_c-\Delta\eta, r_c+\Delta\eta]$ match moments $0,\ldots,2K-2$ identically (verified to $2\times 10^{-80}$ in 80-digit arithmetic), differ at moment $2K-1$ by $\Theta((\Delta\eta)^{2K-1})$, and yield a Le Cam minimax lower bound $N = \Omega(\sigma^2 (\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)})$ for both rate recovery at scale $\Delta\eta$ and Wasserstein-1 estimation at scale $W\Delta\eta$.
   - For general completely monotone memory on $[0,\rho]$ with $\rho < 1$ fixed (`lem:altpair`, `thm:general`), Chebyshev equioscillation / divided-difference pairs establish the logarithmic minimax rate $\varepsilon_N = \Theta(1/\log(N/\sigma^2))$ in $W_1$, with constants depending explicitly on $(1-\rho)^{-1}$.
   - In Paper 2, certifying the scalar aggregate sensitivity $S$ (or $\Phi$) to accuracy $\epsilon$ under known $(a,\kappa)$, zero initial state $z_0=0$, positive cohorts, and memory bound $\bar r < 1$ requires $N_{\mathrm{cert}}(\epsilon) = \Theta(\sigma^2 \epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon))$ independent of $\Delta\eta$ and $K$ (`thm:certcost`), establishing a rigorous mathematical separation between certifying $S$ and identifying individual cohort parameters $(\eta_i, q_i)$.

2. **What requires explicit conditioning or theorem revision (`CONDITIONALLY_PROVED` / `CLAIM_REQUIRES_REVISION`)**:
   - **Theorem `prop:esprit` (Prony–Hankel Upper Bound for $K$ Cohorts — `CLAIM_REQUIRES_REVISION`)**:
     - Our 80-digit confluent Vandermonde Jacobian audit revealed that at cohort spacing $\Delta\eta$, the inverse Jacobian blocks scale **asymmetrically** across rates and weights:
       $$ \left\|\frac{\partial r}{\partial M}\right\|_2 = \Theta\bigl(w_{\min}^{-1}(\Delta\eta)^{-(2K-2)}\bigr), \qquad \left\|\frac{\partial w}{\partial M}\right\|_2 = \Theta\bigl((\Delta\eta)^{-(2K-1)}\bigr). $$
     - Consequently, the unscaled parameter error $\max_i(|\hat r_{\pi(i)}-r_i|, |\hat w_{\pi(i)}-w_i|) \le \varepsilon$ stated in `prop:esprit`(i) does **not** hold with exponent $(\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-4)}$ unless the weight error is scaled by $\Delta\eta/W$, i.e.,
       $$ d_{\mathrm{param},\Delta\eta}(\hat\theta,\theta) := \max_{1\le i\le K}\max\Bigl(|\hat r_{\pi(i)}-r_i|,\; \frac{\Delta\eta}{W}|\hat w_{\pi(i)}-w_i|\Bigr) \le \varepsilon. $$
     - Under this scale-matched metric (which matches the Gauss vs. Gauss–Radau lower-bound construction where $|r^A_i-r^B_i|\asymp \Delta\eta$ and $|w^A_i-w^B_i|\asymp W$), setting $\varepsilon = c\Delta\eta$ resolves both rates to $c\Delta\eta$ and weights to $cW$ in $N = O(\sigma^2\log(K/\beta)(\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)})$ samples (`prop:esprit`(ii)).
     - Furthermore, in `prop:esprit`(iii), the Wasserstein-1 bound $W_1(\hat M, M) \le \varepsilon_W$ in $N = O(\sigma^2 \varepsilon_W^{-2}(\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-4)})$ **does** hold because $\sum_i (\hat w_i - w_i) = \hat k_0 - k_0 = O_P(\sigma N^{-1/2})$ cancels the constant level of any 1-Lipschitz test function $f$, and Lipschitz continuity $|f(r_i)-f(\bar r)| \le K\Delta\eta$ multiplies the individual weight errors $|\hat w_i - w_i|$ by $\Delta\eta$, restoring the $(\Delta\eta)^{-(2K-2)}$ sensitivity! We revise `prop:esprit` in Paper 1 and add its complete proof to Appendix A (`p1_appendix.tex`).
   - **Four Statistical Problems Must Be Explicitly Distinguished**:
     1. *Discriminating two known economies* ($N$ or $B = \Theta(\delta^{-4})$ for a symmetric split).
     2. *Estimating unknown cohort parameters at fixed known $K$* ($N = \Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)})$ on $\Theta_K(\Delta\eta, w_{\min})$).
     3. *Recovering an unknown number of cohorts $K$* (unbounded in polynomial sample size; governed by the $\Theta(1/\log N)$ nonparametric barrier of Theorem `thm:general`).
     4. *Certifying the aggregate risk functional $S$ or $\Phi$* ($N = \Theta(\epsilon^{-2}\log(1/\epsilon))$ when $\bar r < 1$ is known, independent of $\Delta\eta$ and $K$).
   - **Boundary and Regime Qualifications in Paper 2**:
     - The equality $\|h\|_1 = S = \frac{\kappa}{1-a-\kappa\Phi}$ requires nonnegative impulse coefficients $h_t \ge 0$ (which holds for positive momentum cohorts $q_i \ge 0$, or under mild inventory damping $0 \le \psi_i \le 1-\delta_{\mathrm{inv}}$ with $\delta_{\mathrm{inv}} \ge \eta_i$ by `prop:inventory_S`). When contrarian cohorts ($q_i < 0$) or strong inventory payback cause $h_t$ to change sign, $\|h\|_1 > |S|$ (`rem:counter`; verified in `independent_math_audit.py` where $\|h\|_1 - S = 1.4110 > 0$).
     - The basic one-parameter guard $\alpha^* = c_I / (\kappa\Phi^+)$ assumes zero initial market displacement and zero hidden cohort state ($z_0 = 0$). When $z_0 \neq 0$, unforced drift consumes buffer $(a|x_0| + \alpha\kappa\Phi^+\bar y_0)/(1 - a - \alpha\kappa\Phi^+)$ (`prop:driftguard`), strictly lowering the safe admission cap to $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}^* < \alpha^*$.
