                                                Abstract
         Adaptive financial agents alter the observations on which they subsequently act. We study
     what an observer can infer about this feedback in restricted market–learning models. In a
     common-rate benchmark, different allocations of feedback across agents generate identical
     aggregate price responses, while channel-specific measurements distinguish allocations under
     explicit rank and scale conditions. Randomized price probes yield confidence bounds under
     independent Gaussian episodes; architectural disclosures permit a terminal-row regression
     contrast. In the retained primary synthetic study, this contrast covers the true parameter
     in 95.76% of 2,880 panels and issues a decision in 25.87%, with 2.68% error conditional on
     deciding. Numerical conic bounds remain unverified candidates. Separate funded simulations
     show why signal stability does not ensure operational safety. A post-review correction retrains
     ten tabular policies in an inventory-only environment: the active-minus-frozen displacement
     interval includes zero, the policies do not outperform no-trade on this outcome, and insufficient
     action support prevents structural certification. Reanalysis of frozen exchange data exposes a
     sample-lineage discrepancy and unsuccessful dynamic fits. Public-data proxies set illustrative
     scales but do not validate execution. The contribution is a set of model-specific identification
     boundaries and reproducible negative controls, not a universal detector of machine-market
     risk.

Keywords: adaptive agents; endogenous risk; financial stability; market microstructure; perfor-
mative prediction; system identification.

1     Introduction, Related Work, and Information Architecture
1.1   Introduction
In modern electronic financial markets, automated algorithms react to price movements in
sub-millisecond timeframes, update internal predictive representations, and submit subsequent
orders that move the very prices on which they learn. When many decision systems share
common signals, risk models, infrastructure, or optimization objectives, evaluating individual
algorithms in isolation misses the emergent feedback of their interaction. During episodes of
market stress—from the May 2010 Flash Crash to the March 2020 Treasury market dislocation
and the 2022 commodities freezes—the coupling between automated execution, adverse selection,
and dealer balance-sheet constraints can trigger sudden liquidity withdrawal and cascading
liquidation spirals.
   This paper investigates whether an exchange surveillance authority or macroprudential
regulator can detect and certify whether adaptive market participants are jointly amplifying
disturbances using only observable transaction data. Specifically: what mathematical object
describes the joint response of market prices and adaptive participants? What observable
information is required to estimate that object? And can public surveillance identify which
specific group of market participants is driving systemic amplification?
   We establish four core contributions:
1. A model-specific group-identification boundary. In the common-rate observationally
   symmetric benchmark, two economies with swapped feedback allocations have the same
   aggregate price response at every horizon (Theorem 5.1). Additional measurements b = ηU q
   separate labeled channels under explicit rank and scale assumptions (Theorem 5.2). With
   known positive η, full column rank of U gives recovery; with known positive Q = 1⊤ q, full
   rank of the augmented matrix [1⊤ ; U ] gives a uniform recovery guarantee and can require
   only n − 1 additional independent channels. Rank deficiency does not rule out pointwise
   uniqueness at a nonnegative boundary. With unknown η, full-rank U and known Q > 0
   recover the common scale; without that scale information, nonzero observations generally
   leave a ray segment, except when the admissible rate interval is a singleton.
2. Observable active probing and set-membership intervals. We develop a randomized
   probe-order protocol that estimates aggregate closed-loop feedback S and input-output im-
   pulse responses from public quote revisions without requiring private policy access. Under
   the declared intervention and noise contract, randomized active price probing yields cali-
   brated uncertainty bounds but faces intrinsic abstention near stability boundaries. When
   complemented by verified architectural disclosures under Contract I1 (exact prices, disclosed
   learning rates, verified zero reset, and complete channel inventory), an audited terminal-row
   OLS pivot achieves 95.76% parameter coverage across 2,880 primary panels, with 25.87%
   decisiveness, 0.69% unconditional error, and 2.68% conditional error among issued decisions.
3. The decoupling of signal stability and operational solvency. In a funded transaction
   environment with inventory, leverage, and margin limits, we demonstrate that a locally stable
   signal model with S = 0.82 < 1 (spectral radius ρ(J) ≈ 0.99009) produces a 45.42% dealer
   halt rate under balance-sheet stress. This proves that linear stability certificates cannot be
   extrapolated through nonlinear solvency limits.
4. Corrective transfer and empirical diagnostics. Ten tabular policies are retrained after
   accounting and audit corrections. Their finite-grid screens abstain for insufficient recorded
   support rather than establishing a structural violation. They do not improve on no-trade’s
   price displacement, and the active/frozen interval includes zero. Two frozen Coinbase samples
   give different cross-sectional associations; failed chronological Binance fits and unvalidated
   public-data scale mappings remain visible.

1.2   Related work and the precise research gap
Adaptive expectations and endogenous financial risk

Arthur et al. [1] model endogenous expectations in an artificial stock market. Brock and
Hommes [2] analyze heterogeneous beliefs and routes to complex asset-price dynamics. These
are direct precedents for any claim that agents and market conditions co-evolve. A convincing
extension must identify something not already explained by adaptive strategy selection: for
example, a measurable feedback operator that predicts behavior across materially different
decision architectures.
   Funding and trading conditions provide a second foundation. Brunnermeier and Pedersen
[3] connect market liquidity and funding liquidity, including mutually reinforcing liquidity
deterioration under specified conditions. Consequently, losses generated by common risk controls
or liquidation pressure cannot automatically be attributed to AI. A meaningful test must retain
ordinary liquidity and balance-sheet feedback in the non-AI benchmark.

Algorithmic interaction and financial-market experiments

Calvano et al. [4] show that Q-learning pricing algorithms can sustain supracompetitive out-
comes in a repeated oligopoly experiment without direct communication. This establishes that
individually trained agents can generate consequential collective behavior, although product-
market pricing is not itself a model of systemic financial loss. Colliard et al. [9] bring learning
algorithms into securities-market pricing and identify consequences of limited experimentation
and noisy feedback. Their mechanisms are necessary comparators for a proposed market-making
application.
   Anand et al. [8] connect learning and reasoning architectures to coordination and financial
fragility in fund-redemption experiments. Our model does not reproduce that environment or
establish transfer across their architectures. The narrower comparison is between aggregate

exchange exercises in Section 7 are separate observational diagnostics, not validation of this

Performative prediction and learning in games

Perdomo et al. [5] formalize situations in which a deployed predictor changes the distribution on
which it is subsequently evaluated. Narang et al. [6] extend the analysis to multiple decision
makers and distinguish performatively stable equilibria from Nash equilibria. These works
already provide a language for prediction–action–distribution feedback.
   A recent preprint by Farina and Perdomo [7] studies online algorithms and mixed performative
stability. It is important not to equate that learning-theoretic solution concept with every form
of financial stability. Convergence of average decisions or equilibrium distributions does not,
without an additional argument, bound interim drawdowns, market depth, collateral shortfalls,
or the solvency of a leveraged institution. Our intended bridge concerns such financial outcomes,
not a denial of existing convergence results.

Dynamical systems, robustness, and market simulation

Linearization, Lyapunov analysis, and common-metric robustness are established mathematical
tools [13, 14]. Rebranding a spectral radius is not a new theorem. Their use here is to make
assumptions, state variables, and failure modes explicit before attempting empirical measurement.
The challenge is particularly acute for time-varying agents, discrete decisions, and partially
observed policy states.
   For experimental infrastructure, the ABIDES paper describes a discrete-event market simulator
with exchange interaction and configurable communication latency [15]. It is an appropriate
candidate for a later implementation, not evidence that our proposed agent population or market
mechanism has already been validated. A second implementation or an independent simulator
would help distinguish a general result from a software-specific artifact.

Identification from imperfect input–output observations

Set-membership identification already asks which models are compatible with measured data
and declared uncertainty [19]. Data informativity distinguishes recovering a unique model from
learning enough to establish a property such as stability or stabilizability [21]. Positive-realization
and steady-state-gain information have also been incorporated into regularized identification
[23, 24]. Consequently, positivity constraints, confidence sets and gain side-information are
essential predecessors, not newly invented concepts in this paper.
   Our finite-family confidence construction is deliberately narrow: Gaussian independent
episode vectors with arbitrary within-episode covariance. Sign-perturbed sums demonstrates
that exact finite-sample identification methods also exist under other assumptions [25]; we
have not implemented or subsumed them. Likewise, linear-system sample-complexity lower
bounds are an established literature [22]. The new price-response two-point example supplies a
model-specific observation limit, not a new general testing principle. The difference between
noisy hidden kernels and noisy measured prices is central to the present integration.

1.3     Research question, scope, and claim discipline
The operational question is:
       Can changes in the joint market–learning feedback system identify economically mean-
       ingful changes in disturbance amplification, beyond what is explained by conventional
       liquidity, leverage, and volatility measures?
There are three different levels of claim. A mathematical result follows from an explicitly stated
model. A mechanism result requires controlled experiments demonstrating that a specified
feedback channel causes an outcome. An external-validity result requires evidence that the
mechanism and its measurement apply beyond the experimental environment. This paper supplies
analytical theorems, a controlled group measurement-and-mechanism study, and exploratory
public-market analysis across live cryptocurrency markets. We explicitly distinguish endogenous
algorithmic adaptation from static market liquidity, while avoiding unverified causal claims
regarding AI specificity versus equally adaptive non-AI algorithmic institutions.
   The primary domain for the first empirical paper should be a single market with an explicit
price-formation mechanism, inventories, and funding constraints. An equity-like limit-order-
book environment is a reasonable experimental starting point. A fund-redemption game is a
possible alternative, but these should not be combined into one vaguely specified “global market”
simulation. Banking, insurance, payments, and household finance have different state variables
and institutional constraints; they are later transfer tests.
   We structure the analysis around six core microstructural dimensions: machine autonomy
exposure, behavioral concentration, adaptation rate, leverage, liquidity, and intervention latency.
These are organizing dimensions rather than sufficient statistics by assumption. A critical surface
in those coordinates may be model dependent, nonmonotone, or not estimable with available
observations. Failure to find a sharp boundary is an admissible research outcome.

2.2    Local stability, finite-horizon risk, and switching
A standard local criterion

Proposition 2.1 (Fixed-system local stability; standard result). Suppose the deterministic
joint map in equation (4) is continuously differentiable near a fixed point z ∗ . If ρ(J ∗ ) < 1, that
fixed point is locally exponentially asymptotically stable. If ρ(J ∗ ) > 1, it is unstable. The case
ρ(J ∗ ) = 1 is not resolved by this first-order criterion.

   A proof of the stable case and the scope of the unstable case are given in section A. This
proposition applies an established linearization principle; it is not a claim of a new financial law
[14].
   To summarize the instantaneous spectral amplification of the joint system, define the descrip-
tive diagnostic
                                       MMClocal = ρ(J ∗ ).                                      (10)
It is a local, model-conditioned diagnostic. Values above or below one do not by themselves
specify crisis probability, loss magnitude, or welfare. At equality, nonlinear terms can determine
the outcome: zt+1 = zt − zt3 is attracting sufficiently near zero, whereas zt+1 = zt + zt3 is repelling,
although both derivatives at zero equal one.

An identification impossibility without provenance

Proposition 3.1 (Observational equivalence of decision-maker labels). Consider two economies
with the same initial market-state distribution, the same market transition kernel conditional
on aggregate actions, and the same conditional law of aggregate actions given every observable
history. The economies can have different assignments of human and machine decision-maker
labels. They nevertheless induce the same law for all observable market histories. No estimator
using only those histories can consistently distinguish the two label assignments or identify
different autonomy shares attached solely to those assignments.

Proof. The initial observable distributions coincide. If observable histories coincide in distribution
through t, the assumed conditional action law and common market transition kernel imply the
same distribution for the next observation. Induction establishes equality of all finite-dimensional
observable distributions. Any statistic of those observations therefore has the same law in both
economies; it cannot converge in probability to two distinct label-dependent constants.

   This is an application of ordinary observational equivalence, not a claim that financial
attribution is impossible with richer data. Account-level decision provenance, randomly assigned
architecture, authorized policy-update experiments, or credible external variation can break the
equivalence. Historical prices alone do not reveal whether a self-exciting pattern was produced
by humans, fixed algorithms, adaptive agents, or a mixture.

Estimating the response system

The general operator-estimation problem below is broader than the completed scalar experiment.
Sections 4.1–4.3 provide one restricted solution using signed filled probes and prices; they neither
reconstruct every private policy state nor identify AI participation from prices alone.
   In a simulator with full state access, estimate J by perturbing each normalized coordinate
around a specified operating point and measuring the resulting joint next-state response. Use
multiple perturbation sizes to test local linearity and independent random streams to quantify
agent stochasticity. Finite differences of a mean response estimate a mean-map derivative, not a
pathwise derivative of a discrete sampled action.
   In observational data, fitting zt+1 = Jzt + et+1 requires sufficient excitation, a defensible
state representation, and restrictions controlling confounding and feedback-induced endogeneity.
Hidden policy states typically require a state-space or input–output model. A reduced-form
estimate may predict outcomes without identifying architecture-specific mechanisms; the paper
must report which claim its design supports.
   An uncertainty set J should reflect estimation uncertainty and relevant model misspecification.
There is no distribution-free license to assign a narrow confidence interval to ρ(J) by ignoring
dependence, near-defective matrices, hidden states, or selected operating points. A claim of
pre-crisis warning additionally requires lead time, false-alarm rates, and a prospectively fixed
event definition.

The four-metric evaluation standard and conditional error rates

In partial-identification settings, an essential econometric distinction exists between unconditional
error and conditional decision error. Across an evaluation suite of N panels, let Ndec be the
number of panels where the estimator issues a definitive classification (stable or unstable), and
let Nerr be the number of incorrect classifications. We track four distinct metrics:
   1. Decisiveness: Dec = Ndec /N ;
   2. Coverage: Cov = 1 − Nparam_uncovered /N ;
   3. Unconditional Error Rate: Erruncond = Nerr /N ;
   4. Conditional Error Rate: Errcond = Nerr /Ndec (defined for Ndec > 0).
When an estimator operates near the critical bifurcation boundary (S → 1− ), the true feedback
parameter lies infinitesimally close to the decision threshold. In this boundary regime, an
aggressive heuristic that attempts to force decisiveness may exhibit an apparently acceptable
unconditional error rate (e.g., 7.5% of all episodes) solely because it abstains on most trials
(90.5% abstention, 9.5% decisiveness). However, its conditional error rate among decisive calls
can be catastrophic:

6.3   Learned-Agent Transfer: Corrective Inventory-Only Experiment
The linear theory does not automatically describe a learned trading policy. We therefore examine a
separate tabular Q-learning environment, with an explicit distinction between response screening
and structural certification.

Economic and accounting contract. The corrected environment has four ledgers: the
trading agent, a fundamentalist, a noise trader, and the dealer. Simultaneous customer orders
are netted; if their aggregate exceeds the dealer’s inventory range, all orders receive the same
proportional fill. Cash and shares transfer between counterparties, including the half-spread paid
to the dealer. A capacity halt prevents subsequent execution, but price marking continues to
the common 128-step horizon. This model permits negative cash and has no funding, margin, or
liquidation test. It is therefore not the funded engine studied above. On the corrected evaluation
paths, the largest all-party cash residual is 3.64 × 10−11 .
   The state has 6,125 bins for price deviation, return, agent inventory, dealer utilization and order
imbalance; orders are (−2, −1, 0, 1, 2). Reward is marked equity change minus 0.01It2 +0.005|∆It |.
These penalties are preferences in the objective, not cash transfers or an estimate of social loss.

Post-review corrective protocol. Ten independent policies receive 100,000 transitions each,
using training seeds 926101–926110. Validation seeds 926201–926205 select checkpoints every
20,000 transitions. Evaluation uses ten shared tapes, seeds 926301–926310, with a −2 price
shock at step 16. The learning rate is 0.05, discount factor 0.98, and exploration probability
decays multiplicatively by 0.99995 per update from 1 to a floor of 0.02. These are the executed
parameters, not the different settings previously described in prose. Validation uses a copy
and cannot advance the training random stream. Every active/frozen comparison restarts from
identical checkpoint and tie-breaking states. Frozen policies remain bitwise unchanged.
   This is a corrective, post-review analysis, not a newly preregistered confirmation. The
historical artifacts and added reward-sensitivity diagnostics remain archived; their economic
and model-class interpretations are superseded where they depend on the earlier execution or
audit errors. A smoke run exercised the same namespaces before the full corrective run; no
untouched-test or prospectively confirmatory designation is made.

Support-first screening. The audit holds return, inventory, utilization and imbalance fixed
while varying price. It uses the expected action across greedy ties rather than a random draw at
each query. A screen requires at least ten recorded updates to every action at each queried state.
This threshold is a descriptive support rule, not a calibrated confidence guarantee. Missing
support produces abstention. A constant zero response has undefined R2 and is compatible
with zero local gain; it is not evidence against the positive-memory class. Even a supported,
linear-looking response on this finite grid cannot establish a complete hidden-state architecture
or its spectral radius. All ten corrected policies abstain for insufficient action support.

8.1   Intervention under uncertainty and liquidity feedback
A proposed intervention can reduce order size, slow an update, retain a previously approved
policy, route a decision to review, or abstain from a specific action. These are not interchangeable.
In particular, freezing learning need not cancel outstanding orders, and abstaining from new
positions need not remove existing inventory risk.

Why abstention is not automatically stabilizing

Consider the scalar positive-feedback model with a = 0.4, η = 0.2, κ = 1, and baseline g0 = 0.45.
Suppose an intervention retains a fraction u of demand sensitivity but depth falls to a fraction
q(u) of baseline. The effective scale becomes
                                                       u
                                          g(u) = g0        .                                     (60)
                                                      q(u)

At u = 0.5 and unchanged depth, g = 0.225 and the model is more strongly stable. At u = 0.5
and q(u) = 0.25, g = 0.9 and the model is unstable. These are constructed scenarios, not
estimated liquidity elasticities. They show why evaluating abstention only through reduced
individual trading ignores an essential equilibrium response.
   Any empirical intervention comparison must therefore record bid–ask spreads, depth, execution
failures, inventory exposure, and losses borne by other participants. An intervention that reduces
one agent’s losses by transferring risk or withdrawing market-making capacity is not automatically
a system improvement. Operational deployment is outside this manuscript’s scope.

Surveillance considerations and legal boundaries of active probing

An essential institutional question is whether randomized active probing could be deployed in live
markets by exchanges or regulatory authorities. Such protocols could raise market-manipulation
and deceptive-practice concerns depending on their design, intent, execution, and jurisdiction.
For example, under U.S. securities regulations (e.g., SEC Rule 10b-5) and European frameworks
(e.g., Article 12 of the EU Market Abuse Regulation), orders without genuine investment intent
may face regulatory scrutiny; however, the precise legal characterization depends heavily on
implementation details, the identity of the probing party, its authorization, and applicable
jurisdiction. We formalize relevant statutory boundaries and macroprudential alternatives
(e.g., Limit-Up/Limit-Down mechanisms) in Section S16 of the accompanying Mathematical
Supplement. Active probing is therefore framed strictly as a mathematical identification
benchmark establishing the minimum information required for feedback classification, not as an
operational surveillance recommendation.

8.3     Limitations, responsible use, and wider significance
Scientific limitations

The original tractable network model uses a common first-order learning rate; the integrated
scalar studies extend it to heterogeneous first-order rates. Both retain a fixed reference point
and nonnegative response coefficients. It omits endogenous innovation in strategies, strategic
deception, discontinuous liquidation rules, endogenous entry and exit, and general-equilibrium
wealth effects. Its spectral boundary is neither an asset-pricing theorem nor a universal condition
for crises. Some instability modes may be damped by limits, while finite disturbances can cause


                                                48
From Human Markets to Machine Markets                                         Research Manuscript


defaults even around a locally stable equilibrium.
    The complete joint state may be inaccessible for modern agents. Hidden memory, discrete
version changes, stochastic sampling, and proprietary risk overlays can undermine smooth
finite-dimensional approximations. Estimated feedback may be highly uncertain exactly where
extreme events matter. Identifying a persistent empirical feedback coefficient does not identify
an economically optimal intervention.
    While analytical and linear identification checks establish the mathematical limits of ob-
servability, real markets impose nonlinear solvency and inventory constraints. The funded
market model introduced above explicitly incorporates binding financial limits, recognizing that
linear interval estimation cannot be blindly extrapolated across nonlinear solvency boundaries.
Independent identically distributed Gaussian episodes, fixed parameters, exogenous filled pulses,
and a valid positive learning-rate floor are substantive assumptions. A repeatable episode is
natural in simulation but not automatically obtainable from a single evolving market. Selecting
windows after observing stress, using endogenous fills as if randomly assigned, or carrying an
invalid floor into the solver breaks the identification argument.
    The response rectangle discards covariance geometry and the recurrence inequalities over-
approximate compatible models. Some inconclusive outcomes reflect this conservatism, not a
proven information limit. The corrected comparator study is post-hoc, with only five panels in
each fixed cell. Its pooled frequencies do not establish universal superiority. Local single-rate
fits may fail under multi-rate data, and unavailable inference must not be counted as successful
coverage. The conic implementation withholds structural certification because its complete
floating-point constraint assembly is not outward-enclosed. A true fixed-step instability may be
limited by nonlinearities and capital restrictions in a different model, while stable prices do not
prevent funded agents from defaulting. The original stable funded-engine counterexample and
the later, effectively unstable capacity scenario are distinct experiments.
    The results provide no evidence of a global machine-participation threshold, real-market crisis
prediction, architecture-independent transfer, or the superiority of an abstention intervention.
They also do not establish a new scientific paradigm. Any submission must communicate this
evidence boundary in the abstract as well as the methods.

Research ethics and operational boundaries

All agent-interaction and information-corruption experiments should remain in controlled envi-
ronments unless separately authorized. No experiment proposed here requires placing deceptive
orders, manipulating public markets, or using customer data without permission. Human-subject
work and sensitive institution-level records require relevant review, consent or lawful basis, data
minimization, and secure handling.
   Publication should document model versions, datasets, licenses, prompts that may legally
be shared, code changes, and observed failure modes. Public generated explanations may be
studied as outputs, but should not be treated as verified internal reasoning or a causal account
of an agent’s decision. No claim depends on access to private model reasoning.

What would make the contribution globally useful

The potential value is a measurement and validation framework that helps institutions understand
interactions among decision systems, rather than another predictor of asset prices. To support
wider use, the framework would need reliable estimation from realistic observations, added value
over existing risk measures, transfer across relevant architectures and institutions, and evidence
about intervention costs and spillovers. The FSB’s monitoring work and its 2026 consultation
on responsible adoption provide institutional context for such questions, not endorsement of this
manuscript [11, 12].

   A globally relevant result need not claim that all financial laws have changed. A precisely
demonstrated failure of an existing diagnostic, together with a better measurable replacement
and clearly delimited applicability, could materially advance the field. Conversely, evidence that

be an important correction to exaggerated claims.

8.4   Conclusion
The defensible question is not whether finance loses its familiar principles when AI becomes
prevalent. It is which feedback properties can be established from available observations
when adaptive decision rules interact with price formation. The original framework requires
the complete market–learning state for local analysis, distinguishes asymptotic stability from
transient and switching risk, and rejects provider diversity or individual abstention as automatic
safety certificates.
   The finite-window and full-moment audits strengthen the measurement question while clarify-
ing diagnostic limits. Exact finite windows can conceal opposite stability classes; fixed-order noisy
examples can remain hard to distinguish. Yet conventional full-moment identification extracts
more useful information than simple summary bounds. Thus neither a vivid counterexample nor
a favorable weak-baseline result justifies claiming a universally superior estimator.
   Randomized active probes recover closed-loop response uncertainty under the declared model
and sampling contract. Aggregate responses alone cannot identify group allocations in the
common-rate symmetric benchmark. The rank results explain what additional channel informa-
tion resolves that ambiguity. These are restricted identification statements, not an unrestricted
theory of economic crises. Public-data reanalysis illustrates a separate difficulty: two frozen
samples yield different associations, and conditional dynamic fits remain poor. Neither dataset
supplies an authenticated internal architecture or validates real-market execution.
   The corrected learned-agent experiment also supplies a boundary, not successful transfer. Its
response audit lacks sufficient action support; the active/frozen displacement interval includes
zero, and frozen policies have slightly greater displacement than no-trade on the fixed test tapes.
This does not establish a universal null, a welfare ranking or the mechanism behind participation.
Unsupported responses must not be relabeled as proved violations of the structural class.
   The resulting direction is a separation of guarantees, not a completed universal detector.
Architectural audit, observed statistical uncertainty and operational financial risk must each
be established at their own level. The next external requirements are truthful complete-state
provenance, a calibrated observation and funding environment, an appropriately strong equal-
information comparator for each target, outside proof and novelty review, and an independent
team’s reproduction. A global safety framework requires institutional evidence and validated
economic outcomes, not simply a larger manuscript.

A.3     What the boundary does not imply
A positive real eigenvalue passing through one identifies loss of local linear stability in the
specified model. It does not determine the nonlinear attractor, whether prices crash or grow,
whether an institution defaults, or whether a finite disturbance remains inside the modeled region.
A nonlinear extension could saturate or switch into another regime. An observed financial crisis
can also occur without this crossing, for example when an external shock overwhelms solvency
buffers.
   The result uses a single persistence scalar a and a common η. Allowing asset-specific
persistence, asymmetric learning rates, delayed observation, or signed response matrices removes
the simple formula unless further structure is proved. A claimed universal surface in six aggregate
variables would need to show that the omitted state is either irrelevant, bounded, or measured.
The current theorem does not establish such sufficiency.

A.4     Stochastic and nonlinear robustness
For a nonlinear map Φt with a common reference point, suppose the induced P -norm of every
derivative on a convex ball of radius r is at most γ < 1. Then the same Lipschitz argument
yields the disturbance bound in equation (59). If b̄ ≤ (1 − γ)r and the initial state is inside
the ball, that ball is invariant. With an evolving reference point, its motion must appear as an
additional disturbance; ignoring that drift changes the claim.
   If an estimated uncertainty set covers the actual matrices with probability at least 1 − δ over
the relevant horizon, the robust bound holds on that coverage event. Marginal per-time coverage
does not automatically imply simultaneous horizon coverage. Model updates, adaptive sampling,
and post-selection can invalidate a naive confidence statement. These statistical issues are work
remaining, not solved by the deterministic norm inequality.

       compatible observations, the exact set is {U † b/η : η ∈ [η, 1]}. It is a nondegenerate
       segment along a ray when b ̸= 0 and 0 < η < 1. Zero observations identify q = 0; a
       singleton rate interval also identifies q. An unknown nondegenerate rate interval does not
       identify the rate when q = 0.
    5. Case 5 (Possible nullspace ambiguity): With known rate and a feasible q0 , the
       nonnegative identified set is (q0 + ker U ) ∩ Rn+ . At a strictly positive point its local
       dimension is n − rank(U ), which can equal one. Boundary constraints can reduce the
       dimension to zero. With unknown rate, use the projection of the joint (q, 1/η) feasible set
       in the supplement, rather than calling every set a multidimensional subspace.
Finally, absent an auxiliary total-mass constraint, full column rank rank(U ) = n is necessary for
uniform point identification across the interior: if rank(U ) < n, there exists a non-zero direction w
such that U w = 0, meaning any interior point q admits an open neighborhood of observationally
equivalent interior parameters q + ϵw generating identical responses b. Boundary constraints
(such as nonnegativity or known sparsity) can resolve particular isolated configurations on the
boundary, but do not provide uniform interior identifiability. With known Q, any ambiguity
direction must also satisfy 1⊤ w = 0; the augmented rank condition applies when η is known.
Known Q and n − 1 probes do not generally guarantee joint identification of (η, q): for U = (1, 0)
and Q = 0.4, (η, q) = (0.2, (0.3, 0.1)) and (0.3, (0.2, 0.2)) both give b = 0.06. This does not rule
out identifying the rate alone: if c⊤ U = 1⊤ , then η = c⊤ b/Q even when U is rank deficient. For
example, U = (1, 1) identifies η from known Q > 0 but not the allocation. This completes the
proof of Theorem 5.2.

B.1     Information architecture and observational boundaries
To prevent circular identification, the framework strictly segregates information across three
observational tiers and formal contracts:
    1. Public market observables (Ipub , Contracts I0 and I2 ): Limit order book snapshots,
       trade prices, execution volumes, and (for Contract I0 ) randomized active probe alloca-
       tions. Estimators operating under Contract I0 (recurrence LP, interval deconvolution,
       covariance-aware output-error) receive active randomized probe interventions and prices,
       while estimators under Contract I2 (cross-sectional regressions, passive state-space fits)
       receive strictly passive observational market data. Both contracts operate strictly on Ipub ,
       with no architectural disclosures or private state data.
    2. Architectural disclosures (Iarch , Contract I1 ): Verified rate intervals [η0 , 1], complete
       feedback channel inventories, and protocol-enforced zero initial memory resets. Esti-
       mators operating under Contract I1 (audited terminal-row OLS) receive Ipub ∪ Iarch .
