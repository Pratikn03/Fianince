I. INTRODUCTION
As physical artificial intelligence (Physical AI) systems—
such as autonomous vehicles, smart grid power inverters,
robotic manipulators, and grid-scale battery storage—transition
from isolated simulators to open-world deployment, safety
certification becomes the primary bottleneck. A prevalent
assumption in modern predictive safety filters and control
barrier functions (CBFs) is either full state observation or
stationary Gaussian estimation error with bounded covariance
[1], [2].
In reality, real-world physical telecommunication and percep-
tion pipelines frequently violate these assumptions. Telemetry
dropouts, sensor degradation, clock jitter, and adversarial distur-
bances degrade telemetry non-stationarily [3], [4]. When safety
filters evaluate barrier conditions or constraint satisfaction
solely on degraded observations, they suffer from what we
designate theObservation–Action Safety Gap (OASG): the
controller perceives a positive safety margin, yet the underlying
unobserved true physical state penetrates the safety boundary.
To resolve this challenge, we organize the ORIUS framework
around one foundational thesis:
An autonomous physical system should release an
action only when its available information, uncer-
tainty model, and verified control authority support
the required safety guarantee. ORIUS characterizes
when such release is possible, when it is impossible,
and how to enforce it efficiently across heterogeneous
physical systems.
Rather than framing safety filtering as an empirical optimiza-
tion heuristic, ORIUS addresses four fundamental questions:
1) Knowability:When can a physical system distinguish
safe states from hazard states under degraded observa-
tion?
2) Possibility:Under what observation degradation and
actuator authority does any acceptably safe action exist?
3) Achievability:Can an online algorithm compute and
release such an action with a provable trajectory-level
safety guarantee?
4) Usefulness:Can certified action release be achieved
without unnecessarily forfeiting task performance or
exceeding real-time computational deadlines?
