Abstract
Aggregate price–flow data reveal model-dependent feedback diagnostics rather than
trader identities. In a reset linear cohort model, symmetric near-coincident rates re-
quire bounded-power probing budgets of at least orderδ−4; a separate scalar Gaussian
experiment yields a finite-window abstention layer. Reanalysis of recorded Coinbase
data shows substantial sensitivity to flow timing and price filtering, while covariance re-
strictions are rejected. Raw reconstruction of 768 Binance asset-days confirms smaller
feedback under observed-flow timing; fee contrasts and ordering-sensitive venue de-
compositions do not identify causal learning or transmission. Finally, a declared loss
operator and shock set yield an exact budget radius; fixed-spectrum and funded-dealer
simulations distinguish transient magnification from persistent-flow accumulation. In-
dependent replication and policy validation remain open.
Keywords:Adaptive learning, algorithmic trading, financial stability, market microstruc-
ture, system identification, non-normal dynamics, Kreiss constant.

1 Introduction
Electronic trading creates a feedback channel when price changes affect subsequent orders
and those orders affect later prices. The existence of this channel does not identify its
participant-level source: aggregate flow can reflect adaptive algorithms, order splitting, liq-
uidity provision, or common information. Nor does decay of price disturbances establish
that a dealer’s loss budget will remain intact. This paper separates these identification and
financial-risk questions.
Our central question is:what can observable aggregate data establish about feedback, and
what additional assumptions are required to turn dynamic stability into a financially mean-
ingful loss bound?We combine a linear adaptive-memory model with an audit of observable-
state definitions and timing, followed by controlled loss-exposure illustrations. The empirical
analysis is deliberately reduced-form. It does not identify artificial-intelligence trading, in-
dividual learning rules, or a causal market-maker response from anonymous records.
Conditional identification results.Within a reset linear cohort model with known
price-impact and anchor parameters, price responses identify merged rate–weight measures
rather than the number or identity of cohorts sharing a rate. For a uniformly stable compact
family of symmetric split alternatives, bounded-power deterministic probes with Gaussian
observation noise face a separation lower bound of orderδ−4. This is a lower bound for
the stated experiment, not an attained universal rate for all passive market data. A sepa-
rate scalar Gaussian price-surveillance experiment gives an explicit finite-window abstention
bound near its unit-root boundary. The observation experiment matters: the scalar result
is not asserted for noiselessly observed latent cohort states.
Measurement sensitivity.The primary empirical reanalysis uses recorded Coinbase
BTC, ETH, and ADA book snapshots and signed executed flow from April 7–19, 2021. A
300-second EMA price deviation is estimated on 10-second bins with only end-bin observed
flow. Mean reduced-form feedback is approximately0.00194,0.00098, and0.00062, respec-
tively. Forward-bin flow alignment produces larger estimates, while raw-return specifications
have feedback intervals spanning zero. These comparisons establish that measurement and
timing materially affect the feedback diagnostic. They do not establish an algorithmic
learning mechanism.

Bound
We now confront the fundamental divergence between price stability and financial interme-
diary solvency. Spectral radius describes asymptotic decay in the linear approximation. Loss
exposure additionally depends on the shock set, output operator and finite-time magnifica-
tion.
The Non-Normal Market Jacobian.Consider the closed-loop Jacobian matrixJfrom
(??). In financial markets, price impact and memory adaptation are asymmetric: order
flow directly affects prices through contemporaneous impactκ, while price innovations affect
memory through learning rateη. As a result,JJ⊤−J⊤J̸= 0: the closed-loop market Jaco-
bianJis genericallynon-normalunder asymmetric price-impact and learning parameters

eigenbasis. A diagonalizable non-normal matrix lacks such a basis, and a defective matrix
has insufficient eigenvectors; these facts alone do not determine output magnification. Con-
sequently, even when all eigenvalues are strictly inside the unit circle (|λi(J)|≤ρ(J)<1),
