The monetary reserve and cost functional are imposed model inputs, not estimated market
losses. The result concerns an open-loop controlled experiment with no natural process excita-
tion. A fixed lifetime quantity constraint, an average-power constraint and a repeatedly refreshed
risk allowance are different experiments. In particular, without accumulated costs a maintained
bounded-amplitude probe can continue; the theorem does not impose a capital ceiling on every safe
adaptive strategy. Applying it to public markets requires actual executions, exposures, model-error
coverage and a valid observation law.
The paper-specific advance proposed here is the explicit connection between memory resolution,
probing resources and monetary output, with matching orders for a known pair. Partial fractions,
Gaussian testing, convolution inequalities and positive-system gains are established tools. Closely
spaced source recovery is already studied in mixture and super-resolution theory (Manole and Ho,
2022; Liu and Ammari, 2023); non-normal financial bubbles and Kreiss diagnostics also precede this
paper (Sornette et al., 2023). These comparisons exclude first-use claims. Independent assessment
of the importance and novelty of this particular connection remains necessary.

Independent resets and known innovations are extra information conditions, not assumptions
verified in the empirical samples. This counterexample does not prove that anonymous public
records identify participants. It shows that passive process excitation can accumulate memory
information even when controlled quantity and the designated account’s capital remain fixed. Reset
costs, other exposures and unknown noise laws would change that experiment. The paper’s capital
and quantity conclusions consequently belong to their declared controlled contracts.
