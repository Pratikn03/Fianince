S11.2 Why a theoretical solution does not finish the empirical study
The response ellipsoid proves coverage only under(S14). It does not prove that one historical
market stream supplies iid reset episodes. The continuous-time theorem does not prove that
actual agents obey the model. The group theorem does not show which real accounts are adaptive.
A new interval construction does not prove an improvement over the original method until both
are evaluated with matched inputs, coverage targets, and numerical safeguards.
Similarly, computing features on Period A and fitting a regression using Period B outcomes does
not by itself measure forecasting performance of a rule frozen before those B outcomes were
seen. A later untouched date set remains necessary for that particular predictive claim. Missing
historical spread or depth observations must be removed from the corresponding primary analysis
or explicitly restricted to a retrospective diagnostic.
The correct remaining empirical work is to report interval coverage, widths, decision exposure,
conditional and unconditional errors, model conflicts, runtime, and actual forward predictive
loss under a frozen protocol. A null result, noncomparability finding, or frequent abstention is
admissible. No proof here guarantees a grade, acceptance, a targetp-value, or a fixed percentage
of project completion.

We separate the observations needed for aggregate feedback inference from those

S16 Legal and regulatory governance of active probing
S16.1 Market abuse regulations and manipulative intent
Under market manipulation frameworks such as SEC Rule 10b-5 in the United States and
Article 12 of the EU Market Abuse Regulation (MAR), submitting orders or executing transactions
that give false or misleading signals regarding the supply of, demand for, or price of financial
instruments is strictly prohibited. Executing orders without genuine investment intent to probe
counterparty algorithms can trigger regulatory scrutiny under manipulative and deceptive practice
standards.
S16.2 Operational boundaries for supervisory authorities
Consequently, active probing is established in this research strictly as a theoretical identification
benchmark, not an operational trading recommendation. If an exchange operator or macropru-
dential authority evaluates active system identification, any physical execution protocol requires
explicit legal, regulatory, and supervisory authorization. Standard market-wide mechanisms,
such as the national Limit-Up/Limit-Down (LULD) Plan, impose non-discriminatory price bands
and trading pauses precisely because live causal attribution across heterogeneous participants
remains an open institutional challenge.
