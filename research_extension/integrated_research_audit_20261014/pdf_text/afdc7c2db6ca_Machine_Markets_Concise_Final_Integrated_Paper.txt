From Human Markets to Machine Markets
Feedback Stability, Hidden Learning, Observable Stress Tests, and Next-Stage Validation
Concise final integrated manuscript, version 0.6

Abstract
Adaptive financial agents can change the market observations on which their subsequent
decisions depend. This concise final edition integrates the joint market–learning framework,
exact finite-window counterexamples, full-horizon identification audit, observable stress tests,
and a scoped next-stage validation. The established results remain model-conditioned and
synthetic. The new validation adds a trained small neural policy matched against fixed and
adaptive non-neural controls, a noisy/dependent observation benchmark, a single-account
intervention study, and a checksum-verified public Binance depth-band/trade-tape transfer
layer. Across three training seeds and 128 paired held-out episodes per seed, no neural
policy clears the predeclared 0.5-unit gain threshold against the adaptive controller. Under
constrained noisy/dependent observations, coverage is 0.790 with 0.075 wrong-confident and
0.905 unresolved calls. A 50% throttle reduces a local synthetic event rate from 0.090 to
0.000. The public crypto files are aggregate depth bands and trades, not queue-level L2, and
do not identify spreads or fills. No real-market safety guarantee, AI-specific causal effect,
independent replication, human approval, or publication acceptance is claimed.
Keywords:adaptive agents; endogenous risk; financial stability; market microstructure; perfor-
mative prediction; system identification.
JEL codes:C63, D83, G01, G14.
Evidence status.Model-conditioned theory, the declared synthetic protocols, the matched-policy
null study, and the public depth-band/trade-tape calibration layer are complete locally. Queue-level
exchange replay, independent outside replication, established priority, human authorship/ethics
review, and financial-safety deployment remain open. A correct response estimate or descriptive
market statistic does not validate unobserved model assumptions.
1 Introduction
The central question of this research program is whether large-scale delegation of financial
decisions to adaptive artificial agents changes the conditions under which financial markets
absorb or amplify disturbances. A trader may update a forecast in response to a price movement;
execution based on that forecast can then alter the next observation used for learning. When
many agents share information, response patterns, infrastructure, or update schedules, assessing
each decision system separately may miss important properties of their interaction.
This is not a claim that learning, reflexivity, or endogenous instability began with modern
artificial intelligence. The Santa Fe artificial stock market explicitly studied agents whose
expectations adapt to the market they collectively generate [?]. Heterogeneous-agent asset-
pricing models likewise generate complex dynamics through endogenous strategy selection [?].
The present problem must build on those contributions rather than restate their central insight
with new terminology.

Nor does a change in the identity of decision makers invalidate accounting identities or
logically derived no-arbitrage implications under their maintained assumptions. What may
change are the behavior of decision rules, the selection and stability of equilibria, the persistence
of disturbances, and the applicability of estimated relationships when the population of decision
systems changes. The phrase “from human markets to machine markets” names a research
question about mechanisms and measurement, not an asserted replacement of economic theory.
There is a concrete reason to investigate those mechanisms. The Financial Stability Board
identifies common-model correlations, third-party dependencies, cyber vulnerabilities, and model-
governance issues as possible channels of AI-related financial risk [?]. Its subsequent monitoring
report emphasizes information and measurement limitations [?]. These documents motivate a
research agenda; they do not establish the magnitude of AI-caused market losses or a threshold
beyond which markets become unstable.
Recent research also makes a broad claim that “architecture matters” insufficient as a novelty
claim.? ] compare Q-learning and large-language-model investors in a stylized fund-redemption
environment and obtain different coordination and fragility outcomes.?] study learning-based
market makers and show how experimentation and noisy profit feedback affect pricing. Our
research target is narrower and more demanding: determine when observable market–learning
feedback can support transferable stability diagnostics, and specify the evidence needed to
interpret those diagnostics causally.
The economic intuition is straightforward. Suppose a market has a stabilizing adjustment
mechanism when strategies are held fixed. A price disturbance nevertheless changes a learning
state, such as a return forecast or perceived risk level. The updated state generates an order re-
sponse whose price impact reinforces the original disturbance. Even when the market-adjustment
block and the learning-memory block each decay on their own, their coupling can destabilize the
joint system. Conversely, the same adaptation can be harmless, or stabilizing in models with
negative feedback. The sign, direction, magnitude, and timing of the response matter more than
the label attached to the decision maker.
We organize the analysis around three questions. First, what mathematical object describes
the joint response of the market and its adaptive participants? Second, what information is
required to estimate that object or a useful proxy? Third, what evidence would justify using it
to predict instability or evaluate a preventive intervention? Treating these as separate questions
avoids presenting a theoretical quantity as though it were already an observable supervisory
statistic.
The integrated manuscript now has four evidential layers. The original framework derives the
complete first-order system and tests its algebra. The first companion constructs exact finite-
window ambiguity. The second tests a proposed diagnostic against stronger full-information
estimators and narrows the defensible claim. The new study supplies an observable-only
measurement protocol, propagates its uncertainty and executes it in a simple account-consistent
market. The mathematical ingredients are largely established; the contribution proposed here is
the explicit measurement chain and its documented limits, not a renaming of ordinary stability
or system identification.

2 Related work and the precise research gap
2.1 Adaptive expectations and endogenous financial risk
? ] model endogenous expectations in an artificial stock market.?] analyze heterogeneous
beliefs and routes to complex asset-price dynamics. These are direct precedents for any claim
that agents and market conditions co-evolve. A convincing extension must identify something not
already explained by adaptive strategy selection: for example, a measurable feedback operator
that predicts behavior across materially different decision architectures.
Funding and trading conditions provide a second foundation.?] connect market liquidity
and funding liquidity, including mutually reinforcing liquidity deterioration under specified
conditions. Consequently, losses generated by common risk controls or liquidation pressure
cannot automatically be attributed to AI. A meaningful test must retain ordinary liquidity and
balance-sheet feedback in the non-AI benchmark.
2.2 Algorithmic interaction and financial-market experiments
? ] show that Q-learning pricing algorithms can sustain supracompetitive outcomes in a repeated
oligopoly experiment without direct communication. This establishes that individually trained
agents can generate consequential collective behavior, although product-market pricing is not
itselfamodelofsystemicfinancialloss.? ]bringlearningalgorithmsintosecurities-marketpricing
and identify consequences of limited experimentation and noisy feedback. Their mechanisms are
necessary comparators for a proposed market-making application.
The closest architecture-oriented predecessor is? ]. Their fund-redemption experiments
connect learning and reasoning architectures to coordination and financial fragility. A manuscript
that simply compares an LLM with a Q-learning agent would therefore require a much more
specific contribution. Our proposed distinction is to estimate a common feedback representation,
evaluate when it transfers across agent families, and test the additional predictive value of that
representation over conventional market statistics. That distinction is a target, not an achieved
empirical result.
2.3 Performative prediction and learning in games
? ] formalize situations in which a deployed predictor changes the distribution on which it is
subsequently evaluated.? ] extend the analysis to multiple decision makers and distinguish
performatively stable equilibria from Nash equilibria. These works already provide a language
for prediction–action–distribution feedback.
A recent preprint by? ] studies online algorithms and mixed performative stability. It is
important not to equate that learning-theoretic solution concept with every form of financial
stability. Convergence of average decisions or equilibrium distributions does not, without an
additional argument, bound interim drawdowns, market depth, collateral shortfalls, or the
solvency of a leveraged institution. Our intended bridge concerns such financial outcomes, not a
denial of existing convergence results.
2.4 Dynamical systems, robustness, and market simulation
Linearization, Lyapunov analysis, and common-metric robustness are established mathematical
tools [? ?]. Rebranding a spectral radius is not a new theorem. Their use here is to make
assumptions, state variables, and failure modes explicit before attempting empirical measurement.
The challenge is particularly acute for time-varying agents, discrete decisions, and partially
observed policy states.

finite-sample identification methods also exist under other assumptions [?]; we have not
implemented or subsumed them. Likewise, linear-system sample-complexity lower bounds are an
established literature [?]. The new price-response two-point example supplies a model-specific
observation limit, not a new general testing principle. The difference between noisy hidden
kernels and noisy measured prices is central to the present integration.

There are three different levels of claim. Amathematical resultfollows from an explicitly stated
model. Amechanism resultrequires controlled experiments demonstrating that a specified
feedback channel causes an outcome. Anexternal-validity resultrequires evidence that the
mechanism and its measurement apply beyond the experimental environment. This version

The primary domain for the first empirical paper should be a single market with an explicit
price-formation mechanism, inventories, and funding constraints. An equity-like limit-order-
book environment is a reasonable experimental starting point. A fund-redemption game is a
possible alternative, but these should not be combined into one vaguely specified “global market”
simulation. Banking, insurance, payments, and household finance have different state variables
and institutional constraints; they are later transfer tests.
We preserve the concept note’s six dimensions: machine autonomy exposure, behavioral

dimensions rather than sufficient statistics by assumption. A critical surface in those coordinates
may be model dependent, nonmonotone, or not estimable with available observations. Failure to
find a sharp boundary is an admissible research outcome.

7.3 An identification impossibility without provenance
Proposition 7.1(Observational equivalence of decision-maker labels).Consider two economies
with the same initial market-state distribution, the same market transition kernel conditional
on aggregate actions, and the same conditional law of aggregate actions given every observable
history. The economies can have different assignments of human and machine decision-maker
labels. They nevertheless induce the same law for all observable market histories. No estimator
using only those histories can consistently distinguish the two label assignments or identify
different autonomy shares attached solely to those assignments.
Proof. The initial observable distributions coincide. If observable histories coincide in distribution
through t, the assumed conditional action law and common market transition kernel imply the
same distribution for the next observation. Induction establishes equality of all finite-dimensional
observable distributions. Any statistic of those observations therefore has the same law in both
economies; it cannot converge in probability to two distinct label-dependent constants.
This is an application of ordinary observational equivalence, not a claim that financial
attribution is impossible with richer data. Account-level decision provenance, randomly assigned
architecture, authorized policy-update experiments, or credible external variation can break the
equivalence. Historical prices alone do not reveal whether a self-exciting pattern was produced
by humans, fixed algorithms, adaptive agents, or a mixture.
7.4 Estimating the response system
The general operator-estimation problem below is broader than the completed scalar experiment.
Sections ??–?? provide one restricted solution using signed filled probes and prices; they neither
reconstruct every private policy state nor identify AI participation from prices alone.
In a simulator with full state access, estimateJ by perturbing each normalized coordinate
around a specified operating point and measuring the resulting joint next-state response. Use
multiple perturbation sizes to test local linearity and independent random streams to quantify
agent stochasticity. Finite differences of a mean response estimate a mean-map derivative, not a
pathwise derivative of a discrete sampled action.

17.2 Environment and economic accounting
The first market should contain an exchange, finite cash and inventory balances, explicitly funded
positions, and a price-formation mechanism. Every fill transfers inventory and cash between
counterparties, with fees and external cash flows separately accounted for. The simulator must
reject unfunded or otherwise infeasible actions according to the declared rules. In a leveraged
extension, margin calculations and forced liquidation must be implemented and audited.
If public historical order-flow or price data are used for calibration, separate periods for
calibration, validation, and stress evaluation are required. Matching a handful of stylized facts
is a useful diagnostic but does not prove that a simulator captures crisis mechanisms. Price
replays without endogenous market impact cannot test the central feedback hypothesis: the
agents must be able to change subsequent market states.
ABIDES is a candidate infrastructure described in prior work [?]; the selected implementation,
version, matching rules, and modifications must be recorded. An independent second simulator
should use a materially different implementation while preserving the experimental economic
contract.
17.3 Agent families and matched baselines
A minimum design should compare adaptive expectation rules, a simple value-learning agent, a
more expressive learning policy, and an inference-only language-model policy. Optional online
adaptation for an LLM must be operationally defined: weights, external memory, retrieved
examples, prompts, or tool policies may update differently. Merely calling an LLM repeatedly is
not evidence of online parameter learning.
Each adaptive policy needs a paired baseline with the same architecture, information, con-
straints, and checkpoint, but the relevant adaptation channel disabled during evaluation. Addi-
tional baselines should include nonadaptive rule-based agents, ordinary risk-control feedback,
and adaptive expectation agents not described as AI. A human-like rule is not a human-subject
benchmark. A claim about replacing human decisions eventually requires actual human evidence
or a validated behavioral model with stated limits.
Differences in capital, risk tolerance, signal quality, latency, execution permissions, and
available computation should be held fixed where possible and explicitly decomposed where
impossible. A language model with richer information and a longer decision budget is not a
clean architecture-only comparison with a small tabular learner.

17.4 Factor design and compute discipline
Begin with a small mechanism-identification pilot. A proposed affordable pilot crosses three
participation levels, three response-sharing conditions, three update schedules, and two depth
regimes:3 ×3×3×2 = 54cells. Ten independent market seeds per cell would require 540
trajectories per paired policy condition. These are planning choices, not a power calculation or
completed run count.
Use the pilot only to identify implementation failures, estimate variance, and choose financially
meaningful outcome scales. Confirmatory seeds, shock paths, and held-out architectures must
then be fresh. A proposed second stage expands only the dimensions needed to distinguish
competing mechanisms. A large six-dimensional grid or 10,000-agent population should not
precede basic accounting validation and estimator recovery.
Avoid treating thousands of ticks within one market run as independent replications. The
independent unit is generally the market episode or another justified cluster. Paired shock paths
can improve precision, but they do not remove dependence within episodes or make different
agents in the same market independent samples.
17.5 Shock families and timing
Predeclare shocks to fundamentals, depth, funding requirements, information quality, update
synchronization, and decision-service availability. Apply information-corruption scenarios only
inside an isolated, authorized simulation. A shared model change should be tested both
synchronously and asynchronously, and compared with an equally large change to a non-AI
policy.
Hold shock realizations paired across treatment and baseline where the comparison requires
identical external conditions. Randomize shock timing within a predeclared range to reduce
phase-specific artifacts. Stress amplitudes must be reported in economically interpretable units
as well as normalized model units. There is no presumption that one calendar-time horizon
applies to both an exchange and a fund-redemption environment.
17.6 Primary outcomes and statistical analysis
For a price-forming environment, a useful primary outcome is a predeclared, horizon-limited
cumulative price dislocation from a simulated fundamental reference, normalized by the scale of
the initiating shock. Report the unnormalized numerator and denominator, and avoid ratios
near a zero denominator. In an empirical market, the fundamental reference is not observed
directly and requires a separate identification argument.
Secondary outcomes should include liquidation losses, execution shortfall, spread, depth, order
imbalance, inventory concentration, and recovery time. Distributional tail loss is meaningful
only after defining whose loss is measured and over what shock distribution. Mark-to-market
wealth transfers should not be mislabeled aggregate social losses. Deadweight liquidation costs,
default shortfalls, and real-economy outcomes are distinct quantities.
Compare a baseline predictor using volatility, depth, spreads, leverage, and ordinary order-flow
statistics with an augmented predictor that also uses the proposed feedback variables. Evaluate
temporal and architecture-held-out prediction with uncertainty, calibration, and economic effect
sizes. If the outcome is a distress event, report lead time and false alarms rather than only an
area-under-curve statistic. Thresholds and model-selection rules must be fixed without access to
the held-out outcomes.
Use cluster-aware uncertainty estimates or independent-run resampling, declare the multiple-
testing family, and report failed and null conditions. A practical power analysis should use pilot
variance and a justified smallest effect of interest, not a universal “30 seeds” rule.

17.7 Boundary estimation and terminology
A sharp-looking heatmap is not proof of a phase transition. First test whether a continuous,
nonlinear, or threshold model better describes held-out outcomes. Distinguish a local eigenvalue
crossing from a finite-sample increase in crisis frequency and from a true large-population phase
transition. A statistical-physics claim would require an appropriate limiting sequence, scaling
analysis, and finite-size evidence.
Report a region of uncertainty around any fitted boundary. When the estimator cannot
distinguish stable and unstable mechanisms under realistic observation noise, that limitation is
a result. A model-conditioned warning that is frequently indeterminate may still be scientifically
useful, but it should not be marketed as a universal instability detector.

19 Limitations, responsible use, and wider significance
19.1 Scientific limitations
The original tractable network model uses a common first-order learning rate; the integrated
scalar studies extend it to heterogeneous first-order rates. Both retain a fixed reference point
and nonnegative response coefficients. It omits endogenous innovation in strategies, strategic
deception, discontinuous liquidation rules, endogenous entry and exit, and general-equilibrium
wealth effects. Its spectral boundary is neither an asset-pricing theorem nor a universal condition
for crises. Some instability modes may be damped by limits, while finite disturbances can cause
defaults even around a locally stable equilibrium.
The complete joint state may be inaccessible for modern agents. Hidden memory, discrete
version changes, stochastic sampling, and proprietary risk overlays can undermine smooth
finite-dimensional approximations. Estimated feedback may be highly uncertain exactly where
extreme events matter. Identifying a persistent empirical feedback coefficient does not identify
an economically optimal intervention.
The original algebra checks and synthetic measurement, policy and intervention experiments
are not calibrated to a real exchange. The public crypto corpus is a depth-band/trade-tape
transfer layer, not queue-level L2: it lacks submitted orders, cancellations, queue priority, spread
and fills. A large number of repeated synthetic episodes does not supply external validity.
Independent identically distributed Gaussian episodes, fixed parameters, exogenous filled pulses
and a valid positive learning-rate floor are substantive assumptions. A repeatable episode is
natural in simulation but not automatically obtainable from a single evolving market. Selecting
windows after observing stress, using endogenous fills as if randomly assigned, or carrying an
invalid floor into the solver breaks the argument.
The response rectangle discards covariance geometry and the recurrence inequalities over-
approximate compatible models. Some inconclusive outcomes reflect this conservatism, not a
proven information limit. An equally informed full-covariance output-error estimator or set
should be tested before any broad efficiency claim. A true fixed-step instability may be limited
by nonlinearities and capital restrictions in a different model, while stable prices do not prevent
funded agents from defaulting.
The results provide no evidence of a global machine-participation threshold, real-market crisis
prediction, architecture-independent transfer, an AI-specific causal effect, or the superiority of an
abstention intervention. They also do not establish a new scientific paradigm. Any submission
must communicate this evidence boundary in the abstract as well as the methods.
19.2 Research ethics and operational boundaries
All agent-interaction and information-corruption experiments should remain in controlled envi-
ronments unless separately authorized. No experiment proposed here requires placing deceptive
orders, manipulating public markets, or using customer data without permission. Human-subject
work and sensitive institution-level records require relevant review, consent or lawful basis, data
minimization, and secure handling.
Publication should document model versions, datasets, licenses, prompts that may legally
be shared, code changes, and observed failure modes. Public generated explanations may be
studied as outputs, but should not be treated as verified internal reasoning or a causal account
of an agent’s decision. No claim depends on access to private model reasoning.

19.3 What would make the contribution globally useful
The potential value is a measurement and validation framework that helps institutions understand
interactions among decision systems, rather than another predictor of asset prices. To support
wider use, the framework would need reliable estimation from realistic observations, added value
over existing risk measures, transfer across relevant architectures and institutions, and evidence
about intervention costs and spillovers. The FSB’s monitoring work and its 2026 consultation
on responsible adoption provide institutional context for such questions, not endorsement of this
manuscript [? ?].
A globally relevant result need not claim that all financial laws have changed. A precisely
demonstrated failure of an existing diagnostic, together with a better measurable replacement
and clearly delimited applicability, could materially advance the field. Conversely, evidence that
conventional feedback and liquidity models already explain the alleged machine transition would
be an important correction to exaggerated claims.
20 Conclusion
The defensible question is not whether finance loses its familiar principles when AI becomes
prevalent. It is which feedback properties can be established from available observations
when adaptive decision rules interact with price formation. The original framework requires
the complete market–learning state for local analysis, distinguishes asymptotic stability from
transient and switching risk, and rejects provider diversity or individual abstention as automatic
safety certificates.
The companion studies strengthen the measurement question while correcting the initial
diagnostic claim. Exact finite windows can conceal opposite stability classes; fixed-order noisy
examples can remain hard to distinguish. Yet conventional full-moment identification extracts
more useful information than the original summary bound. Thus neither a vivid counterexample
nor a favorable weak-baseline result justifies claiming a universally better estimator.
The integrated evidence closes several narrower internal gaps: randomized filled-order quan-
tities and quoted prices can recover a conditional response interval; noisy and dependent
observations expose coverage and unresolved-call limits; a matched small neural policy does
not clear the adaptive non-neural baseline; and a local throttle reduces events in one synthetic
account. The public Binance layer adds checksum-verified held-out depth-band and trade-
tape targets, but not queue-level replay. These results remain conditional and do not support
AI-specific causality, real-market safety or publication acceptance.
The next consequential gates are independent external replication, queue-level/fill calibration
with authorized data, matched empirical AI/non-AI causal experiments, out-of-sample opera-
tional intervention validation, and human novelty, authorship, ethics and venue review. A global
safety framework still requires real institutional evidence, validated economic outcomes and
wider adoption, not simply a larger manuscript.

A.3 What the boundary does not imply
A positive real eigenvalue passing through one identifies loss of local linear stability in the
specified model. It does not determine the nonlinear attractor, whether prices crash or grow,
whether an institution defaults, or whether a finite disturbance remains inside the modeled region.
A nonlinear extension could saturate or switch into another regime. An observed financial crisis
can also occur without this crossing, for example when an external shock overwhelms solvency
buffers.
The result uses a single persistence scalar a and a common η. Allowing asset-specific
persistence, asymmetric learning rates, delayed observation, or signed response matrices removes
the simple formula unless further structure is proved. A claimed universal surface in six aggregate
variables would need to show that the omitted state is either irrelevant, bounded, or measured.
The current theorem does not establish such sufficiency.
A.4 Stochastic and nonlinear robustness
For a nonlinear mapΦt with a common reference point, suppose the inducedP-norm of every
derivative on a convex ball of radiusr is at mostγ <1. Then the same Lipschitz argument
yields the disturbance bound in??. If ¯b≤(1−γ)r and the initial state is inside the ball, that
ball is invariant. With an evolving reference point, its motion must appear as an additional
disturbance; ignoring that drift changes the claim.
If an estimated uncertainty set covers the actual matrices with probability at least1−δover
the relevant horizon, the robust bound holds on that coverage event. Marginal per-time coverage
does not automatically imply simultaneous horizon coverage. Model updates, adaptive sampling,
and post-selection can invalidate a naive confidence statement. These statistical issues are work
remaining, not solved by the deterministic norm inequality.
