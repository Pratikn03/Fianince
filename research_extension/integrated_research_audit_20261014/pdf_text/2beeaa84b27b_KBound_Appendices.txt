The formal theory of K-Bound (Section 4 through
Section 5) characterizes the exact population partial-
identiﬁcation frontier under a declared bound β on
the hidden disagreement-conditional residual γ . In
contrast, the empirical gate KGA (Section 6) predicts
measured evaluation-cell beneﬁt ∆ cell and constructs
a prediction interval [ ˆ∆ − ε, ˆ∆ +ε ]. This appendix pro-
vides the rigorous mathematical bridge between these
two formulations, corrects a common dimensional
misconception, provides a formal counterexample to
naive transfer, and establishes suﬃcient conditions
under which an enlarged conformal interval provably
covers population beneﬁt ∆ .

not estimate the population residual bound.
A.2
Counterexample: Perfect Cell Pre-
diction Does Not Imply Population
Coverage
We now prove that even an ideal empirical predictor
with zero cell-prediction error ( ε = 0) can fail to cover
population beneﬁt and can commit to false adaptation
with substantial probability.
Proposition 4 (Failure of automatic cell-to-popula-
tion transfer) . There exists a data-generating process,
a ﬁxed model pair (f0, f a), and a beneﬁt predictor ˆ∆
such that P(| ˆ∆ − ∆ cell| = 0) = 1 (perfect cell coverage
with ε = 0), yet the cell gate selects adapt with prob-
ability 0. 45 despite the true population beneﬁt being
strictly negative ( ∆ = − 0. 10).
Proof. Fix binary predictors f0(x) ≡ 0 and fa(x) ≡
1. Let the target input space consist of two points,
X = { a, b } , with input distribution
P(X = a) = 0 . 45, P(X = b) = 0 . 55,
and deterministic target labels Y (a) = 1 and Y (b) = 0 .
The zero-one population risks are:
RT (f0) = P(f0(X) ̸= Y ) = P(X = a) = 0 . 45,
RT (fa) = P(fa(X) ̸= Y ) = P(X = b) = 0 . 55.
The true population beneﬁt of candidate adaptation
is strictly negative:
∆ = RT (f0) − RT (fa) = 0 . 45 − 0. 55 = − 0. 10 < 0.

A.7 Explicit Implementation Boundaries
and Operational Scope
We distinguish three procedures and their evaluation
scope.
1.
Empirical KGA ( ˆ∆ ± ε ): The cell-beneﬁt pre-
diction interval uses the residual construction spec-
iﬁed for each study (Section 6). Current primary
CIF AR results, the 108-cell ImageNet-C replay,
and the corrected 27-cell SAR audit use Protocol
B’s separate ﬁtting, calibration, and scoring roles.
Historical pooled leave-one-out analyses retain Pro-
tocol A’s outcome-dependence limitation; natural
studies retain their separately documented data
partitions. These are cell-beneﬁt decisions, not
population certiﬁcates.
2. Population Extension ( ˆ∆ ± (ε + b)): The com-
pound decision interval incorporating ﬁnite-sample
Hoeﬀding concentration
b(m, δ ) (Theorem 4). This
procedure is exercised under controlled synthetic
diagnostic simulations with exact ground truth
(an observed 100% empirical population-inclusion
rate and zero wrong-direction commitments in the
ﬁnite trials); it is not a population guarantee for
the historical empirical benchmark tables, which
evaluate transductive evaluation-cell beneﬁt rather
than independent population draws. The sepa-
rate 108-cell benchmark replay below exercises the
enlarged interval as a sensitivity analysis, not as
fresh-sample validation.

F Inference Discipline
F.1
Point Estimates, Paired Gaps, and
Clusters
For a baseline b ∈ { always-adapt, always-freeze} , the
paired routing gap at condition j is
Gbj = regret b,j − regretKGA,j .
A positive mean gap means lower regret for KGA. All
paired routing-gap intervals reported in this paper
use this baseline-minus-KGA sign convention. The
point-estimate two-comparator criterion requires both
means to be positive. Interval support additionally
requires the lower conﬁdence limit for both paired
means to exceed zero at the declared resampling unit.
These deﬁnitions are intentionally separate: Table 17
can establish a point ordering, but not an inference
claim without the associated unit, interval artifact,
and justiﬁed coverage assumptions. A positive com-
puted lower endpoint is numerical interval support un-
der the resampling model, not veriﬁed coverage. Mul-
tiplicity adjustments do not repair invalid marginal
intervals or tests.
Condition-level resampling treats cells as exchange-
able and can understate uncertainty when sever-
ities and compositions share a corruption family.
Corruption-family resampling preserves observed
within-family dependence, but few clusters limit re-
liability; conservativeness is not guaranteed. Seed
resampling measures variation across repeated train-
ing or test streams only when the seeds represent
the intended deployment randomness. The compact
paper therefore does not use one generic “bootstrap
CI” label. An inference claim must state whether
the unit is cell, corruption–severity pair, corruption
family, seed, backbone, domain, or physical session.

The event in Eq. (4) is unconditional and concerns
measured cell beneﬁt. Its reported frequency di-

adapt decisions and is undeﬁned when no adaptation
occurs. A zero observed count should be accompanied
by the number of adapt decisions; otherwise a con-

exposes that denominator.
If the scored residual is included in the same ﬁ-
nite residual vector whose order statistic decides the
action, the number of residuals above the order statis-
tic is algebraically constrained. Such coverage is
not an independent empirical validation. Excluding
the scored residual removes direct self-inclusion, but
pooled LOO inclusion remains rank-constrained. It is
not independent validation and does not supply the
exchangeability or stability needed for a ﬁnite-sample
theorem. Clean split calibration and LOO empirical
calibration therefore remain diﬀerent protocol labels.

H
Extended KGA Implementation
Contract
H.1 State Isolation
The frozen and candidate predictors must not share
mutable normalization buﬀers, optimizer state, or
parameter storage during preview. The candidate is
created from a checkpoint, adapted on the unlabeled
window, and evaluated without overwriting the active
state. Only an adapt decision may replace the active
checkpoint. Freeze and abstain discard the temporary
state. A unit test should hash the active parameters
before and after each non-adapt decision and require
equality.
H.2 Evidence Determinism
Evidence extraction must deﬁne preprocessing, batch
order, numerical precision, class ordering, normaliza-
tion constants, and missing-value behavior. Paired
features require frozen and candidate outputs on the
same samples in the same order. Source statistics and
ATC thresholds are immutable calibration artifacts.
Evidence schema versions should be explicit rather
than inferred from vector length, because two schemas
can share a dimension while changing semantics.
H.3 Calibration Identity
The calibration identity should hash the source check-
point, candidate adapter class, adapted parameter
names, optimizer and learning rate, update steps,
evidence schema, beneﬁt-model hyperparameters, fea-
ture scaler, residual construction, α , and inference-
unit deﬁnition. The radius belongs to that identity.
Reusing it after a candidate or evidence change is
not a benign software optimization; it changes the
random variable whose residual is being covered.
H.4 Runtime Accounting
A deployment report should time cap-
ture/preprocessing, frozen inference, candidate
adaptation, candidate inference, evidence extraction,
beneﬁt prediction, interval decision, copy creation,
and rollback separately. Mean and tail latency
should be reported at the decision-window level.
Peak memory should include both model states and
optimizer buﬀers. The compact manuscript leaves
these cells unﬁlled because the canonical cross-dataset
manifest does not provide one comparable runtime
measurement. Omitting an unsupported number
is preferable to quoting controller-only latency as
end-to-end cost.

K Prospective Natural-Shift Con-
ﬁrmation Protocol
The sealed CCT-20 study supplies a completed
natural-shift target evaluation. Its 45 checkpoint–
location cells contain 1 helpful and 44 harmful can-
didate updates, but KGA makes 44 FREEZE, 1 AB-
STAIN, and 0 ADAPT decisions. It therefore matches
always-freeze and has lower regret than always-adapt,
providing evidence of conservative harm avoidance
under natural shift. The predeclared stronger rout-
ing criterion was not met because the policy never
selected ADAPT and did not select the single help-
ful cell. A second locked attempt on So2Sat-LCZ42
stopped at its development-feasibility gate with no
eligible Tent or SAR candidate. No held-out gate-
calibration rows or target inputs were accessed. The
following protocol therefore describes a new versioned
conﬁrmation, not a continuation or reinterpretation
of v1.
All So2Sat v1 gate-ﬁt outcomes are open and cannot
be used to tune a fresh conﬁrmatory arm. Its target
protocol also deﬁnes one action per city, whereas the
current runner creates one action per city–checkpoint
cell. That mismatch did not aﬀect the development
stop, but the target runner must remain disabled
until a versioned lock resolves the action unit. The
design below is future evidence and is not counted as
a completed target experiment in this paper.
K.1 Lock Before Target Labels
The protocol should name one source checkpoint,
one candidate adapter, one evidence schema, one
beneﬁt-model class, one calibration rule, one α , and
one target-domain family. Adapter hyperparameters
must be selected on development domains and then
frozen. The lock should hash the checkpoint, code re-
vision, conﬁguration, calibration records, and planned
scoring script. It must also name the test unit and
the multiplicity family. A later change creates a
new protocol version; it does not overwrite an earlier
development-stopped or incomplete arm.
Natural heterogeneity should be built through inde-
pendently deﬁned environments rather than by pool-
ing whichever benchmark conditions produce opposite
signs. Examples include hospitals, camera locations,
laboratory batches, acquisition days, physical devices,
or visual domains. Environment membership may be
observed at deployment, but the measured cell-beneﬁt
label remains hidden. The target test environments
should be absent from estimator ﬁtting, residual cali-
bration, threshold tuning, and candidate selection.
K.2 Three-Way Data Separation
Development data ﬁt hθ and select the evidence
pipeline. A separate calibration set produces residuals
for Eq. (11). The held-out target set is opened ex-
actly once after all software and conﬁguration hashes
match the lock. Splitting adjacent frames, patches,
or samples from the same session across these stages
is leakage even when ﬁlenames diﬀer. The split must
occur at the environment or session level that deﬁnes
deployment exchangeability.
The target stream is replayed identically for always-
freeze, always-adapt, simple gates, KGA without a
radius, and the full certiﬁcate. Model randomness
should either be frozen for paired replay or repeated
through independently trained checkpoints declared
in advance. Every policy receives the same ordered
windows. Labels are stored behind a separate au-
dit interface and are unavailable to the live decision
process.
K.3 Endpoints and Decision Rule
The primary endpoint is paired regret against each
ﬁxed policy. Success requires both baseline minus
KGA regret gaps to be positive and both predeclared
intervals to exclude zero, with coverage justiﬁed for
the declared estimand and sampling model. If the
intended claim concerns population risk rather than
measured cells, the protocol must also justify that
transfer. The false-adapt endpoint is
F Au with the
adapt count reported beside it. Secondary endpoints
are F Ac, adapt rate, freeze rate, abstain rate, commit-
ment rate, task accuracy, and latency. Conditional
false-adapt is descriptive unless a separate selective-
risk result is proved.
The primary certiﬁcate rule remains unchanged:
adapt if ˆ∆ − ε > 0, freeze if ˆ∆ + ε < 0, and abstain
otherwise. The experiment must not lower ε , change
α , or select a diﬀerent beneﬁt regressor after observing
target labels. Exploratory sensitivity analyses may be
reported, but they remain outside the primary bar.

Theorem Dependencies and De-
limited Scope
The compact paper uses three related logical state-
ments. First, the disagreement reduction is an iden-
tity under the binary setup. Second, the population
frontier is a uniform statement over a rich, declared
class. Third, the empirical certiﬁcate is a marginal
implication from interval coverage. The population
statements concern ∆ ; the reported empirical calibra-
tion concerns ∆ cell. Connecting these targets requires
an additional validity argument. Neither the assump-
tions nor the quantiﬁers are interchangeable.
Why the boundary is separate. For |M | < β ,
the construction chooses two latent residuals that
yield opposite nonzero beneﬁts. At |M | = β > 0, one
admissible residual can make the beneﬁt zero while
another retains the sign of M ; opposite nonzero signs
need not exist. Both cases block a uniform strict com-
mitment, but for diﬀerent reasons. At M = β = 0 ,
every law in the zero-residual class has zero beneﬁt
under the model. Abstention remains the maximal
action only under the paper’s strict-direction conven-
tion, not because either task-risk choice is worse.
Why a calibration residual need not be drift.
Let PT = PS, with equiprobable inputs a, b , deter-
ministic labels Y (a) = 1 , Y (b) = 0 , and candidate
fa ≡ 1. Set f0(a) = 0 , f0(b) = 1 , and s ≡ 1
2 . The
candidate is correct on half the source inputs, so this
constant score is source-calibrated in the ordinary
sense. Yet disagreement occurs only at a, where the
candidate is always correct. Thus M = 0 , γ = 1
2 ,
and ∆ = 1
2 , although the source and target laws are
identical. Conditioning on disagreement, not a dis-
tribution change, explains the nonzero residual. The
frontier requires a bound on this residual regardless
of its cause.
Why coverage is the premise. The certiﬁcate
proof is elementary once Eq. (10) holds: on the cov-
erage event, an interval wholly above zero cannot
contain a nonpositive ∆ cell. The scientiﬁc burden is
therefore to justify coverage for the declared outcome
and calibration/deployment pair. Joint exchangeabil-
ity of the residual scores is one route; known shift cor-
rection or another valid construction may be another.
Risk alignment can explain why a useful predictor
transfers, but it is not an additional logical premise
after coverage is given.
Why cell coverage is not population coverage.

1. Let the target input be a with probability 0. 45

that maps a to +1 and b to − 1 predicts ∆ cell perfectly
without reading the new label. With radius zero, cell
coverage is perfect, yet the gate selects ADAPT with
probability 0. 45 despite negative beneﬁt under the

Why a null is not an impossibility result. A
null benchmark row can arise because the true eﬀects

to obtain a useful empirical interval.

The paired-transport construction addresses a dif-
ferent estimand and assumption class. Relative to
the matrix-constraint conﬁdence regions in [
57], it
retains a shared joint table for two predictors and
optimizes their accuracy contrast under a supplied
conditional-transport budget. Tighter bounds than
separate subtraction follow on that same feasible set;
no dominance over another method’s feasible set is
implied. The probability-box theorem, numerical bi-
nomial endpoints and ﬂoating-point objective bounds
have the distinct scopes described in Appendix P.
The numerical endpoints are not certiﬁed inverse-
CDF bounds.
KGA uses historical labeled cell outcomes and is a
separate empirical construction. Its interval-to-action
implication applies inherited calibration ideas; it does
not establish a new conformal validity theorem or
estimate the population residual budget. Coverage
transfer requires its own assumptions. The analysis
in [ 37] quantiﬁes coverage loss beyond exchangeabil-
ity, including for original conformal procedures, and
studies modiﬁcations that can improve robustness.
Learned adaptation selection also predates this work.
MORPHEUS [ 58] selects before candidate execution,
whereas KGA’s shadow-candidate features incur can-
didate computation. A comparison must match both
information access and cost.
The theoretical guarantees concern prespeciﬁed
decisions and sampling contracts. Marginal wrong-
direction control does not imply an error bound con-
ditional on ADAPT. Candidates selected using evalu-
ation evidence, repeated uses and adaptive searches
require additional control. The maintained formaliza-
tion’s scope and exclusions are given in the formal
supplement; its check count is not a measure of origi-
nality.

R.3 Scope of independent conﬁrmation
No eligible fresh natural-shift study was established
by the bounded metadata review. Previously opened
environments cannot become fresh by changing a
seed. DomainNet’s six domains are insuﬃcient even
for the minimum ﬁnite 90% environment-level split-
conformal rank before reserving development and
target units; nine calibration units would only satisfy
the rank condition, not establish exchangeability or
adequate precision. So2Sat’s failed locked screen re-
mains binding and its target remains unscored. These
limitations leave useful independent natural-shift rout-
ing unresolved. The accompanying eligibility report
speciﬁes the population, access-history, sample-size,
protocol and cost requirements for a future study.
S Audit-Assisted Bounds for a
Fixed Finite Frame
Once labels are available for retrospective audit, a dif-
ferent design-based question becomes possible. Fix a
ﬁnite frame of N observations, its frozen and adapted
predictions, and its scoring rule. Draw audit indices
independently and uniformly with replacement after
ﬁxing these objects. Conditional on the frame and
its labels, any ﬁxed correctness indicator evaluated
at the sampled indices is iid Bernoulli with its ﬁnite-
frame frequency as success probability. Dependence
among the original images does not invalidate this
randomization argument; it does prevent interpret-
ing the frame as independent future environments.
Correct labels, faithful identities and the speciﬁed
randomization remain premises.
Paired accuracy and the binary residual. Let
D be the known prediction-disagreement stratum and
d = |D|/N . When d > 0, audit draws are indepen-
dently uniform within D, with replacement. Outside
D the paired correctness diﬀerence is zero. Within D,
let p+ and p− denote helpful and harmful outcome
frequencies. Two outward binomial intervals with
error budgets α/ 2 give
∆ N ∈ d[L+ − U− , U + − L− ]
with probability at least 1 − α over the audit draws.
No independence between the two intervals is required.
This construction also permits set-valued labels: both
diﬀering predictions can be correct, or both wrong.
It gives no macro-F1 guarantee.
For a single-label binary task, exactly one predic-
tion is correct on D. A single interval [L, U ] for
adapted correctness gives ∆ N ∈ 2d[L − 1/ 2, U − 1/ 2].
If ¯s is the known ﬁxed score mean on D, then
γ N ∈ [L − ¯s, U − ¯s], yielding
ˆβ = max{| L − ¯s|, |U − ¯s|} .
This is a labeled-audit conﬁdence bound for the ﬁxed
frame, not a label-free estimate of a uniform residual-
class radius. When D is empty, ∆ N = 0 and the
conditional residual is undeﬁned.
