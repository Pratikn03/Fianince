## Proposed title and abstract

**What Prices Reveal About Market Interventions**

Exchanges can respond to reinforcing order flow by changing a rule for the entire market or by restricting a particular class of traders. We study the information required for each action. In a model of traders who react to public prices, the aggregate feedback kernel determines how their combined orders respond to price histories. Distinct trader populations can generate the same public record when their kernels coincide. An intervention whose consequences depend only on aggregate feedback can therefore be evaluated within a class of populations that agree on the kernel. A restriction aimed at a particular class can have different effects within that class of observationally equivalent populations. In an exponential-memory model, distinguishing suitably constructed populations with $K$ nearby memory rates requires a number of Gaussian reset experiments of order $\Delta^{-(4K-2)}$ as the cluster width $\Delta$ shrinks. A matching weight-recovery rate holds on a separated rescaled class. We characterize how class-tagged flows alter this information problem. The analysis identifies when participant records can change an intervention decision even when the aggregate response is well estimated.

## Proposed introduction

An exchange observes unusually persistent order flow after a price move. It can attenuate feedback across the market, or it can restrict the traders responsible for the largest share of it. The first response requires an estimate of aggregate feedback. The second also requires the exchange to know who supplies it and how the rest of the market will respond after the restriction. Suppose two trader populations generate the same public trading record. An exchange might be able to assess a common reduction in feedback in both populations, yet be unable to establish that restricting the largest class would remove enough feedback. What must an exchange observe before it can distinguish these decisions?

We answer this question in a market where a subset of traders reacts to public prices and their combined orders feed back into subsequent prices. In the linear model, each group contributes a sequence of lagged responses. Their weighted sum is the feedback kernel. Under the model's common information and market rules, populations with the same kernel generate the same public outcomes under the same shocks. Identification of the kernel requires the observation design and restrictions stated below. Given the kernel, the relevant question for a proposed intervention is whether its effect varies across populations that share it.

This distinction matters even when nearby trader types are identifiable in principle. In our exponential-memory model, two populations with different weights can match their first $2K-1$ moments, indexed from zero through $2K-2$. Their first unmatched moment is of order $\Delta^{2K-1}$. The public responses are correspondingly close. Under Gaussian measurement noise, the information in a reset experiment scales with the square of this response gap, so distinguishing the populations with bounded error requires order $\Delta^{-(4K-2)}$ experiments. Many aggregate functionals remain estimable without a cost that diverges as these types approach each other. A precise estimate of market-wide feedback can therefore coexist with severe uncertainty about the effect of a class-specific restriction.

The distinction also gives a criterion for evaluating disclosure. Class tags matter when they distinguish populations that imply different actions, or when they reduce the uncertainty relevant to an action with a tight safety margin. This criterion directs attention to an economic outcome: how often the public record would lead an exchange to approve a restriction that leaves too much feedback in place, or to reject one that would have worked. The value of a record depends on which decision it changes and the loss attached to a mistake.

## The highest credible ceiling

The current paper can stand as a specialized financial-microstructure theory paper after proof and scope review. A credible top general-interest finance version needs a sharper economic result: **a quantitative answer to what extra market surveillance buys for a specific decision**. That requires either convincing evidence from a real intervention with participant records or a theory result that changes the design of oversight under strategic response. Journal placement remains a judgment, and this comparison does not establish priority over all working papers.

**Theory target.** Start from the manuscript’s existing “Market-wide versus targeted action” proposition. Its hard pair can place the residual feedback after halting the largest class on opposite sides of a common ceiling. The next step is to put that comparison into a loss function with both the cost of a breach and the cost of restricting liquidity. Specify whether the decision maker is given the identity of the largest class or must identify and select it from the record. These are different decision problems. Let $P$ and $Q$ be public-record laws for two economies in which the same restriction is respectively safe and unsafe. For any randomized decision rule, the sum of the two error probabilities is at least $1-\lVert P-Q\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}$. The bound follows by writing the rule's probability of approving the restriction as a function valued in $[0,1]$ and applying the definition of total variation. With equal prior weights, average classification error is at least half that quantity. This is a decision-theory baseline. The paper-specific result to establish is how the existing hard pair translates into economically meaningful regret, and how alternative tag designs reduce that regret under the same reporting budget and decision timing. The new result should identify a regime where targeted action is worth its information cost and a regime where a common intervention is preferable. A market-wide comparator should have a positive safety margin so that near-threshold classification does not dominate both problems.

**Empirical target.** Build two records from the same underlying events: anonymized market data and class-tagged data. Define cohorts and estimate both models before an event that changes the exposure of one class, such as an independently documented targeted rule change. Use pre-event predictions of post-event flow and price response, with a common market-wide comparator and inference that reflects the number of independent events. Report how the recommended action changes and its uncertainty. Where no credible event exists, use the tagged record as a measurement benchmark and label any simulated restriction as a counterfactual under maintained rules. Wallets are units of observation. A wallet's behavior alone cannot establish firm identity, algorithm ownership, or a strategy's invariance after intervention. Historical fills and historical margin-state reconstruction are separate feasibility checks. [Hyperliquid historical data documentation](https://www.hyperliquiddocs.io/historical-data.html)

## Evidence and editorial decisions

- Keep the exponential-memory identification bound and the policy comparison in the main paper. Move pooling, drift, and broader memory families to the supplement unless they directly change the decision being studied.
- Put the “4,000 years” number in a calibration table. It is a model-implied illustration, and its usefulness turns on the chosen spacing, noise and stability parameters.
- Report P1F as void under its registered coverage rule. It retained zero of 30 symbols and produced no forecast statistic. Under a future usable design, a positive forecast gain alone would not identify informed trading because persistent noninformational flows can also generate gains.
- Scope the observation claim to the modeled public-signal cohort and the specified observation experiment. Keep the claims about equilibrium invariance tied to the same game and information structure. Distinguish the effect of shutting off flow within the model from an equilibrium in which remaining traders may change strategies.
- Replace blanket author-verification language in the reviewed PDF with the actual review status. Report the Lean results as checks of named statements under stated hypotheses and record the paper-only arguments awaiting review.
- Make an early figure show the two intervention predictions side by side for the same pre-intervention public record. Use the model's own pair and show which feature of the records a tag reveals. Keep measured observations visually distinct from model outputs.
