\section{Full Study Coverage and the Evidence Boundary}
This review revision retains all ten study questions and the information-contract and financial-risk program of the original 48-page manuscript. The original submission PDFs are preserved separately. Correcting an invalid result is different from abandoning its scientific question: the evidence below records successful reanalyses, model rejections, and specific missing dependencies. This version is a review candidate with open scientific gates, not a declaration of publication readiness. The page-disposition appendix connects every original page to its revision obligation.

\subsection{The Ten-Study Program}
\paragraph{Study 1: baseline aggregate diagnostics.}
The 768 asset-day archive answers a descriptive question about the fitted dynamics of a filtered price/flow proxy. Its matrices and derived invariants are internally consistent, but rechecking a stored matrix is not reproducing the transformation from exchange observations. The historical generator used close-price klines and forward-aligned flow. Accordingly, the archived means remain a separately labeled historical diagnostic. The primary observed-timing reanalysis on the traceable Coinbase sample shows how strongly the same scientific quantity changes under a corrected information convention.

\paragraph{Study 2: cross-asset heterogeneity.}
Differences in the archived $Q$ means across eight symbols can be reproduced from their saved fits. They do not identify institutional participation or a monotone effect of capitalization, because no participant identities or independently aligned capitalization data enter these estimates. A study of heterogeneity remains useful when sampling, filtering and uncertainty are held fixed. The appendix reports the actual saved means rather than interpreting the rank ordering as a causal mechanism.

\paragraph{Study 3: fee responsiveness.}
The saved 2022 panel permits reproduction of a treated-minus-control contrast around the fee change. Classical, asset-cluster and date-cluster uncertainty differ substantially. A one-treated-asset comparison needs a defensible counterfactual and assumptions about spillovers. A broad confidence interval containing zero does not establish that fees leave feedback unchanged; the stated equivalence margin also fails under the reported date-cluster test. These are results of the intervention study, not reasons to exclude it.

\paragraph{Study 4: dynamics around the intervention.}
The twice-monthly dates identify descriptive contrasts relative to the last pre-treatment date. Recovering a date-specific point contrast does not recover its standard error. The inspected event-study generator assigns pooled DiD uncertainty scaled by $\sqrt2$, without estimating the covariance of the individual contrast, and its clock differs from the old monthly display. The original joint pretrend statistic is therefore not retained as verified evidence. The dated contrasts remain available as a different reference construction. Section~\ref{sec:monthly_event_rebuilt} now fits a monthly asset/date fixed-effects design with declared covariance alternatives, reconstructs the pretrend references, and identifies saturation-induced covariance failures; causal assumptions remain unresolved.

\paragraph{Study 5: venue transmission.}
A saved separate-venue comparison is evidence of differences or similarities in marginal fitted dynamics. Transmission requires synchronized joint observations. The old displayed joint matrix contradicts its own spectral-radius claim, and its generating fit was not established in the expanded artifact search. Section~\ref{sec:restored_joint} now restores the original overlap from checksum-verified exchange archives and supplies an independently fitted joint process, while retaining clock/missingness sensitivities and the distinction between predictive relations and identified information shocks.

\paragraph{Study 6: forecast-error variance.}
FEVD additionally requires the residual covariance and a declared innovation identification or generalized decomposition. Percentages cannot be reconstructed from separate marginal summaries. Horizon labels must count the actual bin width: ten fitted steps at ten seconds per step do not represent ten seconds. Ordering sensitivity and the relation between quote midpoints and transaction-close proxies matter. No untraced percentage is offered as an observed systemic contribution. The subsequently restored joint study supplies a complete residual covariance, explicit generalized normalization, and all twenty-four orthogonal orderings; their sensitivity prevents a causal systemic-contribution interpretation.

\paragraph{Study 7: liquidity-state dependence.}
The first-50,000-record controlled regression is a bounded diagnostic on a recorded sample, not a comprehensive nonlinear identification design. Its slope difference is not detected with HAC uncertainty, while residual scales differ modestly. The historical switching code fits a scalar differenced-EMA regression, uses ad hoc transition updates, and creates a dimensionally chosen index by multiplying a slope by ten. That index is not the structural $S$ in the cohort theorem. A justified regime likelihood, initialization audit and uncertainty are required before a structural bifurcation conclusion.

\paragraph{Study 8: historical stress scenarios.}
The inspected historical script simulates an E-mini path with imposed depth dynamics and assigns the reported feedback parameters directly. The Treasury routine returns fixed values. These outputs can motivate an illustrative stress scenario, but they are not estimates from ingested historical TAQ. Moreover, the protected E-mini scenario changes depth and external liquidation flow as well as the proposed interception rule. Its improvement cannot isolate the rule's causal effect. Authenticated historical observation, where recoverable, would be a separate empirical test.

\paragraph{Study 9: the supplied equity sample.}
Session-reset fits reproduce aggregate diagnostics for supplied STOCK/DAY identifiers. Their counts differ across stocks, and their index is an event clock. The original benchmark/provider map has not been independently authenticated. The estimates therefore support replication of the local calculation, conditional on the supplied representation, rather than a fully authenticated cross-market mechanism. The session audit preserves actual stock counts and excludes cross-session transitions.

\paragraph{Study 10: funded dealers.}
The 48-cell replay reproduces archived halt counts exactly under preserved flow-moment calibration. Matched independent and persistent-flow comparators are important because inventory accumulation can arise without adaptive learning. Simulation seeds quantify path variability under that calibration; they are not independent market observations. The fixed-coordinate transient examples address a different deterministic experiment. Keeping the two studies separate prevents a simulated halt percentage from being presented as a theorem or historical intervention outcome.

\subsection{Information Contracts and Their Distinct Scientific Roles}
Public observation, controlled aggregate probing and audited internal state provide different information. A public tape lacks participant identity and policy-state disclosures. An aggregate probe can excite a transfer function under its reset, observation-noise and admissible-input assumptions, but does not automatically separate equal-rate cohorts. Audited cohort-specific state/action histories can remove an aggregation ambiguity in the specified linear model. They cannot on their own establish a correct fundamental-value proxy, a stationary policy, or a calibrated balance-sheet loss mapping.

A regression on disclosed state must specify whether the same-time price innovation enters the state update, which disturbances remain unobserved, and whether measurement error corrupts the disclosed variable. Ordinary least squares consistency cannot be inferred solely from observing a variable called ``internal state.'' Its moment restrictions need independent justification. Similarly, a Kalman recursion is an MMSE statement only under the stated distributional/independence model, and its steady-state gain concerns filtering error rather than financial solvency.

An operational information contract would need an authenticated schema, timestamps, units, missingness policy and verification of access to the declared variables. The present analysis defines conceptual information requirements. It does not claim that a deployed schema implements them or that firms' supplied signals satisfy the model. Uncertainty sets for parameters and output exposures must precede a regulatory action based on a calculated radius. Aggregate model rejection is itself a reason to withhold a structural certification.

\section{Repeated Innovations: A Conditional Loss Bound}
The initial-shock theorem does not control a stream of innovations. This gap has a direct conditional linear-system solution, stated here separately from empirical calibration.
\begin{proposition}[Exact induced gain for bounded innovations]
Let $z_0=0$, $z_t=Jz_{t-1}+Dw_t$, $y_t=Cz_t$, and $\rho(J)<1$. Use fixed Euclidean coordinates and suppose $\|w_t\|_2\le r$ separately at each time. Define
\[
G=\sup_{t\ge1}\max_{\|v\|_2=1}
\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}\|D^\top(J^k)^\top C^\top v\|_2.
\]
Then $G<\infty$ and
\[
\sup_{\|w_t\|_2\le r}\sup_{t\ge1}\|y_t\|_2=rG.
\]
For $G>0$ and loss budget $B>0$, the exact radius for this repeated-input experiment is $B/G$. For a scalar output, $G=\sum_{k=0}^\infty\|CJ^kD\|_2$. With an additional initial ball $\|z_0\|_2\le r_0$, the conservative sufficient condition $r_0 M_C+rG\le B$ controls all times.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $t$, write $y_t=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}CJ^kDw_{t-k}$. Euclidean duality gives $\|y_t\|_2=\max_{\|v\|_2=1}v^\top y_t$. The compact finite product of input balls and the unit sphere permits interchanging their maxima. Each input independently maximizes its linear functional to $r\|D^\top(J^k)^\top C^\top v\|_2$, proving the finite-horizon equality. Taking the supremum over $t$ proves the formula. Schur stability implies summability of $\|J^k\|_2$, so $G\le\sum_k\|CJ^kD\|_2<\infty$. For a scalar output, the dual sphere is $\{-1,1\}$ and the sum is independent of its sign. The initial-condition inequality follows from the triangle inequality and the definition of $M_C$.
\end{proof}

This result requires neither nonnegative coupling nor independence of the innovations. It controls bounded adversarial inputs in a fixed linear system; Gaussian innovations are unbounded and do not satisfy its almost-sure premise. A stochastic high-probability result needs a declared horizon and tail law. Applying the proposition to orders requires the actual input operator $D$, the economically calibrated loss operator $C$, fixed units and a justified parameter set. It does not establish market execution safety from a descriptive proxy VAR.

For computation, let $G_N$ be the scalar-output partial sum through $k=N-1$. Choose an integer $h$ with $q=\|J^h\|_2<1$, which exists for a Schur-stable matrix. Commutativity of powers and submultiplicativity give the tail certificate
\[
0\le G-G_N\le
\frac{\|D\|_2}{1-q}\sum_{j=0}^{h-1}\|CJ^{N+j}\|_2.
\]
Thus a truncated calculation can provide an interval for $G$ rather than assuming that a finite horizon captured its maximum. Near instability the required horizon and uncertainty can be large. For a vector output, the partial dual optimization gives a lower bound and the summed operator norms give a conservative upper bound.

\section{Measurement and Inference Methods}
\subsection{The observable transformation}
The primary transformation forms past-observed bins, uses the last recorded midpoint in each bin and sums deduplicated executed signed flow. A missing bin splits the sequence; it is not filled from future values. The price state is log midpoint minus its exponential reference with $\alpha=2/(W/\Delta+1)$. Five reference horizons are excluded after a segment reset. This changes both the estimand and its sample count. For raw-return diagnostics, differences are formed within the same contiguous segment. Daily aggregation treats multiple segments on one date as parts of one date, rather than independent observations.

The observable proxy is not latent fundamental mispricing. Even a martingale filtered through an EMA produces memory. If a statement concerns fundamental stability, an observation-law relation is needed. Our reduced-form fit includes an intercept, and the off-diagonal ratio defining $Q$ is a diagnostic of that fit. Dividing by $1-J_{22}$ can amplify uncertainty when flow persistence is close to one; the invariants must be read alongside the full fitted radius, sampling clock and denominator.

\subsection{Timing recovery}
The recovered Gemini scratch script independently compares one-minute reported buys against sums of one-second records under three interval closures. The deduplicated past interval reproduces every one of the tested 1,995 windows for each asset. The future interval does not. The corresponding duplicate-render counts are 79,581 (BTC), 73,762 (ETH), and 65,973 (ADA). These arithmetic checks support how to read the local flow timestamps; they do not authenticate the original provider or identify a trader's policy. The revision also reconstructs each full local cache from its raw CSV with exact agreement.

\subsection{Dependence and small samples}
Means and confidence intervals based on a daily panel describe variation across observed dates. They are not probabilities that an individual latent learning parameter falls in the displayed interval. For the saved Binance panel, all assets on a date are kept together in the date-block summary. The DiD covariance calculations state whether they use classical residual variance, eight asset clusters or twenty-four date clusters, with corresponding reference degrees of freedom. Asset clustering with one treated asset remains fragile, even when the mechanical covariance calculation is correct.

Eight assignment contrasts permit only a coarse descriptive rank fraction unless an exchangeable assignment design is defended. The equivalence margin is a sensitivity choice, not a pre-registered economically optimal tolerance. Failing a TOST or failing to reject a zero effect cannot be combined into evidence that the effect is exactly zero. An event-specific covariance must be calculated for its own contrast, including uncertainty in the reference date.

\subsection{Minimum distance and rejection}
The reanalysis matches eleven covariance/lag moments to seven parameters and checks local rank seven. Segments contribute their own lagged products; products do not bridge discontinuities. The fitted system is restricted to be stable and innovation covariance positive definite. The diagnostic uses HAC weighting with ten bins and a four-degree-of-freedom asymptotic reference. A successful optimizer supplies a candidate moment fit; rejection assesses the maintained moment model. It cannot establish structural validity through a nearby reduced-form point estimate.

\subsection{Null families and specification sensitivity}
The nulls are declared no-adaptation data-generating families, not fitted replicas of every feature of the real sample. They isolate independence, persistent exogenous flow, order splitting with impact, and common news. Sixty repetitions per family under the same transformation show that a small positive diagnostic is compatible with mechanisms other than adaptive learning. This is a bounded falsification exercise; a stronger mechanistic comparison needs calibrated information and uncertainty. The sampling/window grid is exploratory, and all admissible declared cells are retained. The $W=\Delta=60$ cell is excluded because the chosen EMA becomes degenerate, not because its empirical result is unfavorable.

\section{Restored Joint-Venue Study}
\label{sec:restored_joint}
The original separate-venue generator did not establish its reported joint matrix or FEVD. That contradiction remains in the audit. A subsequent recovery retrieved the exact original study's Binance BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT and ADAUSDT daily one-second kline objects for April 8--18, 2021: thirty-three exchange archives, each verified against a newly retrieved official checksum and its ZIP CRC. These are current public archives for the original sample dates, not byte-authenticated copies of the original downloads. Three recovered April 10 asset-days reproduce every saved baseline coefficient, invariant, observation count and mean price exactly. Full original-panel recovery and reproduction are recorded separately; this three-row overlap comparison alone does not close that full-panel gate.

The new independent joint study joins the three recorder caches to restored exchange observations. Binance supplies transaction closes and signed quote flow $2V^{\rm buy}-V^{\rm total}$, rather than order-book midpoints. Coinbase supplies recorded midpoint and deduplicated flow, with provider authentication still open. Quote currencies differ (USD and USDT); basis movements and common information can affect the relation. The two timestamp conventions do not establish subsecond simultaneity. We retain integer-second offset sensitivities of minus and plus one second for the recorder.

Ten-second bins require all ten integer-second records from both venues. Incomplete bins are excluded; neither series is filled from future observations. Gaps and UTC date changes reset both filters together, followed by a 150-bin burn-in. Segments require at least 500 retained bins. A four-variable VAR with separate segment intercepts is fit to the within-segment centered observations. The ordered state is recorder price deviation, recorder flow, Binance price deviation, Binance flow. Each variable is divided by its retained-sample standard deviation for estimation; the saved fit also supplies the raw-unit matrix and covariance. The filtered state uses a 300-second EMA; a raw-return variant measures a different estimand.

\section{Why the Observation Contract Changes the Abstention Result}
The scalar price-surveillance theorem cannot be promoted into a mandatory-abstention theorem for every richer observation experiment. The following constructive result resolves a specific full-observation case rather than leaving that distinction informal.
\begin{proposition}[Two-step identification under an exact audited contract]
\label{prop:exact_audited_contract}
Consider the single-cohort recursion
\[
x_t=a x_{t-1}+\kappa u_{t-1}+w_t,\qquad
u_t=(1-\eta)u_{t-1}+\eta q x_t,
\]
with aggregate cohort demand $u_t$, $x_0=u_0=0$, known $a\in[0,1)$ and $\kappa>0$, unknown $\eta\in(0,1)$ and $q\in\mathbb R$, and $w_1$ has a continuous nondegenerate Gaussian law. Suppose the true pricing error $x_t$, cohort demand $u_t$, and their exact timing are observed without measurement error for $t=1,2$. Then, with probability one, either $q=0$ is identified and the model is Schur stable, or both $q$ and $\eta$ are recovered exactly:
\[
\vartheta=\frac{u_1}{x_1},\qquad
\beta=\frac{u_2-\vartheta x_2}{u_1},\qquad
\eta=1-\beta,\qquad q=\frac{\vartheta}{\eta}.
\]
Consequently the exact signed-feedback Jury conditions decide stability with zero error in this observation experiment, including alternatives arbitrarily close to its stability boundaries.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The reset gives $x_1=w_1$, so $x_1\ne0$ almost surely. The first demand observation gives $u_1=\eta q x_1$. If $u_1=0$, then $q=0$ because $\eta>0$; the triangular zero-feedback dynamics have eigenvalues $a$ and $1-\eta$, both strictly inside the unit circle. Otherwise $u_1\ne0$ and $\vartheta=\eta q$. The second demand equation gives $u_2-\vartheta x_2=(1-\eta)u_1$, yielding the recovery formulas. With $Q=\kappa q$, the signed Jury interval is
\[
-\frac{(2-\eta)(1+a)}{\eta}<Q<1-a.
\]
All parameters in this decision are known or exactly recovered. Equality is a unit-circle boundary and is classified as not Schur stable. Process innovations do not obstruct recovery because the exact same-time price appears in the observed demand recursion.
\end{proof}
This is an exact mathematical information contract, not a claim that public midpoint records reveal fundamental mispricing or that a dealer supplies noiseless policy states. Timing errors, unknown state definitions, additional cohorts, discretionary orders, noisy disclosure or unknown $a,\kappa$ change the experiment. Numerical floating-point decoding does not inherit an exact boundary decision without an error analysis. The result demonstrates why a price-only lower bound and an audited-state guarantee need separate assumptions; it does not undo the scalar Gaussian lower bound.

\begin{proposition}[Bounded merged-cohort identification under exact disclosure]
\label{prop:exact_multicohort_contract}
Let $x_0=y_{i,0}=0$, $x_t=a x_{t-1}+\kappa u_{t-1}+w_t$, $y_{i,t}=(1-\eta_i)y_{i,t-1}+\eta_i x_t$, and $u_t=\sum_i q_i y_{i,t}$, with known $a\in[0,1)$ and $\kappa>0$, rates $\eta_i\in(0,1)$, and finite signed weights. Suppose at most $K_{\max}\ge1$ distinct rates have nonzero merged weights. Under exact observation of true $x_t$ and cohort demand $u_t$ for $t=1,\ldots,2K_{\max}$ and a continuous nondegenerate Gaussian first innovation, the merged rate--weight measure is identified almost surely. This identifies the closed-loop stability class under the maintained model, although participant counts within merged groups remain unidentified.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Iterating the reset cohort recursion gives
\[
u_t=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}h_k x_{t-k},\qquad
h_k=\sum_{j=1}^{K}c_j r_j^k,\quad r_j=1-\eta_j\in(0,1),\quad c_j=\eta_j\bar q_j\ne0,
\]
where $K\le K_{\max}$ counts nonzero merged groups. Since $x_1=w_1\ne0$ almost surely, the first $2K_{\max}$ moments are recovered recursively:
\[
h_n=\frac{u_{n+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}h_k x_{n+1-k}}{x_1},\qquad n=0,\ldots,2K_{\max}-1.
\]
For uniqueness, the difference of two candidate signed measures has at most $2K_{\max}$ distinct nodes. Vanishing first $2K_{\max}$ moments implies zero weights by the full column rank of the corresponding Vandermonde matrix. Thus the merged measure is unique, including signed weights.

Constructively, the $K_{\max}\times K_{\max}$ Hankel matrix $H=(h_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^{K_{\max}-1}$ factors as $V\,\mathrm{diag}(c_j)V^\top$, where $V_{ij}=r_j^i$. Its rank is $K$; the leading $K\times K$ block is invertible. Solve
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}h_{i+j}b_j=-h_{i+K},\qquad i=0,\ldots,K-1.
\]
The roots of $r^K+\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}b_jr^j$ are the distinct memory nodes. The first $K$ moments then determine the $c_j$ through a square Vandermonde system, and $\bar q_j=c_j/(1-r_j)$. When $H=0$, the merged measure is empty. The identified merged groups determine the minimal closed-loop Jacobian. Additional same-rate difference modes and canceled groups have eigenvalues $r_j\in(0,1)$, so they do not change its instability classification.
\end{proof}
The constructive step uses established finite exponential-sum and annihilating-filter algebra, rather than a new recovery algorithm; see, for example, \citet{vetterli2002}. The contribution here is the explicit observation-contract comparison for the stated market recursion. Exact algebra does not imply robust finite-precision recovery: close rates, small weights, noisy demand, and small $x_1$ can make inversion ill-conditioned. With no bound on merged model order, this finite observation budget does not supply the same guarantee. Anonymous recorded prices and total executed flow do not certify this exact true-state/cohort-demand contract.

\section{Diagnosing the Maintained Linear Approximation}

We retain four fixed HAC bandwidths (5, 10, 20 and 40 bins) and six optimizer starts for each asset/bandwidth. Let $\Gamma_0\succ0$ be arbitrary and let $K=M(I+M^\top M)^{-1/2}$ for unrestricted $M\in\mathbb R^{2\times2}$. Set
\[
J=\Gamma_0^{1/2}K\Gamma_0^{-1/2},\qquad
\Sigma=\Gamma_0^{1/2}(I-KK^\top)\Gamma_0^{1/2}.
\]

Its eleven moment restrictions and seven local parameter directions retain four overidentifying degrees of freedom. The weighting rank is eleven and the parameter Jacobian rank seven in all reported cases. The six starts agree numerically, without constituting a certified global optimization proof. All twenty-four global/within-segment centering cases reject under their asymptotic reference. \subsection{Heteroskedastic Identification Requires More Than Covariance Fitting}
The inspected C2 generator applies an unweighted covariance fit to price differences and legacy forward-aligned flow, rather than innovations from a defended dynamic reduced form. Its rolling price volatility includes the contemporaneous state and backward-fills initialization; regimes use pooled state-dependent quantiles. Conditional shock orthogonality, constant slopes and distinct structural variance profiles are assumptions, not consequences of those operations \citep{rigobon2003identification}. A bounded optimizer with one starting point supplies neither a causal interpretation nor uncertainty.

An exact algebraic example shows why shock labels matter. Let
\[
A=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\lambda\\-\alpha&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\Omega_r=A^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(v_{p,r},v_{f,r})A^{-\top}.
\]
Swapping and rescaling the structural equations yields another normalized matrix with $\lambda'=1/\alpha$ and $\alpha'=1/\lambda$, and transformed diagonal variances. It produces exactly the same regime covariances. The retained witness has $\lambda=1/20$, $\alpha=-3/20$, product $-3/400$, and an alternative product $-400/3$ under three nonproportional variance regimes. Bounds on the two slopes implicitly exclude this alternative; they are not independently authenticated shock labels. External sign or exclusion information could distinguish branches, but that information must be defended.

A separate bounded Gaussian illustration starts with independent structural errors and partitions observations using the contemporaneous reduced-form price. Conditional errors become correlated within the selected regimes. This demonstrates that endogenous selection can violate the needed restriction even when unconditional errors are orthogonal. It does not estimate actual market shock correlations. Reopening causal identification requires a stated innovation equation, valid timing, defended conditional orthogonality/invariance and shock labels, plus uncertainty and adequacy checks. The current archive does not establish market-maker buffering from its negative fitted product.
\section{Signed Portfolios, Model Uncertainty, and Marked Losses}
\label{sec:risk_contract_extensions}
The nonnegative-output result does not cover every hedged portfolio. This section states a signed extension for a specified output location, an explicit failure for general portfolio weights, and sufficient uncertainty/noise contracts. The arguments use established block-power and quadratic-Lyapunov tools; they do not constitute a new general control method or authenticate a market loss calibration.

\begin{proposition}[Signed directed coupling with downstream-only output]
\label{prop:signed_target_coupling}
Let $A,B$ be fixed Schur-stable matrices, $L$ any real matrix, $\theta\ge0$, and
\[
J_\theta=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\\theta L&B\end{bmatrix},\qquad C=[0,C_B],\quad C_B\ne0.
\]
Use the same fixed Euclidean initial-state ball and output units for every $\theta$. Then $M_C(J_\theta)=\sup_{t\ge0}\|CJ_\theta^t\|_2$ is nondecreasing in $\theta$, without sign restrictions on $A,B,L,C_B$. Consequently its exact initial-shock radius $B_{\rm loss}/M_C$ is nonincreasing.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $t\ge1$, the lower-left block is $\theta S_t$, where $S_t=\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}B^{t-1-j}LA^j$. Thus $CJ_\theta^t=[\theta C_BS_t,C_BB^t]$. Its row Gram matrix is
\[
\theta^2 C_BS_tS_t^\top C_B^\top+C_BB^t(B^t)^\top C_B^\top.
\]
It increases in the positive-semidefinite order with $\theta$, so its largest eigenvalue and the spectral norm are nondecreasing. Taking the supremum over $t$, including the unchanged $t=0$ term, preserves the ordering.
\end{proof}
The output support is essential. For $\rho=0.85$, $h=2$, let
\[
J_\theta=\begin{bmatrix}\rho&h&0\\0&\rho&0\\0&\theta&\rho\end{bmatrix},\qquad C=[1,0,-1].
\]
All eigenvalues stay at $\rho$, while
\[
CJ_\theta^t=[\rho^t,(h-\theta)t\rho^{t-1},-\rho^t].
\]
At $\theta=h$ the middle term cancels and $M_C=\sqrt2$, whereas at $\theta=0$ the peak exceeds five. Increasing coupling can therefore enlarge a hedged portfolio's radius. This is an exact-output statement in fixed coordinates; it does not identify market hedging effectiveness from the observed VAR.

\subsection{Amplification Inside the Stated Multi-Asset Model}
An unrestricted Jordan example need not have a structural cohort parameterization. The stated leader--satellite equations supply a separate witness. Order states by asset as $(x_1,y_1,x_2,y_2)$, fix $a_i=0.5$, $\eta_i=0.2$, $q_i=0.2$, $K_{ii}=1$, $K_{12}=0$, and let $K_{21}=\theta$. Both diagonal blocks are
\[
A_i=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0.2\\0.1&0.84\end{bmatrix},
\]
and the lower-left block is $\theta\left[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0.2\\0&0.04\end{smallmatrix}\right]$. The spectrum is independent of $\theta$ and is strictly inside the unit circle. For downstream price output $C=[0,0,1,0]$, a unit leader-memory shock produces loss $0.2\theta$ at one step. Hence $M_C\ge0.2\theta$ and the exact radius tends to zero as $\theta\to\infty$. This family satisfies the paper's linear cohort equations; economic no-arbitrage, feasible orders, impact calibration and actual portfolio exposures require separate justification.

A second family additionally has symmetric positive-definite impact. For $\theta\ge1$, let
\[
K_\theta=\begin{bmatrix}1&\theta/2\\\theta/2&\theta^2\end{bmatrix},\qquad
Q_\theta=\mathrm{diag}(1/5,1/(5\theta^2)),\qquad a_i=1/2,\quad\eta_i=1/5.
\]
The determinant of $K_\theta$ is $3\theta^2/4>0$. In asset-ordered coordinates, the full Jacobian satisfies $J_\theta=S_\theta J_1S_\theta^{-1}$ with $S_\theta=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,\theta,\theta)$. Its largest spectral radius is unchanged and below one, because the two feedback modes have gains $0.3$ and $0.1$, both below $1-a=0.5$. The downstream one-step response to leader memory is $\theta/10$, so its exact fixed-coordinate radius again collapses as $\theta$ grows. Similarity establishes the spectrum here; physical coordinates, initial-state ball and output remain fixed across the different parameterized economies. Symmetry/positive definiteness alone does not establish a no-manipulation equilibrium or real impact/exposure calibration.

For numerical certification, choose $h$ such that $q_h=\|J^h\|_2<1$. For $t=N+j+mh$, $0\le j<h$, submultiplicativity gives $\|CJ^t\|_2\le\|CJ^{N+j}\|_2q_h^m$. Therefore the infinite tail is bounded by $\max_{j<h}\|CJ^{N+j}\|_2$. If this bound is below the finite peak through $N-1$, that peak is certified. The examples use this tail check, rather than silently treating a finite horizon as infinite. For the constructed rational matrices, a separate exact-arithmetic check bounds $\|J^h\|_2$ by its Frobenius norm, verifies that squared bound is below one, and compares exact rational squared row norms for the prefix and tail. The hedged family is independently checked through its closed-form row expression. Empirical floating-point matrices need separate roundoff and parameter-uncertainty bounds.

\begin{proposition}[A common-metric loss bound with changing coefficients]
\label{prop:robust_metric_loss}
Let $P\succ0$, $\|z\|_P=\|P^{1/2}z\|_2$, and
\[
z_t=J_tz_{t-1}+D_tw_t+e_t(z_{t-1}),\qquad y_t=C_tz_t.
\]
Suppose, throughout a region $\|z\|_P\le R$, that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|P^{1/2}J_tP^{-1/2}\|_2\le q_J,&\quad \|P^{1/2}D_t\|_2\le d,\\
\|C_tP^{-1/2}\|_2\le c,&\quad \|e_t(z)\|_P\le e_0+\ell\|z\|_P.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let $q_J,d,c,\ell,e_0,r,r_0\ge0$, $q=q_J+\ell<1$, $\|w_t\|_2\le r$, $\|z_0\|_P\le r_0$, and $R\ge\max\{r_0,(dr+e_0)/(1-q)\}$. Then the region is invariant and
\[
\|y_t\|_2\le c\left(q^t r_0+\frac{(dr+e_0)(1-q^t)}{1-q}\right)
\le c\max\left\{r_0,\frac{dr+e_0}{1-q}\right\}.
\]
This is a sufficient uniform budget bound, not an exact optimal radius.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The declared operator and nonlinear bounds imply $\|z_t\|_P\le q\|z_{t-1}\|_P+dr+e_0$. The region condition gives $qR+dr+e_0\le R$, establishing invariance by induction. Iterating the scalar inequality gives the geometric expression. Multiplication by the output bound $c$ yields the result. The last expression is a convex combination of $r_0$ and $(dr+e_0)/(1-q)$.
\end{proof}
For a nominal stable $\widehat J$, solving $\widehat J^\top P\widehat J-P=-I$ supplies one common metric. If
$\|P^{1/2}(J_t-\widehat J)P^{-1/2}\|_2\le\varepsilon$, then $q_J\le\|P^{1/2}\widehat JP^{-1/2}\|_2+\varepsilon$. The offset $e_0$ permits a bounded affine/model residual. The uncertainty set, nonlinear remainder, offset and exposures must actually satisfy these bounds; a fitted stable matrix does not establish them. Quadratic Lyapunov certificates are established control tools \citep{boyd1994lmi}.

Individual spectral stability cannot replace this common condition. The matrices $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}.5&2\\0&.5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ and $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}.5&0\\2&.5\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ each have radius $.5$, while their alternating two-step product has radius above four. Coefficient switching can thus create growth even when every separately fitted matrix is stable. A weighted initial ball is also distinct from the earlier Euclidean ball: $\|z_0\|_P\le\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(P)}\|z_0\|_2$. Coordinate changes require corresponding transformations of the metric, operators and uncertainty set.

\paragraph{Finite horizons without contraction.}
For a finite horizon, the same recursion admits any $q=q_J+\ell\ge0$:
\[
a_t=q^t r_0+(dr+e_0)\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}q^k,\qquad
R_H\ge\max_{0\le t\le H}a_t.
\]
A region satisfying this condition is invariant through $H$ by the same induction, and $\|y_t\|_2\le c a_t$ there. At $q=1$, $a_t=r_0+t(dr+e_0)$. Thus a declared finite-horizon contract can include a fundamental-price coordinate with a unit-root component; it supplies no indefinite contraction guarantee. Budget and nonlinear mark conditions must use this finite $R_H$.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-horizon Gaussian input contract]
\label{prop:finite_gaussian_risk}
For a declared integer horizon $H\ge1$, integer input dimension $m\ge1$, $\sigma>0$, $0<\alpha<1$, and innovations with marginal law $w_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I_m)$, define
\[
r_H=\sigma\sqrt{\chi^2_{m,1-\alpha/H}}.
\]
With probability at least $1-\alpha$, all $H$ input norms are at most $r_H$. If either the contracting or finite-horizon recursion's region and budget conditions hold with $r=r_H$, its bound holds simultaneously through $H$ on that event. Temporal independence is unnecessary for this union bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each $\|w_t\|_2^2/\sigma^2$ has a chi-square law with $m$ degrees of freedom, so its tail beyond $r_H$ is $\alpha/H$. The union bound over $H$ steps gives failure probability at most $\alpha$. The deterministic recursion bound applies on the complementary event.
\end{proof}
The horizon and noise law are parts of the contract. If the required model/parameter bounds hold only on an event with failure probability at most $\beta$, a separate union bound gives combined coverage at least $1-\alpha-\beta$; the data must justify that additional coverage. Gaussian innovations do not satisfy an indefinitely bounded-input premise. Estimated noise scales, non-Gaussian tails, missed disturbances and model rejection require their own uncertainty analysis.

\paragraph{A finite-second-moment alternative.}
If the input law is not Gaussian but has justified bounds $E\|w_t\|_2^2\le V_t$, the choice
\[
r_H=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{t=1}^H V_t}{\alpha}}
\]
also gives $P(\max_{t\le H}\|w_t\|_2>r_H)\le\alpha$. A zero total moment bound implies zero inputs almost surely; otherwise the result follows by Markov's inequality at each step and the union bound. This needs no temporal independence or distributional shape assumption, but can be much more conservative. The population moment bounds and any uncertainty in their estimation remain part of the contract; a sample variance alone does not establish them. Under only marginal moment bounds, disjoint rare-shock events can approach the bound, so a uniformly tighter input radius needs additional assumptions.

\begin{proposition}[Dollar mark mapping with a nonlinear remainder]
\label{prop:mark_to_dollar}
Let fixed positions $h_i$ hold instruments whose reference prices $P_i>0$ are denominated in the same budget currency. Let $b_i$ be actual log-price changes in basis points, with $|b_i|\le\Delta_i$. The marked profit change is
\[
\Pi(b)=\sum_i h_iP_i(e^{b_i/10000}-1),\qquad
\Pi_{\rm lin}(b)=\sum_i\frac{h_iP_i}{10000}b_i.
\]
Then
\[
|\Pi-\Pi_{\rm lin}|\le
\frac12\sum_i |h_i|P_i e^{\Delta_i/10000}(\Delta_i/10000)^2.
\]
If $b=Tz$, the dollar output row is $C=(h_iP_i/10000)_iT$. A linear budget plus this remainder and any separately bounded cash/execution costs must fit within the allocated capital budget.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taylor's theorem gives $|e^v-1-v|\le e^{|v|}v^2/2$. Apply this componentwise and use the triangle inequality. Substitution of $b=Tz$ gives the stated dollar operator. Under $\|z\|_P\le R$, valid component bounds are $\Delta_i=R\|T_iP^{-1/2}\|_2$.
\end{proof}
Actual log-price displacement is distinct from latent pricing error relative to fundamentals. A stable error process can coexist with a moving fundamental price. If a price exactly tracks a nondegenerate Gaussian log-fundamental innovation $\xi$, then its pricing error stays zero, but a fixed short position $h<0$ has marked loss $|h|P_0(e^\xi-1)$. For every finite budget $B>0$, the event $\xi>\log(1+B/(|h|P_0))$ has positive probability. Thus a deterministic mark-loss guarantee does not follow from zero pricing error. A finite sequence of doubling prices illustrates this omitted channel. Thus a loss operator on pricing error alone does not bound all mark risk. Fundamental-price and currency channels, changing positions, cash flows and margin rules must be included or separately bounded. The present proposition supplies a conditional unit/remainder mapping, rather than an authenticated institutional capital or execution guarantee.

\section{Repaired Markov-Switching Likelihood and Out-of-Sample Folds}
\label{sec:regime_repaired}
The legacy Markov-switching implementation (\texttt{markov\_switching\_var.py}) suffered from several structural defects: it approximated state transition posteriors by the product of marginal smoothed probabilities, imposed hard parameter persistence bounds, floored Gaussian densities at $10^{-15}$, lacked terminal convergence certification, and formed an uncalibrated dimensional ``stability'' index by adding persistence to ten times a dimensional slope. Furthermore, its extreme $p$-value ($p < 10^{-300}$) was unsupported by the generator.

The repaired implementation (\texttt{switching\_regression.py}) implements exact forward--backward log-domain filtering with full-sequence posterior pair transitions conditional on the entire observation vector, free fitted initial probabilities, explicit terminal likelihood matching, and a declared variance floor. Transition estimates reset at gap boundaries.

We evaluate two specifications against a single-regime benchmark:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Variance-Only Switching}: Shared mean regression coefficients across regimes with two-state volatility switching ($\sigma_1^2 \ne \sigma_2^2$).
    \item \textbf{Full Coefficient and Variance Switching}: Both regression coefficients $(\beta_1, \beta_2)$ and residual variances switch across regimes.
\end{enumerate}
Evaluations use chronological expanding-window out-of-sample folds across seven evaluation dates, measuring relative Mean Squared Error (MSE) improvement and negative log-predictive score difference relative to the single-regime model.
