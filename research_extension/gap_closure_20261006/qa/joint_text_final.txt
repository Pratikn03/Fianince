  From Human Markets to Machine Markets:
 Feedback Stability, Auditable Memory, and Financial Constraints

                   Pratik Niroula∗ and               Prashant Niroula†

                                            Abstract

      Aggregate price–flow data reveal model-dependent feedback diagnostics rather than
      trader identities. In a reset linear cohort model, symmetric near-coincident rates re-
      quire bounded-power probing budgets of order δ −4 ; a separate scalar Gaussian exper-
      iment yields a finite-window abstention layer. Reanalysis of recorded Coinbase data
      shows substantial sensitivity to flow timing and price filtering, while covariance re-
      strictions are rejected. Saved Binance fee contrasts do not establish a causal response
      or equivalence. Finally, a declared loss operator and shock set yield an exact budget
      radius; fixed-spectrum and funded-dealer simulations distinguish transient magnifica-
      tion from persistent-flow accumulation. Independent replication and policy validation
      remain open.


Keywords: Adaptive learning, algorithmic trading, financial stability, market microstruc-
ture, system identification, non-normal dynamics, Kreiss constant.

1     Introduction
Electronic trading creates a feedback channel when price changes affect subsequent orders
and those orders affect later prices. The existence of this channel does not identify its
participant-level source: aggregate flow can reflect adaptive algorithms, order splitting, liq-
uidity provision, or common information. Nor does decay of price disturbances establish
that a dealer’s loss budget will remain intact. This paper separates these identification and
financial-risk questions.
    Our central question is: what can observable aggregate data establish about feedback, and
what additional assumptions are required to turn dynamic stability into a financially mean-
ingful loss bound? We combine a linear adaptive-memory model with an audit of observable-
state definitions and timing, followed by controlled loss-exposure illustrations. The empirical
analysis is deliberately reduced-form. It does not identify artificial-intelligence trading, in-
dividual learning rules, or a causal market-maker response from anonymous records.

Conditional identification results. Within a reset linear cohort model with known
price-impact and anchor parameters, price responses identify merged rate–weight measures
rather than the number or identity of cohorts sharing a rate. For a uniformly stable compact
family of symmetric split alternatives, bounded-power deterministic probes with Gaussian
observation noise face a separation lower bound of order δ −4 . This is a lower bound for
the stated experiment, not an attained universal rate for all passive market data. A sepa-
rate scalar Gaussian price-surveillance experiment gives an explicit finite-window abstention
bound near its unit-root boundary. The observation experiment matters: the scalar result
is not asserted for noiselessly observed latent cohort states.

Measurement sensitivity. The primary empirical reanalysis uses recorded Coinbase
BTC, ETH, and ADA book snapshots and signed executed flow from April 7–19, 2021. A
300-second EMA price deviation is estimated on 10-second bins with only end-bin observed
flow. Mean reduced-form feedback is approximately 0.00194, 0.00098, and 0.00062, respec-
tively. Forward-bin flow alignment produces larger estimates, while raw-return specifications
have feedback intervals spanning zero. These comparisons establish that measurement and
timing materially affect the feedback diagnostic. They do not establish an algorithmic
learning mechanism.

timates, derived from trade-price klines rather than historical full order books. Its legacy
forward-flow estimator has mean Q̂ ≃ 0.00815. The raw Binance archives are not present in
the inspected cache, so this revision verifies saved-matrix identities and recomputes inference

1.1    Related Literature and Contribution Boundaries
Classical microstructure studies price formation, information and order flow (Kyle, 1985;
Glosten and Milgrom, 1985; Hasbrouck, 1991). Order splitting and book imbalance can
create persistent relations without identifying an adaptive trader (Bouchaud et al., 2004;
Cont, Kukanov, and Stoikov, 2014). Funding constraints and inventory exposure connect
market liquidity to intermediary fragility (Brunnermeier and Pedersen, 2009; Adrian and
Shin, 2010; Greenwood, Landier, and Thesmar, 2015).
    The realization and transient-growth tools used here build on existing control and nu-
merical analysis (Gilbert, 1963; Kreiss, 1962; Spijker, 1991; Trefethen and Embree, 2005).
Non-normal financial-network amplification is not a first-use claim: Wang, Sornette, Wu
and Lera’s 2024 working paper studies evolving influence networks and subcritical financial
instabilities (Wang et al., 2024). Singular mixture models also have assumption-dependent
identification rates (Manole and Ho, 2022); our symmetric probing experiment is a specific
dynamical testing problem rather than a universal new estimation rate. Our scope differs
by specifying an output loss budget, a cohort-response observation experiment, and sensi-
tivity of measured price–flow feedback to timing and filtering. We do not claim novelty for
the general distinction between asymptotic decay and transient growth, or an independently
certified priority for the full framework.

2     Theoretical Model and Identification Boundaries
2.1    Microfoundations of Closed-Loop Price Formation
We formalize an electronic financial market sampled at discrete uniform time intervals
t ∈ {0, 1, 2, . . . }, where time step ∆ represents the operational sampling frequency (e.g.,
1 second). Let pt denote the log mid-price of a traded asset, and let p∗t denote the unob-
served fundamental value, which evolves as a martingale:

                          p∗t+1 = p∗t + ξt+1 ,       ξt+1 ∼ i.i.d.(0, σξ2 ).                    (1)

We define the pricing error (displacement from fundamental) as xt ≡ pt − p∗t .

Dealer Pricing and Kyle Auction Microfoundation. Following the continuous auc-
tion framework of Kyle (1985) adapted to discrete quote adjustments (Hasbrouck, 1991;
Bouchaud et al., 2004), a competitive, risk-neutral market maker sets quotes to absorb ag-
gregate order flow. Total incoming order flow at time t consists of two components: aggregate
demand ut submitted by algorithmic trading agents, and exogenous liquidity/hedging noise
εt+1 ∼ i.i.d.(0, σε2 ). In addition, fundamentalist arbitrageurs submit mean-reverting demand
−φxt whenever prices deviate from fundamentals.
    The market maker updates the transaction price according to linear price impact κ > 0:

                               pt+1 − pt = κ (ut − φxt + εt+1 ) .                               (2)

Subtracting fundamental increment ξt+1 from both sides yields the pricing error recursion:

                          xt+1 = (1 − κφ)xt + κut + (κεt+1 − ξt+1 ) .                           (3)

Defining the direct price persistence parameter a ≡ 1−κφ ∈ [0, 1) and rescaling the combined

disturbance innovation as wt+1 ≡ κεt+1 − ξt+1 , we obtain the foundational pricing equation:

                        xt+1 = axt + κut + wt+1 ,        wt+1 ∼ i.i.d.(0, σw2 ).                    (4)

When fundamentalist arbitrage is strong (φ → 1/κ), a → 0 and prices immediately reflect
fundamentals. When liquidity is thin and arbitrage capital is limited, a approaches unity,
reflecting high price persistence.

Algorithmic Learning Microfoundations. We consider K heterogeneous cohorts of
algorithmic traders. Each cohort i ∈ {1, . . . , K} maintains an internal state variable yi,t ∈ R
representing its estimate of price momentum, trend, or fundamental mispricing. We show
that two stylized signal-update paradigms—steady-state Kalman filtering and regularized
Online Gradient Descent—both reduce locally to the identical linear adaptive recursion.

Remark 2.4 (Scope of the Separation Bound). Merged rates identify aggregate weights, not
the count or identities of participants sharing a rate in the reset linear experiment. The
symmetric split construction makes the leading response difference quadratic in δ, giving a
fourth-order testing lower bound with fixed stability margin and noise/power assumptions.
This does not identify dollar allocations, supply an upper bound, or justify a literal ratio of
detection horizons between δ = 0.05 and a unit perturbation outside the local family.

6     Attribution, Falsification, and Replication Limits
6.1    Aggregate Relations Do Not Identify Algorithms
The merged-cohort theorem concerns a stated linear, reset observation experiment. Anony-
mous prices and flows cannot identify identities within a merged representation. More
broadly, the empirical VAR here does not distinguish a learning algorithm from a persis-
tent exogenous flow process or discretionary response to common news. Participant iden-
tifiers improve disaggregation, but parameter recovery also requires a correct state model,
sufficient regressor rank, and an orthogonality assumption; OLS alone is not an automatic
causal identification guarantee.


6.2    What the Diagnostics Establish
Timing comparisons show that the legacy and end-bin states differ. Raw-return checks
show that filtered persistence is not interchangeable with return persistence. No-adaptation
controls test a finite declared family, not every confounder. Covariance rejection remains
unfavorable evidence about the low-dimensional approximation. Sign-memory adjustment is
a descriptive robustness exercise, not proof of causal learning. The revision makes no blanket
assertion that all placebo tests have passed or that common response proves propagation.

7.3    Conclusion
Observable price–flow data support model-dependent diagnostics, while participant attri-

the stated linear probing experiment, merged cohorts are observationally equivalent and
symmetric close-rate alternatives have a fourth-order separation lower bound. The scalar

The loss-budget identity shows why stable eigenvalues alone do not bound worst-case output

from observing a variable called “internal state.” Its moment restrictions need independent
justification. Similarly, a Kalman recursion is an MMSE statement only under the stated

C      Measurement and Inference Methods
C.1     The observable transformation
The primary transformation forms past-observed bins, uses the last recorded midpoint in
each bin and sums deduplicated executed signed flow. A missing bin splits the sequence;
it is not filled from future values. The price state is log midpoint minus its exponential
reference with α = 2/(W/∆ + 1). Five reference horizons are excluded after a segment reset.
This changes both the estimand and its sample count. For raw-return diagnostics, differences
are formed within the same contiguous segment. Daily aggregation treats multiple segments
on one date as parts of one date, rather than independent observations.
    The observable proxy is not latent fundamental mispricing. Even a martingale filtered
through an EMA produces memory. If a statement concerns fundamental stability, an
observation-law relation is needed. Our reduced-form fit includes an intercept, and the
off-diagonal ratio defining Q is a diagnostic of that fit. Dividing by 1 − J22 can amplify
uncertainty when flow persistence is close to one; the invariants must be read alongside the
full fitted radius, sampling clock and denominator.

against sums of one-second records under three interval closures. The deduplicated past
interval reproduces every one of the tested 1,995 windows for each asset. The future interval

Means and confidence intervals based on a daily panel describe variation across observed

displayed interval. For the saved Binance panel, all assets on a date are kept together in

The reanalysis matches eleven covariance/lag moments to seven parameters and checks local

definite. The diagnostic uses HAC weighting with ten bins and a four-degree-of-freedom
asymptotic reference. A successful optimizer supplies a candidate moment fit; rejection
assesses the maintained moment model. It cannot establish structural validity through a
nearby reduced-form point estimate.

I     Remaining Gates and the User-Requested Deferrals
The active local work is full claim-ledger closure, residual/model diagnostics, existing pol-
icy/manifest verification, and reconciliation of the complete source and evidence. Raw panel
and synchronized cross-venue recovery remain necessary before their empirical gates close.
Provider authentication, observational causal identification and independent mathemati-
cal/econometric review cannot be replaced by a compilation or test pass.
    New large datasets, new complex learning-agent experiments and extra market modules
are deferred until existing evidence is reliable. The currently located datasets and exist-
ing experiment outputs remain in the recovery/audit scope. T9 metadata and small-result
checks were reopened when the volume became readable; original raw panel recovery remains
unresolved. Historical TAQ estimation reopens when an authenticated existing source is re-
covered; illustrative simulation parameters do not fill that gap. External replication and
journal submission follow a concrete reproducible package, not an agent declaration of com-
pletion.
    A rejection, null effect or invalid interpretation is already a result and must be reported
now. These unfavorable findings are not deferred. The full revision retains their scientific
questions while correcting their evidentiary status.
