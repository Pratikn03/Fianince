\section{The value of disclosure for safe trading}\label{sec:disclosure}

The companion paper measures the value of disclosure in samples needed to identify the cohorts \citep{niroula2026}. For a pre-trade control, the relevant value is the safe trading capacity it unlocks.

\begin{theorem}[Decision value of disclosure]\label{thm:disclosure}
Under the risk contract of Section~\ref{sec:contracts}, compare an observation contract $O$ with a richer contract $O'$ that reveals everything $O$ reveals and more, both with exact data.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Monotonicity.} The compatible sets are nested, $\Theta_{O'}\subseteq\Theta_O$, so $\Phi^+_{O'}\le\Phi^+_O$ and the admitted level of Theorem~\ref{thm:robustguard} satisfies $\alpha^*_{O'}\ge\alpha^*_O$.
\item \emph{Strictness.} $\alpha^*_{O'}>\alpha^*_O$ if and only if $\alpha^*_O<1$, $c_I>0$, and $O'$ excludes every compatible economy with $\Phi=\Phi^+_O$. Under the window contract that economy is the upper principal representation of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}: disclosure has decision value exactly when it rules out a cohort at the memory bound with the extremal weight.
\item \emph{Long memory.} Under the window contract with memory bound $\bar r$, $\alpha^*_O\to0$ as $\bar r\to1$ for any fixed window (Theorem~\ref{thm:window}(iii)), whereas audited exposure, which reveals $\Phi$, admits the oracle capacity $\alpha^\circ$. The decision value of audited disclosure therefore tends to the whole oracle capacity as memory lengthens.
\item \emph{Net value.} With value $w$ per unit of admitted capacity and disclosure cost $c(O')$, the net value $w(\alpha^*_{O'}-\alpha^*_O)-c(O')$ is zero or negative whenever $O$ already identifies $\Phi$ (a window whose moment vector lies on the boundary of the moment space) or the budget does not bind.
\item \emph{Noisy proxies.} With noisy data the certificates of two contracts are confidence bounds, not nested sets, and a richer contract can admit less. Under class-tagged flows measured with noise over $T$ periods and estimated as in the companion paper, class $i$'s exposure $\hat\Phi_i=\hat w_i/(1-\hat r_i)$ has delta-method standard error
\[
{\rm se}(\hat\Phi_i)=\frac{1}{(1-r_i)\sqrt T}\Bigl[V_{ww}+2\Phi_iV_{wr}+\Phi_i^2V_{rr}\Bigr]^{1/2},
\]
with $V$ the asymptotic covariance of $(\hat w_i,\hat r_i)$, so the tagged bound $\sum_i\hat\Phi_i+z_{1-\gamma}{\rm se}$ admits less than the public certificate whenever its margin exceeds $\Phi^+_O-\Phi$. Intersecting the two certificates, each at level $1-\gamma/2$, gives a bound valid at level $1-\gamma$ that admits at least as much as either certificate at level $1-\gamma/2$, which restores monotonicity.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The theorem is set inclusion plus the closed form of Theorem~\ref{thm:robustguard}; its content is the location of the value. Disclosure is worth most where public certificates are weakest, in markets whose participants have long memories. In simulation (test T07; Table~\ref{tab:discsim}), with two classes and one of them slow, audited exposure adds 0.027 of admitted capacity to a public certificate when the slow class's memory rate is 0.6 and 0.122 when it is 0.995, out of an oracle capacity of 0.667. Class-tagged flows measured with 10\% noise add 0.02--0.03 at memory rates up to 0.97 but \emph{lower} capacity at 0.995, because a class's exposure $q=w/(1-r)$ is badly conditioned when $r$ is near one: disclosure of a noisy proxy can be worth less than nothing. Part (v) quantifies this. In a second simulation of the same design (test T15), the standard error of the slow class's exposure rises from $0.0004$ to $0.035$ as $1-r$ falls from $0.4$ to $0.005$, roughly in proportion to $(1-r)^{-1}$ over the last decade, while the public certificate's margin grows only with the square root of its horizon. At $r=0.995$ the tagged bound admits $0.506$ against $0.549$ for the public one; the intersection admits $0.537$, close to the better of the two, and covers the true exposure in 99\% of replications (Table~\ref{tab:discsim}). The companion paper's evidence that Binance's and the CFTC's class positions pin down memories that prices leave unrestricted concerns class-level positions, which are proxies, not records of individual algorithms.

\paragraph{Pricing versus guarding.} A conservative bound is the right input for a guard and the wrong input for a price. The corrective rebate of Proposition~\ref{prop:equil} depends on $S^2$; computed from an upper bound that overstates $S$ by 10\% it over-subsidizes reversion and loses 2.4--2.8\% of first-best welfare, and by 50\%, 70--80\% (test T12). Guards should use certified upper bounds; rebates should use point estimates.
