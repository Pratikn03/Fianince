\begin{abstract}
\noindent Algorithmic traders react to prices, and prices react to them. In a linear market with feedback traders, the \emph{feedback kernel}, the response of total feedback flow to past prices, is a sufficient statistic. For any insider and any causal dealer rule, populations with the same kernel produce the same prices, profits and equilibria, and prices identify nothing more. Learning group composition costs the known mixture rate, which survives the feedback loop and holds in an inventory equilibrium. Position limits add a second layer: a capacity-weighted measure over memory and leverage. Calm-market prices reveal only the kernel. Under hard limits they carry no information about how close traders are to their limits; under smooth limits, information grows like the sixth power of the probe. Stress that reaches the limits reveals the measure exactly. The linear result parallels known aggregation results; our contribution is its strategic, closed-loop and supervisory form. Core statements are machine-checked in Lean~4.
\end{abstract}
