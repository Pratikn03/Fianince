\begin{abstract}
\noindent Algorithmic traders react to prices, and prices react to them. In a linear market with feedback traders, one object, the \emph{feedback kernel} (the response of total feedback flow to past prices), is a sufficient statistic for everything that price-based trading determines. For any insider strategy and any causal dealer rule, two trader populations with the same kernel produce the same prices, order flow, insider profits and dealer inventories, and therefore the same equilibria. The kernel is also all that prices identify. Groups with equal memories cannot be separated with any amount of data. Separating $K$ groups whose memories lie $\Delta\eta$ apart takes of order $(\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)}$ experiments. That is the known rate for finite mixtures, and it survives the feedback loop because the memories enter as zeros of the price response; for the same reason, watching the market is no cheaper than trading to learn. In an equilibrium with inventory-averse dealers, the price is the same closed loop with effective parameters and unchanged memories, so both results hold in equilibrium, also with a strategic insider whose information is short-lived. Composition therefore matters only for actions aimed at a particular group: two markets almost identical in their prices can call for different halts. Flow reported by trader class removes the cost of learning it. The idea of a sufficient statistic for aggregate dynamics is not new. It parallels intertemporal marginal propensities to consume in macroeconomics and the aggregation of autoregressions in time series. Our contribution is its strategic, closed-loop and supervisory form. The core statements are machine-checked in Lean~4; the supplement lists what is proved only on paper.
\end{abstract}
