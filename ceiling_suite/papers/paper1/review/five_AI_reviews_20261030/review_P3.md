# P3 — AI-assisted internal review: financial econometrics, empirical inference and reproducibility
**Basis:** attached 124-page PDF, not a fresh-model reviewer. Scores are for pre-SSRN exposure. Printed manuscript page numbers are used (PDF +3 from Introduction onward). **Source verification limits:** I inspected the manuscript and confirmed its GitHub repository is publicly reachable, but I did **not** rerun its CFTC/Binance pipelines, download the full underlying panel, inspect its complete commit history, or reproduce a result table from the repository. Thus its empirical coefficients and numerical CIs remain **UNVERIFIED**, even where the paper itself reports a result as 'registered' or 'recomputed'.

## A. Verdict
**Hold the version until the empirical framing is rewritten.** The strongest empirical result—that an observed position series yields a tighter fitted autoregressive persistence interval than a short-window return-kernel inversion—is unsurprising and does not test the model's distinctive structural restrictions. It is important that the authors disclose their forecasts failed and that their registered merged-response test failed, but the abstract still treats the 8/8 and 29/29 counts as a central corroboration. The disclosed design is legitimate as an exploratory comparison of observation sets; it should not be marketed as validation of the quantitative information barriers without a fair horizon-matched, weak-ID-robust comparator.

## B. SSRN readiness
**5/10 now; 7.5/10 after honest separation of descriptive diagnostics, structural tests, and exploratory analyses; 8/10 only if a reader can actually run the archival replication without private context.**

## C. Issues
### FATAL
No empirical fabrication or fabricated inference was VERIFIED. **FATAL to the present evidence interpretation** if the authors present 'registered' as externally pre-registered, or treat price-only confidence ranges as evidence that the model's *quantitative* minimax exponent has been observed in real markets. The paper currently supplies partial safeguards; preserve and sharpen them.

### MAJOR
1. **Non-equivalent estimands in the main CFTC comparison — printed pp. 52–53, §6.3. VERIFIED by specifications; numerical outcomes unverified.** Quote: “`n_c,t = (long − short)/open interest` follows `n_c,t = α_c + r_c n_c,t−1 + w_c ret_t`”; and “The price-only alternative fits a two-exponential kernel to twelve weekly return lags.” The tagged AR estimates *persistence of observed class positioning*; the price-only fit estimates two latent coefficients from returns. Different regressors, variables and model restrictions mean interval-width comparison is not a controlled comparison of the same estimand. **Exact fix:** write: “The comparison is descriptive and not an equal-information estimator horse race; a position autoregression's persistence need not equal the kernels inferred from prices.” Add a horizon-matched price-plus-flow benchmark on the existing data if feasible; no new market data are needed.

2. **Narrow 13-week bootstrap uncertainty cannot by itself establish uniform validity near unit roots — printed pp. 52–53, §6.3. SUSPECT.** Quote: “Intervals come from a 13-week moving-block bootstrap.” Table 13 includes slow estimates `0.999` and `0.996`, close to the imposed price-only upper bound `0.99` used elsewhere; finite block length 13 may miss long serial dependence and biased near-unity sampling. **Fix:** distinguish pointwise percentile CI from weak-ID-robust confidence sets; report sensitivity to 26/52-week blocks and effective sample. This is a reanalysis of existing data, not automatically a new experiment.

3. **One of H3's 'successes' is not a clear test for market-specific differences — printed p. 53, Table 13 and §6.3. VERIFIED from reported CIs, not independently recomputed.** Quote: “H3 6 of 8, pass” versus “in Treasuries and natural gas the two persistences are indistinguishable.” The authors do report this distinction. **Fix:** say “6/8 have the prespecified sign, but two are statistically indistinguishable; this is not 8/8 evidence of two different class memories.”

4. **'Fast class' chosen by exogenous role labels, not necessarily fastest observed — printed p. 53, Table 13 note. VERIFIED.** Quote: “Fast class: leveraged funds (financial futures) or managed money (commodities); slow class: asset managers or producer/merchants.” In natural gas, the reported 'fast' estimate (0.981) exceeds the slow estimate (0.980). **Fix:** rename `fast` and `slow` as *designated speculative* and *designated comparison* classes, with the faster-memory H3 prediction left as an empirical hypothesis. Do not hard-code the result into the label.

5. **Negative coefficients break the positive-cohort theory — printed p. 54, §6.3. VERIFIED in reported result and authors' caveat.** Quote: “Negative estimated `w_c` also lies outside the positive economy of Section 2, so the dollar bounds of Section 6.2 do not apply to these classes.” This is an appropriate but too-low-visibility limitation. **Fix:** add to abstract: “Some class responses have the opposite sign from our positive-feedback model.” Clearly separate the model's statistical signed-weight extension from its positive-impact financial losses.

6. **CFTC data are positions, not trade-level tagged flow — printed pp. 52–54, §6.3. VERIFIED via CFTC official documentation.** Quote: “The CFTC's class reports restrict persistence rates that prices leave unrestricted.” CFTC collects trader-level positions under reporting rules but publishes aggregated class positions weekly based on a Tuesday snapshot, and classification reflects predominant reported business purpose—not the algorithm that traded. **Fix:** call these “published aggregated class-position disclosures”; do not imply equivalence to Proposition 6.1's class-by-class *transaction flow*. CFTC source: https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm ; https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/MarketSurveillance/LargeTraderReportingProgram/index.htm .

7. **Original Binance benchmark has a known truncated-lag disadvantage — printed pp. 55–56, §6.4. VERIFIED by text.** Quote: “the price-only kernel spans one hour (twelve five-minute lags), while the tagged half-lives are several hours.” This is not merely a footnote; it can systematically manufacture 'unrestricted' price-only bands. The exploratory 96-lag comparison reduces the median interval-width factor to 7.5; the manuscript says the more credible 96-lag comparison passed 29/29 fresh markets. **Fix:** lead with the 96-lag, horizon-matched 29/29 result; demote the 12-lag 30/30 benchmark and 28–124× claims to exploratory motivation. Match maximum allowable persistence, parameterization, horizons and observation noise wherever possible.

8. **Near-unit-root invalidates the naïve 'learning memory' interpretation — printed pp. 55–57, §6.4. VERIFIED (paper's own admission).** Quote from abstract: “the Binance persistence is close to a random walk once corrected for a within-day bias.” A 5-minute `r ≈ 0.99` with drifting class membership may reflect slow inventory turnover/aggregation, not latent adaptive belief speed. **Fix:** label all empirical r as position-persistence coefficients in every figure and table. Put the bias-corrected coefficient and horizon-matched comparator in the main findings summary, not just the latter part of §6.4.

9. **Failure of the theory-facing merged-response test is material — printed pp. 4, 55–57, §§1/6.4. VERIFIED as reported.** Quote: “the merged response was precisely estimated in only 5 of 30 markets” and “This is a failure of the prediction as registered.” The result is honorably present, but is buried under apparent 30/30 wins. **Fix:** add a small boxed *not a promotional box* table in the introduction: observable restriction passes, structural merged-response criterion fails 5/30, forecast usefulness fails. Report the three-parts-required rule up front.

10. **Out-of-sample return result is negative; frame it as such — printed pp. 57–59, §6.4. VERIFIED from printed estimates, not from re-run.** Quote: “the tagged-minus-price-only difference is `∆R² = −1.089 points` (95% date-block bootstrap CI `[−2.605,+0.376]`, Clark–West `t = 0.96`, `p=0.1684`).” The CIs do not establish a statistically reliable degradation, but give no evidence of forecasting gain; the raw point difference is adverse. **Exact fix:** “Predeclared class-based memory features did not improve the 30-minute return forecast; the estimated gain relative to price-derived features was −1.09 percentage points and statistically inconclusive.” Place this in the abstract and contribution table on equal footing with wins.

11. **Follow-up state forecasts are not independent economic validation — printed p. 58, Table 15 and §6.4. VERIFIED by authors' own decomposition.** Quote: “An AR(1) on the current level alone accounts for 100.5% and 99.1% of the improvement.” This is mostly the trivial forecasting advantage of observing a highly persistent *target state*, which the price-only model does not observe. **Fix:** collapse Table 15 to an appendix and characterize it as an information-set sanity check, not as new predictive evidence for trading dynamics.

12. **Registration claim is a provenance claim, not external pre-registration — front matter p. iii. VERIFIED in wording, registration chronology NOT VERIFIED.** Quote: “because each specification reached the public repository in the same push as its results, that order is documented by the commit history rather than by an independent timestamp.” A public commit containing **both** specification and result cannot itself independently certify that the specification predated observation of the data. **Exact fix:** replace generic “registered” in the abstract with “author-declared, code-committed specifications”; include hashes, parent commits, immutable publication timestamps, and a plain statement: **not independently preregistered**. Without independent audit of pre-download logs do not assert proven chronology.

13. **Reproducibility is asserted much more strongly than was validated here — front matter pp. ii–iii and printed pp. 62, 117–121. SUSPECT.** Quote: “Every number in the paper is produced by a script or notebook cell that writes a result file.” The GitHub link `https://github.com/Pratikn03/Fianince` **resolves publicly**, but the paper has numerous notebook 'Parts', names and output identifiers, and data access relies on external public providers. The manuscript description alone does not allow an outsider to reproduce Table 13 without choosing report variants, time alignment, contract rolls and exact downloads. **Fix:** create `replication/P1_README.md` listing one command per table, commit SHA, environment lock, checksums and input licensing, with one explicit 'clean machine' run of Table 13. Full execution remains pending: do not claim it has been reproduced by this review.

14. **Overlapping panel dates / sample selection need a registry — printed pp. 55–58, §6.4. SUSPECT.** Quote: “two follow-ups were registered before their data were downloaded, and each ran once.” There are training symbols, 30 fresh symbols, 29/29 new, overlapping 42-, 58-, and 105-day windows, plus different days-of-month out-of-sample sets. Even if each was untouched, selection across waves risks over-reading 'confirmation'. **Fix:** create a dated split table with asset universes, endpoints, frozen prereg artifacts, exclusions and all failed criteria; no new data required.

15. **Near-zero or one-sided bootstrap widths are weak-ID evidence, not correct intervals — printed p. 53, Table 13, and p. 55, Table 14. SUSPECT.** Quote: “price-only intervals span 0.95–0.96 of the admissible range `[0,0.99]`.” An optimizer hitting the bounds is also evidence of a fragile specification, especially with nonlinear two-exponential fits. **Fix:** report profile-likelihood/weak-identification robust sets and a transparent non-identification category rather than treating every `0–0.99` numerical range as a calibrated 95% CI.

### MINOR
16. **Category definitions — printed p. 52, §6.3. VERIFIED.** Quote: “managed money, producer/merchants and swap dealers in commodities.” Use the CFTC's precise 'managed money' and 'producer/merchant/processor/user' names; the paper's simplification can confuse categories.
17. **Dates and price sources — printed p. 53, Table 13. VERIFIED in description, returns not rechecked.** Quote: “Weekly returns from Binance spot (Bitcoin, Ether) and Yahoo Finance (others…).” They are *not* the exact CME futures contract prices against which CFTC classes position. **Fix:** explicitly explain basis/cross-market mismatch and perform a sensitivity test on actual front-contract CME continuous futures if already available; do not claim one has been done.
