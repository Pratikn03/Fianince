## Referee 1 (econometric theory): "Known mathematics, applied"

> The headline rate (Delta eta)^-(4K-2) is the minimax rate for mixtures with near-colliding components (Heinrich and
> Kahn 2018; Wu and Yang 2020). Pooling is multi-snapshot super-resolution (Li et al. 2022). The new cost-of-a-question
> theorem is Donoho's (1994) modulus theory. Sufficiency is aggregation (Granger 1980; Forni and Lippi 1997). The
> contribution is a translation. Reject.

Answer in the paper: "What is known" already concedes each of these. The rewritten "What is new" now lists, in one
place, the results that have no counterpart in those literatures: sufficiency path by path inside a strategic market,
for every insider and dealer rule; memory rates as transmission zeros, which is why passive data are no cheaper than
probing; the barrier not lifting near instability, where intuition says the loop amplifies differences; partial
disclosure, where the cost is set by the number of untagged memory classes, not traders; pooling, where common
memory across markets is the identifying assumption; and the polynomial-approximation form of the cost of every linear
question, which sorts supervisory questions into cheap, boundary and expensive. Risk after the rewrite: still the
likeliest rejection. A referee who weighs mathematics over setting will not be moved.

## Referee 2 (heterogeneous-agent models): "Unrealistic observer and traders"

> Linear exponential rules and reset experiments are far from markets. Supervisors cannot reset a market. The JEDC
> literature estimates agent shares from prices; this paper says that cannot work, but on a toy model.

Answer in the paper: the passive results (no probes) give the same order; the lower bounds hold over any class that
contains the linear rules, including nonlinear and band-linear ones (Theorem "Any population"); the saturating section
treats position limits; the calibration table gives years of daily data, not abstract orders; the positioning against
Brock and Hommes, Boswijk et al., Franke and Westerhoff and Lux and Zwinkels says what share estimates can and cannot
recover. The introduction now says in its second paragraph that the lower bounds do not rest on probing or on
linearity. Risk after the rewrite: moderate.
