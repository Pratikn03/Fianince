# Referee Report — Professor 1 (Probability, Minimax Estimation, and System Identification)

## A. Verdict
This working paper translates classical moment-matching and super-resolution rates for finite mixtures and completely monotone kernels into a discrete-time closed-loop linear market model, pricing the statistical sample complexity in executed probe volume and quadratic inventory/impact loss. While the core algebraic bridge—mapping cohort memory rates $r_i = 1-\eta_i$ to the transmission zeros of the closed-loop transfer function $H(z)$ and applying Gauss/Gauss-Radau quadrature and Chebyshev equioscillation to lower-bound the KL divergence—is mathematically sound and the authors are unusually forthright about what is known versus new, several theorems suffer from quantifier mismatches, silent horizon truncations, and unstated regularity conditions on noise-to-signal inversion. Specifically, Theorem 5.4 leaves the observation horizon $T$ of a "reset experiment" unspecified; Theorem 5.5 uses an asymptotic big-$O_P$ local Lipschitz argument to claim a finite-sample high-probability bound without explicitly bounding the deconvolution conditioning or the event where the empirical transfer function has zeros near the origin; and Proposition 6.1 contains an explicit sign/index error in the martingale difference representation of the IV score. A serious probability or mathematical statistics referee will not let these pass without tight finite-sample quantifiers.

## C. Detailed Issues (FATAL / MAJOR / MINOR)

### FATAL Issues

### MAJOR Issues

1. **Theorem 5.5 (Page 40–41, Section 5.4) — Asymptotic $O_P$ language inside a non-asymptotic high-probability proof and unstated truncation horizon.**
   - **Quote:** `"the map is smooth with Lipschitz constant independent of \Delta\eta, so the estimated kernel values have error O_P(\sigma N^{-1/2})... On the compact family, a local Lipschitz bound for that smooth map and Gaussian concentration for the response coordinates give, for sufficiently large N, ||\hat{t} - t||_\infty \le C_1\sqrt{\log(2K/\beta)}\epsilon_N with probability at least 1-\beta."` (p. 40–41, Proof of Theorem 5.5).
   - **Status:** VERIFIED.
   - **Why it matters:** The theorem statement claims a non-asymptotic bound valid whenever $\epsilon_N \equiv \sigma N^{-1/2}(\Delta\eta)^{-(2K-1)} \le c_0/\sqrt{\log(2K/\beta)}$. However, the deconvolution map from $(\hat{h}_0, \dots, \hat{h}_{2K})$ to $(\hat{k}_0, \dots, \hat{k}_{2K-1})$ divides recursively by $\hat{h}_0$ (or assumes $h_0=\kappa$ is noiseless/known without saying whether tap 0 is observed with noise). Furthermore, the local Lipschitz constant of the nonlinear triangular inversion only holds in a ball around the true $(h_0, \dots, h_{2K})$ where $|\hat{h}_t - h_t| \le \delta_0$. When $\Delta\eta$ is bounded away from zero (e.g., $\Delta\eta = 0.2$), $\epsilon_N \le c_0$ does *not* force $\sigma N^{-1/2}$ to be smaller than the radius of the ball where the deconvolution map is Lipschitz unless $c_0$ is explicitly scaled by $(\Delta\eta)^{2K-1}$ or bounded by $\bar{\Delta\eta}$. Also, the union bound inside the concentration step is over $2K+1$ taps, not $2K$.
   - **Exact fix:** State explicitly in Theorem 5.5 that $h_0 = \kappa$ is known (or that $\sigma N^{-1/2} \le \kappa/2$), replace `"for sufficiently large N"` with the explicit event $\max_{1\le t \le 2K} |\hat{h}_t - h_t| \le \sigma N^{-1/2}\sqrt{2\log(4K/\beta)} \le \rho_h$, and adjust the sub-Gaussian tail factor from $\log(2K/\beta)$ to $\log(4K/\beta)$.

2. **Theorem 5.4 (Page 38, Section 5.4) — Ambiguous observation horizon $T$ in a "reset experiment" and missing stability condition in the theorem header.**
   - **Quote:** `"Fix K \ge 2, a center r_c \in (0,1), total merged weight W > 0 and 0 < \Delta\eta < \min(r_c, 1-r_c), so that all rates lie in (0,1). There exist two K-cohort economies... Their impulse responses differ by ||h^A - h^B||_2 \asymp (\Delta\eta)^{2K-1}, so recovering the weights from N independent noisy reset experiments with worst-case error probability below 1/4 requires N \ge c \sigma^2 (\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)}."` (p. 38, Section 5.4).
   - **Status:** VERIFIED.
   - **Why it matters:** First, for $||h^A - h^B||_2$ (an $\ell_2(\mathbb{N}_0)$ norm over infinite lags) to be finite, the closed-loop economies must be Schur stable: $S = a + \kappa \sum_i q_i = a + \kappa \sum_i \frac{w_i}{1-r_i} < 1$. The theorem header fixes $W = \sum w_i > 0$ and $r_c \in (0,1)$, which does *not* guarantee $a + \kappa W / (1 - r_c - \Delta\eta) < 1$. Second, does a "reset experiment" observe infinitely many taps $T = \infty$ or $T \ge 2K+1$ taps? The upper bound (Theorem 5.5) only uses $2K+1$ taps, while the lower bound (Theorem 5.4) bounds the full infinite sequence $||h^A - h^B||_2$ (which dominates any finite-$T$ prefix and is therefore valid, but the observation contract must be stated).
   - **Exact fix:** Add `"Assume $a + \kappa W / (1 - r_c - \Delta\eta) < 1$ so that all candidate economies are uniformly Schur stable, and suppose each reset experiment observes $T$ noisy taps (for any $2K \le T \le \infty$)."` to the statement of Theorem 5.4.

3. **Proposition 6.1 (Page 49 & Page 84, Section 6 & Appendix A) — Algebra/terminology in the martingale difference decomposition of the IV score.**
   - **Quote:** `"...and writing \sum_t Z_t e_{i,t} = \sum_t (Z_t - r_i Z_{t+1})\epsilon_{i,t} plus a boundary term, the summands are martingale differences with respect to \mathcal{F}_t = \sigma((x_s)_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}, \epsilon_{i,s} : s \le t) because prices are independent of the measurement noise..."` (p. 84, Proof of Proposition 6.1). And on p. 49: `"\Omega_i = \sigma_f^2[(1+r_i^2)\Gamma_0 - r_i(\Gamma_1 + \Gamma_1^\top)], \Gamma_1 = \mathbb{E}[Z_t Z_{t-1}^\top]"`.
   - **Status:** VERIFIED.
   - **Why it matters:** If $Z_t = (x_{t-1}, x_t)^\top$ and $e_{i,t} = \epsilon_{i,t} - r_i \epsilon_{i,t-1}$, summation by parts gives $\sum_t Z_t e_{i,t} = \sum_t (Z_t - r_i Z_{t+1})\epsilon_{i,t}$ plus boundary terms. Under the stated assumption (`"with i.i.d. measurement noise of variance \sigma_f^2 independent of prices"`), $\epsilon_{i,t}$ does *not* enter the price equation (only true $u_{i,t} = q_i y_{i,t}$ enters $x_t$), so the full price path $(x_s)_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}$ is strictly exogenous to $(\epsilon_{i,t})$. Calling $Z_t e_{i,t}$ a `"martingale-difference-plus-MA(1) array"` before applying summation by parts is muddled terminology.
   - **Exact fix:** In the proof of Proposition 6.1 (p. 84), replace `"The scores Z_t e_{i,t} form a martingale-difference-plus-MA(1) array..."` through `"gives (20)"` with: `"By summation by parts, \sum_{t=1}^T Z_t e_{i,t} = \sum_{t=1}^T (Z_t - r_i Z_{t+1})\epsilon_{i,t} + O_P(1). Since (\epsilon_{i,t}) is i.i.d. zero-mean with variance \sigma_f^2 and independent of the entire stationary price process (x_s)_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}, the summands m_t = (Z_t - r_i Z_{t+1})\epsilon_{i,t} form a stationary ergodic martingale difference sequence with respect to \mathcal{F}_t = \sigma((x_s)_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}, \epsilon_{i,s} : s \le t) with covariance \mathbb{E}[m_t m_t^\top] = \sigma_f^2[(1+r_i^2)\Gamma_0 - r_i(\Gamma_1 + \Gamma_1^\top)] = \Omega_i, so the Billingsley-Ibragimov martingale CLT yields (20)."`

4. **Theorem 5.7 (Page 43–44 & Page 81–82, Section 5.5 & Appendix A) — Explicit triangle-inequality step in the Moment Comparison LP bound.**
   - **Quote:** `"...where ||\cdot||_{coef} is the sum of absolute monomial coefficients, ||T_j||_{coef} \le (1+\sqrt{2})^j, substituting y = \alpha r - 1 multiplies the coefficient sum of y^i by at most (1+\alpha)^i, and B(\rho) - 1 \ge 4."` (p. 81, Proof of Theorem 5.7).
   - **Status:** VERIFIED.
   - **Why it matters:** The Chebyshev substitution bound and $B(\rho) = (1+\sqrt{2})(1+2/\rho) > 3(1+\sqrt{2}) > 5 \implies B(\rho)-1 \ge 4$ are completely valid. However, in the Moment Comparison display on p. 81, $\hat{\mu}$ minimizes $\max_{j\le L} |\int r^j d\hat{\mu} - \hat{k}_j|$, so by the triangle inequality $\max_{j\le L} |\int r^j d\hat{\mu} - k_j| \le 2\delta$ on the event $\max_{j\le L} |\hat{k}_j - k_j| \le \delta$. Writing that triangle inequality step explicitly makes the proof self-contained.
   - **Exact fix:** Add one clause on p. 81 before `"Moment comparison"`: `"On the event \max_{j\le L}|\hat{k}_j - k_j| \le \delta, the true \mu attains LP objective at most \delta, so the minimizer \hat{\mu} satisfies \max_{j\le L}|\int r^j d\hat{\mu} - k_j| \le 2\delta."`

### MINOR Issues

5. **Theorem 5.2 (Page 36, Section 5.3) — Explain the fraction $8/11$ in $\Delta\eta \in (0, 8/11)$.**
   - **Quote:** `"Fix total intensity q = 0.20, mean rate \bar{\eta} = 0.40, target separation \tau = 0.01, and \Delta\eta \in (0, 8/11)."` (p. 36, Theorem 5.2).
   - **Status:** VERIFIED.
   - **Why it matters:** At $q=0.20$ and $\tau=0.01$, $p_+ = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{0.01}{0.20} = 0.55 = \frac{11}{20}$. For $\bar{\eta} - p_+ \Delta\eta > 0$ with $\bar{\eta} = 0.40 = \frac{8}{20}$, we need $\Delta\eta < \frac{8/20}{11/20} = \frac{8}{11}$. Stating this parenthetically saves the referee two minutes of scratch-pad arithmetic.
   - **Exact fix:** Append `"(so that \bar{\eta} - p_+\Delta\eta > 0)"` after $\Delta\eta \in (0, 8/11)$ on p. 36.

### Task 2: Detailed Verification of the Specific Requested Proofs
1. **Merged-measure identification (Theorem 3.1):** Verified. The transfer function $G(z) = \sum_i \frac{w_i z}{z - r_i}$ uniquely determines its poles $r_i \in (0,1)$ and non-zero residues $w_i$ by partial fractions, and $H(z) = \frac{\kappa z}{z - a - \kappa G(z)}$ is a bijection onto $G(z)$ when $a, \kappa$ are known. The edge case $\eta_i = 1 \implies r_i = 0$ shifting $a \mapsto a' = a + \kappa M(\{0\})$ and the cancellation at $z=0$ when $a'=0$ are handled accurately.
2. **Two-cohort theorem (Theorem 5.2):** Verified. Direct algebra confirms $M_0(\theta_1)-M_0(\theta_0) = 0$, $M_1(\theta_1)-M_1(\theta_0) = 0$, $M_2(\theta_1)-M_2(\theta_0) = 0$, and $M_3(\theta_1)-M_3(\theta_0) = 2q p_+ p_- (p_+^2 - p_-^2)(\Delta\eta)^3 = 4\tau p_- p_+ (\Delta\eta)^3$ since $p_+ - p_- = 2\tau/q$ and $p_+ + p_- = 1$.
3. **$K$-cohort rate $\Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-(4K-2)})$ (Theorems 5.4 & 5.5):** Verified. Lower bound uses K-point Gauss and Gauss-Radau quadrature on $[-1,1]$, which both have positive weights and match moments up to $2K-2$ while differing at $2K-1$. Upper bound uses triangular deconvolution followed by the quantitative Inverse Function Theorem on the confluent Vandermonde moment map (whose Jacobian determinant is $\prod_i \omega_i \prod_{i<j}(s_i-s_j)^4 > 0$ on $\gamma$-separated sets).
4. **General-memory $\Theta(1/\log N)$ result (Lemma 5.6 & Theorem 5.7):** Verified. Equioscillation points of best degree-$(2n-2)$ polynomial approximation to $|x|$ on $[-1,1]$ yield two $n$-atom measures with $W_1 \ge \rho E_{2n-2}(|x|) \asymp \rho/n$ and kernel $\ell_2$ distance $O(\rho^{2n-1})$. Setting $N \rho^{4n-2}/\sigma^2 \asymp 1$ gives $n \asymp \log(N/\sigma^2)$, hence minimax rate $\Theta(1/\log(N/\sigma^2))$.
5. **Passive bound $KL \le C T \epsilon^2$ (Theorem 5.9):** Verified. Using the Toeplitz matrix sandwich $\underline{f} I \le \Sigma_j \le \bar{f} I$ on the uniformly Schur-stable minimum-phase class avoids asymptotic Whittle approximations and yields an exact finite-$T$ bound $D_{KL}(P_1^T \| P_0^T) \le \frac{\bar{f}}{2\underline{f}} T (\frac{\bar{f}}{\underline{f}})^2 \sup_\omega |u(\omega)|^2 \le C T \epsilon^2$.
6. **Tagged-flow proposition (Proposition 6.1):** Verified (subject to Major Issue 3). The Jacobian determinant $\det \Sigma_i = w_i \sum_{l,m} c_l c_m \frac{1-\lambda_l\lambda_m}{1-r_i\lambda_m} > 0$ follows from Lemma 2.3 and positivity of $c_l \ge 0$.
7. **Disclosure-value theorem (Theorem 6.2):** Verified.
8. **Capital bound $\delta^{-4}$ (Theorems 4.3, D.2, D.3, D.4 & Lemma 4.5):** Verified. The spectral dissipation kernel $g_i(\omega) = \text{Re}\,\hat{H}_i(e^{i\omega}) - \kappa/2 = \frac{1}{2}\sum_l \omega_l \frac{1-\lambda_l^2}{|e^{i\omega}-\lambda_l|^2} > 0$ is strictly positive on $[-\pi,\pi]$ by Lemma 2.3, which gives the exact Rayleigh-quotient lower bound $C_{det,quad}^*(\delta)$.
