# P4 — AI-assisted internal review: securities regulation and market-structure policy
**Scope:** manuscript PDF and checks of primary official regulatory sources. Not an SEC economist, independent legal opinion or independent reviewer. Printed page numbers (PDF +3 after front matter). **Regulatory-source audit performed 30 October 2026.**

## A. Verdict
**The regulatory facts are mostly better than the financial-policy interpretation.** The $147.0m March 2026 CAT budget is a true, verifiable number, and the manuscript correctly notes that CAT serves enforcement, surveillance and market reconstruction. The problem is the repeated use of an artificial weekly probe-loss benchmark to imply the economic benefit of a national audit trail. A regulation specialist will immediately distinguish tagged transaction records, published class-position aggregates and private algorithm identifiers, and will ask whether the saved statistical sample requirement improves an actual regulatory decision at all. I would ask for major reframing before public circulation.

## B. Readiness
**5.5/10 now; 8/10 after the source-vintage table and policy claim repair.**

## C. Issue register
### FATAL
No verified materially false source number found for **the three principal nominal CAT budget vintages**. It would become fatal to regulatory credibility to claim the theoretical shadow loss is measured regulatory savings or that CAT actually supplies algorithm-level rules.

### MAJOR
1. **CAT 2026 budget is CORRECT but its vintage must be explicit — printed pp. 1, 48, 60–62, §§1/6.2/7/8. VERIFIED with primary source.** Quote: “CAT LLC 2026 Financial and Operating Budget … total expenses of `$147.0 million`.” The **March 31, 2026 revised accrual-basis CAT budget** lists **Total Expenses = $147,044,869**. It separately lists **Total Estimated Incurred Expenses = $138,665,904**, and the previous **original 2026 budget** was roughly $156m. **Fix:** call it “March 31, 2026 updated CAT LLC annual total-expense budget ($147,044,869)” throughout. Official budget: https://www.catnmsplan.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/03.31.26-CAT-2026-Budget.pdf . Do not substitute estimated incurred expenses or an earlier approved version silently.

2. **The 2025 numbers are CORRECT but an important intervening 2025 revision is omitted — printed p. 48, §6.2; p. 60 §7. VERIFIED with SEC official public statements.** Quote: “`$248.8 million` … revised to `$228.3 million` in May 2025.” SEC Chairman's September 30, 2025 statement cites **$248,846,076** in the original November 2024 budget, revised to **$228,334,551** on May 19, 2025. A 2026 SEC concept release says further reduced to **approximately $188m in November 2025**. **Fix:** state all dates and the November 2025 revision if the paper claims these trace a comparable annual budget series; a '2025 $228.3m' unqualified would be outdated. Sources: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-093025-consolidated-audit-trail-new-day-cat ; https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/04/s7-2026-12 .

3. **2016 $1.7bn / 2012 $2.1bn are historical modeled estimates, not modern CAT LLC operating budgets — printed p. 48, §6.2. SUSPECT as to precise original-page numbers (not separately inspected); period mismatch VERIFIED.** Quote: “the SEC's 2016 CAT NMS Plan Approval Order estimate of `$2.4 billion` in initial industry build costs and `$1.7 billion per year` in ongoing industry costs”; “estimating `$4.0 billion` initial and `$2.1 billion` annual ongoing costs.” Those are **industry-wide ex ante regulatory burden estimates**, not CAT LLC's current cash or accrual budget. **Fix:** put historical ex ante estimates in a *separate* table with the calculation scope and reference release; remove them from any contemporary like-for-like comparison until the exact original sources are independently page-checked. The exact historical numbers are not VERIFIED by this review.

4. **CAT's collected content and data purpose are oversimplified — printed p. 60, §7. VERIFIED in main outlines; details need nuance.** Quote: “gives the SEC and the self-regulatory organizations a record of every order in NMS stocks and listed options from origination to execution or cancellation.” Rule 613 covers reportable events on NMS securities **across venues** and identifies orders, accounts and routing events; 'every' should be conditioned on **reporting scope and exemptions**, rather than implying perfect complete data. **Fix:** “CAT is designed to capture specified order and reportable-event information for NMS securities across participating venues and broker-dealers; actual scope is governed by Rule 613, the Plan and exemptions.” Source: https://www.sec.gov/files/divisions/marketreg/rule613-info.htm .

5. **2025 personal-identifying-info change is real — printed p. 60, §7. VERIFIED with primary SEC statement/order.** Quote: “since 2025 it no longer collects the names, addresses and years of birth of natural persons.” More precisely February 2025 relief applies to specified **natural-person records with transformed SSNs/ITINs**, with earlier relief for SSNs/date of birth. Relevant identifiers/corresponding records continue under the plan, and regulators can request protected identifying information from broker-dealers when warranted. **Fix:** state the scope of the relief; don't imply all identifying information was eliminated. Source: https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/crenshaw-statement-consolidated-audit-trail-021025 ; official relief as published 2025-02620.

6. **MiFID II algorithm identifiers: substantively true, imprecise rule citation — printed p. 1, §1; p. 60, §7. VERIFIED.** Quote: “Under MiFID II, EU investment firms must flag algorithmic orders and identify the algorithm responsible for each investment and execution decision.” The concrete order-record obligations are in **Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/580, RTS 24, Article 2 and Table 2 fields 4/5**, which record person or algorithm responsible for the investment/execution decision where relevant, with prescribed exceptions and roles. RTS 6 is primarily organizational requirements for algorithmic trading; do not cite RTS 6 alone as the legal source for the order identifiers. **Fix:** cite RTS 24 article/fields and clarify that these are order-record identifiers, not access to model weights or learning rates. Source: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32017R0580 .

7. **CFTC data must be described as aggregate position reporting, not per-trade labels — printed pp. 52–54, §6.3, p. 59 §7. VERIFIED.** Quote: “The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission requires large traders in regulated futures markets to report their positions and publishes them weekly by class.” This is correct at a high level. The weekly COT reports aggregate **Tuesday** positions under categories from trader reports and Commission review, with **Friday** publication; the confidential daily large-trader system is not the same as the public class report. **Fix:** say that COT reports class-position snapshots and does not identify which algorithm submitted each order. Sources: https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/CommitmentsofTraders/index.htm ; https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/MarketSurveillance/LargeTraderReportingProgram/index.htm .

8. **Probing loss compared with audit trail budget is not a cost-benefit analysis — printed pp. 47–49, §6.2; p. 60 §7; pp. 61–62 §8. VERIFIED as a category mismatch.** Quote: “annualised weekly-renewal probing loss that disclosure makes unnecessary … exceeds the entire 2026 CAT budget.” A regulator does **not** routinely make 52 repeated market-moving experimental trades each year to re-identify cohort rates; the audit trail is predominantly justified for surveillance, reconstruction, enforcement and regulatory investigations. The $147m budget pays for functions not measured in the model and covers a **different market universe** than the crypto/futures case studies. The manuscript acknowledges caveats but repeats the emotive dollar comparison in its conclusion. **Fix:** remove CAT breakeven thresholds from abstract, results map and conclusion; preserve one explicitly **non-comparable scale illustration** in §6.2 with a subtitle “hypothetical outside-prober shadow loss; not a public cost-benefit calculation.”

9. **Account IDs do not identify algorithm memory — printed p. 60, §7. VERIFIED by the model/data contract.** Quote: “participant-level records remove a barrier” and “an audit trail records accounts, not decision rules.” The second quote correctly limits the first; it must be given priority. Even an account identifier need not decompose strategies when a single legal account runs multiple algorithms. **Fix:** “Account-level CAT data may improve attribution, but do not automatically provide the class-homogeneous flow assumed by Proposition 6.1.”

10. **Strong PII inference outruns model — printed p. 60, §7. SUSPECT as policy relevance.** Quote: “For this purpose a stable account identifier is what matters and names are not needed, which is a quantitative reason to keep the account linkage while removing personal data.” The narrow *model-specific* identification goal may not require names, but enforcement often does require legal identities and accountability, with different privacy/security tradeoffs. **Fix:** “For estimating aggregate class response under our model, persistent pseudonymous labels suffice; this does not resolve enforcement identification or CAT data-minimization design.”

11. **The 'economies of scale' argument is not a CAT design explanation — printed p. 51, Proposition 6.3 and discussion. SUSPECT.** Quote: “consistent with the design of market-wide regimes such as CAT rather than venue-by-venue reporting.” The mathematical fixed-cost comparison is correct for its toy cost function but was not estimated from CAT and omits public-goods/evasion benefits and legal duties. **Fix:** “illustrates a possible economy of scale; does not explain CAT's legal architecture or demonstrate welfare optimality.”

12. **Claim about who can access CAT needs correction/precision — printed p. 60, §7. SUSPECT.** Quote: “its data are not available to exchanges, other participants or researchers.” CAT NMS **participants include exchanges and FINRA**, which have access conditioned on regulatory purpose/security; therefore “not available to exchanges” broadly is misleading. **Fix:** “CAT data are not generally available as public market data; regulatory authorities and authorized SRO participants have controlled access. Ordinary outside desks and academic researchers do not generally have unrestricted access.”

## What a regulator might actually find useful
“The paper offers a tractable example of a surveillance identification problem: when multiple reactive participants contribute to a single aggregate price-response function, public price/flow data can poorly resolve the composition of those participants even when aggregate dynamics are estimable. This distinguishes a measurement-value argument for transaction-level identifiers from a broader case for audit trails. It does not estimate the real costs or benefits of CAT, prove that account identifiers reveal algorithm strategies, or show how the Commission would act on inferred memory parameters.”
