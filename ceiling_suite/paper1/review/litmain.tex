\subsection{Relation to the literature}\label{sec:litmain}
Three literatures meet here. In finance, learning algorithms and AI agents in markets are studied by simulating or modelling them \citep{colliard2022,dou2025}; traders who differ in horizon or speed are studied in equilibrium \citep{cespa2015,biais2015,crouzet2020}; the exponentially fading beliefs we use are the extrapolative beliefs of \citet{barberis2015,barberis2018}; and heterogeneous-agent models estimated from prices often leave the type-separating parameters imprecise \citep{brock1998,amilon2008,kukacka2017}, for which our results give one reason. Price formation follows the linear-impact tradition \citep{kyle1985,glosten1985,hasbrouck1991} and the transient-impact literature \citep{obizhaeva2013,gatheral2010,alfonsi2012}; trader types have been inferred from public data, as in the probability of informed trading \citep{easley1996}, and from account data \citep{kirilenko2017}. Learning by trading has an equilibrium counterpart in price experimentation \citep{leach1993}, and execution that learns impact while trading is studied by \citet{moallemi2008} and \citet{parkvanroy2015}. Technically, the closest work is closed-loop identification \citep{ljung1999,forssell1999}, weak identification \citep{andrews2012}, and super-resolution and mixture estimation \citep{moitra2015,heinrich2018,wu2020,batenkov2021,katz2023}. We do not claim the $K$-group exponent as new: in mixture language it is the square of the condition number for $K$ clustered nodes \citep{heinrich2018,wu2020}, and Prony's method attains it \citep{katz2023}; the $\delta^{-4}$ law for one against two close components is classical in resolution analysis \citep{shahram2005}; logarithmic rates are known for mixtures of exponentials \citep{rebafka2015}; and labelled samples are known to be more informative than unlabelled ones \citep{castelli1996}. What is new is the closed-loop form (the memories are zeros, not poles, of the observed transfer function, so they must first be extracted from the loop), the passive equivalence, the translation of sample sizes into executed quantity, capital and money, and the value of class records, including a decision in which attribution changes the right action. Supplement~\ref{sec:lit} gives the full comparison, with tables of the closest results.
