\begin{abstract}
\noindent We formalize the financial system identification of closed-loop algorithmic markets, asking what public price and flow data can reveal regarding traders' adaptive memory and at what financial cost. We prove that public ticker feeds cannot distinguish algorithmic cohorts sharing an adaptation rate, and establishing memory separation $\delta$ requires active executed quantity of order $\delta^{-2}$ or capital of order $\delta^{-4}$. Passive waiting cannot substitute for active probing when new excitation is absent. Via spectral and finite-horizon Rayleigh--H\"older duality, we certify a $61.44$~USDT infinite-horizon capital lower bound at $\delta=0.05$, close the finite-horizon non-convex fee/margin duality gap to $0.98\%$ at $L=80$ (continuous interval-certified lower bound $64.74$~USDT vs.\ feasible $65.38$~USDT), and establish a best evaluated feasible account-capital upper bound of $64.56$~USDT (attained at active horizon $L=240$ and preserved for all $L \ge 240$ by zero-padding monotonicity $C^*(L+1) \le C^*(L)$); for unknown two-cohort mixtures, an exact moment-matched Le Cam two-point construction proves a global non-asymptotic $\Omega((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ sample-complexity lower bound across all measurable estimators (including biased, ridge, and James--Stein shrinkage estimators; $5{,}928$ resets under the literal moment-matched pair and $6{,}173$ under closed-loop curvature refinement at $\Delta\eta=0.10$), paired with a matching $\mathcal{O}((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ cubic moment-inversion upper bound on known-midpoint families and a local perturbation/conditioning bound for parameter-blind Prony--Hankel recovery on separated mixtures. In an empirical 768-unit panel, feedback estimates are fragile to flow timing and filtering. Finally, coupling multi-cohort AI feedback to an explicit double-entry dealer cash, inventory, and Binance Tier-2 maintenance-margin ledger, our Real-Time Resolvent Solvency Guard ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$) establishes conditional bounded-shock forward solvency under worst-case norm-bounded or adversarially signed disturbances ($\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe}} = 4.03$~USDT at initial state, with explicit \texttt{NO\_SAFE\_ACTION} detection), achieving $0.0\%$ liquidation and $100.0\%$ model admission eligibility across $400$ stress episodes, across strictly causal pre-window out-of-sample 1-second crash-burst OLS operators ($t<s$ only, zero lookahead; $100.0\%$ unguarded/eigenvalue liquidation vs.\ $0.0\%$ under $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$), and on $100\%$ unmodified historical spot prices with endogenous order-flow inventory accumulation ($22.0\%$ single-asset and $36.0\%$ 8-asset unguarded liquidation vs.\ $0.0\%$ under $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Electronic execution and market making raise a financial identification question: what can aggregate price and flow observations reveal about trading rules, and which financial constraints govern experiments intended to learn those rules? Microstructure research documents the interaction of trades, quotes and automated liquidity provision \citep{hasbrouck1991,menkveld2013,budish2015}. We study a declared linear feedback economy and its information/resource contracts, then test whether observable measurement diagnostics support the corresponding interpretation.

When trading algorithms condition their order submission and quote-placement decisions on recent transaction prices, prevailing order book imbalances, and trade innovations, the price-formation process ceases to be an open-loop mechanism driven purely by exogenous informational updates regarding fundamentals. Instead, price formation becomes an autonomous \emph{closed-loop dynamical system}: trading decisions generate endogenous price impact, and the resulting price adjustments directly feed back into the learning rules, momentum filters, risk-budgeting heuristics, and execution programs of competing market participants \citep{bouchaud2004fluctuations,cont2010stochastic,cont2014}. 

This closed-loop structural reality was dramatically underscored by major episodic disruptions in electronic markets over the past decade and a half. While disruptions such as the May 6, 2010 Flash Crash in the E-mini S\&P 500 and the October 15, 2014 Treasury flash rally provide compelling historical motivation for analyzing how algorithmic feedback can rapidly deplete liquidity, direct empirical investigation of these episodes is constrained by the non-public nature of regulatory account registries. Modern electronic cryptocurrency order books provide an ideal, transparent empirical testing ground. Electronic cryptocurrency venues provide public trade and book data, but these records do not identify the fraction of activity generated by adaptive algorithms. The supplied samples have the clocks and coverage described below; they do not establish universal nanosecond observation or the absence of off-exchange execution. We therefore exploit high-frequency cryptocurrency order books (complemented by a supplied equity representation whose normalized-source transformation now reproduces exactly, while company-label mapping and physical scale remain unresolved) as an empirical testing ground for our broader theoretical framework.

These information and financial questions concern financial economists, central banks, and market regulators with an urgent, foundational question:
\begin{quote}
\centering
\emph{Can observable market data identify destabilizing adaptive feedback, when does that feedback become financially dangerous, and what information is fundamentally missing from public surveillance data?}
\end{quote}

In this paper, we address this question directly by integrating realization-theoretic system identification with empirical limit order book econometrics and balance-sheet financial constraints. Rather than making speculative assertions regarding black-box artificial intelligence, we organize our theoretical and empirical findings around \textbf{two foundational pillars}:

\paragraph{Pillar 1: Observable Feedback Does Not Imply Identifiable Machine Behavior.}
We identify the merged rate--weight representation under the modeled reset price-response contract, with known anchor and impact (Theorem~\ref{thm:m1_equivalence}). The resource comparison establishes necessary and sufficient quantity order $\delta^{-2}$ for a known symmetric pair and horizon order $\delta^{-4}$ under average power (Theorem~\ref{thm:resource_frontier}). Actual trading cost gives matching capital order $\delta^{-4}$ for deterministic probes financed by a trader who remains solvent after temporary impact dissipates, when the statistical observer receives only noisy price states (Theorem~\ref{thm:trader_capital_frontier}). A separate counterparty account gives a sharp robust quantity/margin allowance (Theorem~\ref{thm:cash_capital_frontier}). Waiting cannot compensate for a fixed lifetime executed-quantity budget. The dealer's inventory change creates a quadratic adverse mark, rather than only a fixed linear price exposure. These are distinct input, cash and observation contracts. Internet Appendix~\ref{ia:breakthrough_foundations} extends Pillar~1 along three axes: (i) spectral and finite-horizon Rayleigh--H\"older duality (Theorems~\ref{thm:ia_exact_capital} and~\ref{thm:ia_nonconvex_duality}), certifying $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{det}}(0.05) = 61.44$~USDT, closing the finite-horizon non-convex fee/margin duality gap to $0.98\%$ at $L=80$ (continuous interval-certified lower bound $64.74$~USDT vs.\ feasible $65.38$~USDT), and establishing a monotone best evaluated feasible account-capital upper bound $\overline{C}_0^{\mathrm{feas}}(\le L) = 64.56$~USDT for $L \ge 240$ (satisfying $C^*(L+1) \le C^*(L)$ via zero-padding, while unpadded full-support eigenvector candidates $C_0^{\mathrm{unpad}}(L)$ rise to $64.61$--$64.62$~USDT at $L \in \{320, 480\}$); (ii) adaptive sequential probing (Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_adaptive_capital}), separating expected trading loss ($45.96$~USDT) from initial tail solvency capital ($p99 = 155.99$~USDT, $37.73\%$ ruin at mean-loss funding); and (iii) unknown-mixture recovery (Proposition~\ref{prop:ia_cohort_information} and Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_minimax_lecam}), proving via an exact moment-matched cubic identity ($\Delta M_3 = 4\tau p_- p_+ (\Delta\eta)^3$) that every measurable estimator (including biased, ridge, and James--Stein shrinkage estimators) obeys the global non-asymptotic minimax lower bound $N^*_{\mathrm{LeCam}}(\Delta\eta) = \Omega((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ ($5{,}928$ resets under the literal moment-matched pair and $6{,}173$ under closed-loop curvature refinement at $\Delta\eta=0.10$, vs.\ $40{,}374$ under the local unbiased CRB), paired with a matching $\mathcal{O}((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ cubic moment-inversion upper bound on known-midpoint families and a local perturbation/conditioning bound for parameter-blind Prony--Hankel recovery.

The empirical reconstruction tests observable measurement and model restrictions rather than claiming latent learning identification. In the original 768-unit panel, mean legacy forward-flow $Q=0.0081536$ becomes $0.0003807$ with observed flow; pooled raw-return $Q=-0.0001625$ has a date interval including zero. The recorder timing comparison is a different sample and is reported separately. Maintained covariance restrictions reject, and the fee-schedule exercise is a one-treated-asset descriptive association with covariance-sensitive uncertainty. These findings establish why a public reduced-form coefficient cannot be substituted for the controlled information contract.

\paragraph{Pillar 2: Dynamic Eigenvalue Stability Does Not Imply Financial Safety.}
For a fixed initial-disturbance and model-output contract, spectral stability ($\rho(J)<1$) alone does not bound the permitted loss-budget radius. A dealer solvency interpretation additionally requires a justified monetary accounting map. Because the closed-loop market Jacobian $J$ is generically non-normal under asymmetric price-impact and memory parameters (with $J J^\top - J^\top J \neq 0$ except on a measure-zero algebraic subvariety), stable eigenvalues can coexist with large declared-output gain under an appropriate disturbance/output contract. The exact initial-state radius for that declared loss-budget experiment is governed by peak output magnification:
\begin{equation}
r^*_{\text{exact}} = \frac{B}{\sup_{t \ge 0} \|C J^t\|_2} \equiv \frac{B}{M_C},
\end{equation}
which the Kreiss constant $\mathcal{K}(J)$ bounds and the spectral radius completely fails to control (Theorem~\ref{thm:m5_kreiss_solvency}). By Spijker's theorem, under full-state observation ($C = I$), we establish the bilateral bounds:
\begin{equation}
\frac{B}{e \cdot n \cdot \mathcal{K}(J)} \le r^*_{\text{exact}} \le \frac{B}{\mathcal{K}(J)},
\end{equation}
proving that $\frac{B}{e n \mathcal{K}(J)}$ provides a guaranteed safe lower bound, whereas $\frac{B}{\mathcal{K}(J)}$ is an upper bound that must never be mistaken for a conservative safety threshold. The separate lagged-memory experiment reproduces 19,200 paths across 48 cells, but its halt event combines equity and position-limit causes. The 22,000-path funded grid instead has positive-equity inventory-limit events in effective supercritical dynamics. Neither supplies observed market defaults or an independently calibrated policy benefit. Long-run variance and marginal variance are distinct quantities. Under an explicit double-entry dealer cash, inventory, linear-book fill, costly hedge execution, and January 2023 Binance USD$\circledS$-M Tier-2 maintenance-margin ledger, Internet Appendix~\ref{ia:crash_free_guard} (Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_crash_free_guard}) proves conditional bounded-shock forward solvency for the Real-Time Resolvent Solvency Guard ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$) under state-dependent remaining margin buffer $B_{\mathrm{rem},t} = E_t - \mathrm{MM}_t$ and worst-case norm-bounded or adversarially signed disturbances ($\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe}} = 4.03$~USDT at $t=0$, with explicit \texttt{NO\_SAFE\_ACTION} detection), achieving $0.0\%$ liquidation and $100.0\%$ model admission eligibility across $400$ stress episodes, across both pooled and strictly causal pre-window out-of-sample 1-second crash-burst OLS operators ($\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}} \in [14.16, 29.38]$, $\rho < 0.90$, where eigenvalue-only guards suffer $100.0\%$ liquidation), and on $100\%$ unmodified historical spot prices with endogenous order-flow inventory accumulation ($22.0\%$ single-asset and $36.0\%$ 8-asset unguarded liquidation vs.\ $0.0\%$ under $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$).

\subsection{Related Literature}
Our work bridges four major strands of literature across financial economics, market microstructure, systemic risk, and dynamical systems theory.

\paragraph{High-Frequency Trading and Market Microstructure.}
Beginning with the seminal contributions of \citet{kyle1985continuous} and \citet{glosten1985bid}, classical microstructure models analyze the informational content of order flow and dealer inventory management under asymmetric information. \citet{hasbrouck1991} pioneered the empirical vector autoregressive decomposition of trades and quotes, establishing that trade innovations exert persistent impacts on security prices. \citet{campbell1993trading} demonstrated that trading volume and return serial correlations reflect interactions between liquidity traders and risk-averse market makers.

In modern electronic markets, high-frequency trading firms and automated algorithms dominate liquidity provision and execution \citep{menkveld2013,budish2015,biais2015}. While \citet{hendershott2011does} document that algorithmic trading improves liquidity and narrows bid-ask spreads during normal trading periods, \citet{budish2015} show that continuous double auctions generate socially wasteful latency arms races, and \citet{biais2015} demonstrate that algorithmic speed advantages can lead to adverse selection spirals. We advance this literature by formalizing the closed loop between algorithmic learning rules and endogenous price impact, establishing conditional realization and resource bounds for the declared controlled experiment. Public ticker data require a separate observation and identification argument.

\paragraph{Price Impact and Limit Order Book Dynamics.}
An extensive literature studies price impact, signed-flow memory and quote imbalance \citep{bouchaud2004fluctuations,cont2014}. Our long-run regression diagnostics retain these as competing explanations. Lag controls and observable kernel signs do not identify parent orders, learner policies or contemporaneous structural shocks.

\paragraph{Intermediary Solvency, Funding Liquidity, and Systemic Risk.}
The distinction between market liquidity and funding liquidity is central to modern theories of financial fragility \citep{brunnermeier2009,adrian2010liquidity,he2013intermediary}. When leveraged market makers suffer balance-sheet drawdowns, risk-management constraints (such as Value-at-Risk limits or regulatory margin requirements) force them to withdraw quotes, widening spreads and amplifying price dislocations \citep{greenwood2015vulnerable,eisenberg2001systemic}. Our conditional loss-output analysis separates spectral stability from a declared loss budget. The empirical studies do not demonstrate intermediary insolvency in a calibrated stable market; the positive price-only response has the more restrictive geometry proved below.

\paragraph{Non-Normal Dynamics and the Kreiss Matrix Theorem.}
Non-normal transients and Kreiss bounds are established mathematical tools \citep{kreiss1962,spijker1991,trefethen2005spectra}. Their application to financial bubbles also precedes this paper: \citet{sornette2023nonnormal} links subcritical financial dynamics to non-normal networks and the Kreiss constant. We therefore do not claim first application to finance. Our proposed contribution connects a particular memory-resolution experiment to executed-quantity and monetary-output resources; the underlying norm and testing tools remain established.

\paragraph{Identification and Experiment Design.}
Known finite hypothesis classes already admit sample-complexity bounds and excitation design \citep{chatzikiriakos2024finite,chatzikiriakos2026active}. The latter studies fully observed states with Gaussian process noise and bounded average input power. Our price-observation experiment instead specifies lifetime executed quantity, eventual-solvency capital and richer financial-disclosure boundaries in a noiseless-state cohort model. These are contract and resource distinctions to assess against established identification theory; we do not claim first finite-model testing or constrained excitation.

\paragraph{The proposed incremental contribution.}
The distinction is the joint specification of the observer, execution resource and financing account. The controlled experiment compares two known scalar price-response laws generated by different latent cohort configurations. Its exact finite-horizon quantity optimum is combined with a self-financing account and an explicit counterexample under exact equity disclosure. Gaussian testing and quadratic transient-impact costs separately precede this paper \citep{chatzikiriakos2026active,alfonsi2016matrix}; the proposed increment is their quantified connection to memory resolution under this particular information contract. An economically relevant observer is an outside analyst restricted to noisy aggregate price reports and disclosed probe quantities, while the financing trader's exact execution/account records are unavailable to that analyst. An account owner with exact marks faces a different experiment. Public quotation residuals cannot be identified with the theorem's measurement-noise variance without a separate observation-error model. The contribution must therefore be assessed as a conditional resolution-and-disclosure result, rather than general participant attribution or a universal capital barrier.

\section{Theoretical Model and Identification Boundaries}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Microfoundations of Closed-Loop Price Formation}
We formalize an electronic financial market sampled at discrete uniform time intervals $t \in \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}$, where time step $\Delta$ represents the operational sampling frequency (e.g., 1 second). Let $p_t$ denote the price level in monetary units of a traded asset, and let $p_t^*$ denote the unobserved fundamental value, which evolves as a martingale:
\begin{equation}
p_{t+1}^* = p_t^* + \xi_{t+1}, \qquad \xi_{t+1} \sim \text{i.i.d.}(0, \sigma_\xi^2).
\label{eq:fundamental_process}
\end{equation}
We define the pricing error (displacement from fundamental) as $x_t \equiv p_t - p_t^*$.

\paragraph{Dealer Pricing, Posted Depth, and Equilibrium Price Impact.}
Following the continuous auction framework of \citet{kyle1985continuous} adapted to discrete order books \citep{glosten1985bid,hasbrouck1991,bouchaud2004fluctuations}, competitive dealers absorb net incoming order flow by posting aggregate limit order depth $D_t > 0$. Total incoming flow combines algorithmic demand $u_t$ and exogenous liquidity noise $\varepsilon_{t+1} \sim \text{i.i.d.}(0, \sigma_\varepsilon^2)$, counterbalanced by fundamentalist arbitrageurs submitting demand $-\varphi x_t$.

The clearing price adjustment across posted depth satisfies:
\begin{equation}
p_{t+1} - p_t = \frac{1}{D_t} \left( u_t - \varphi x_t + \varepsilon_{t+1} \right) \equiv \kappa_t \left( u_t - \varphi x_t + \varepsilon_{t+1} \right),
\end{equation}
where equilibrium price impact $\kappa_t \equiv 1/D_t$ is the structural inverse of available limit depth. Subtracting fundamental increment $\xi_{t+1}$ from both sides yields the pricing error recursion:
\begin{equation}
x_{t+1} = (1 - \kappa_t \varphi) x_t + \kappa_t u_t + \left( \kappa_t \varepsilon_{t+1} - \xi_{t+1} \right).
\end{equation}
Defining direct price persistence $a_t \equiv 1 - \kappa_t \varphi \in [0, 1)$ and combined innovation $w_{t+1} \equiv \kappa_t \varepsilon_{t+1} - \xi_{t+1}$, we obtain the foundational pricing equation (evaluated at steady-state depth $D^*$, with $\kappa \equiv 1/D^*$):
\begin{equation}
x_{t+1} = a x_t + \kappa u_t + w_{t+1}, \qquad w_{t+1} \sim \text{i.i.d.}(0, \sigma_w^2).
\label{eq:micro_pricing}
\end{equation}
When fundamentalist arbitrage is strong ($\varphi \to 1/\kappa$), $a \to 0$ and prices immediately reflect fundamentals. For fixed $\varphi$, decreasing depth increases $\kappa$ and decreases $a=1-\kappa\varphi$; it does not make $a$ approach unity. Maintaining $0\le a<1$ requires $0<\kappa\varphi\le1$. An endogenous arbitrage response would require its own economic specification.

\paragraph{Algorithmic Learning Microfoundations.}
We consider $K$ heterogeneous cohorts of algorithmic traders. Each cohort $i \in \{1, \dots, K\}$ maintains an internal state variable $y_{i,t} \in \R$ representing its estimate of price momentum, trend, or fundamental mispricing. The specified Kalman and regularized gradient models give exponential-memory recursions. Embedding either in the price-first cohort economy additionally requires the stated signal, gain and timing assumptions; the empirical regressions do not identify that embedding.

\begin{proposition}[Steady-State Kalman Filtering Yields Exponential Memory]
\label{prop:kalman}
As an independent microfoundation for exponential-memory signals, suppose an algorithmic execution desk tracks a latent local trend $v_t \in \R$ following a random walk $v_{t+1} = v_t + \zeta_{t+1}$ with independent centered Gaussian innovations of variance $\sigma_\zeta^2>0$, observing $r_t=v_t+\nu_t$ with independent centered Gaussian observation noise of variance $\sigma_\nu^2>0$. Assume a Gaussian initial state and the steady-state prior covariance. The minimum mean-squared error (MMSE) estimator is given by the steady-state Kalman filter recursion:
\begin{equation}
\hat{v}_{t+1} = (1 - \eta) \hat{v}_t + \eta r_t,
\label{eq:kalman_recursion}
\end{equation}
where the steady-state Kalman gain $\eta \in (0, 1)$ is the unique positive root of the algebraic Riccati equation:
\begin{equation}
\eta = \frac{-\sigma_\zeta^2 + \sqrt{\sigma_\zeta^4 + 4 \sigma_\zeta^2 \sigma_\nu^2}}{2 \sigma_\nu^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The scalar state-space model is $v_{t+1} = v_t + \zeta_{t+1}$ and $r_t = v_t + \nu_t$. Let $P_{\infty}$ denote the steady-state prior error variance. The Riccati equation is $P_\infty = P_\infty - \frac{P_\infty^2}{P_\infty + \sigma_\nu^2} + \sigma_\zeta^2$, which simplifies to $P_\infty^2 - \sigma_\zeta^2 P_\infty - \sigma_\zeta^2 \sigma_\nu^2 = 0$. The unique positive root is $P_\infty = \frac{\sigma_\zeta^2 + \sqrt{\sigma_\zeta^4 + 4\sigma_\zeta^2 \sigma_\nu^2}}{2}$. The steady-state Kalman gain is $\eta = \frac{P_\infty}{P_\infty + \sigma_\nu^2} \in (0, 1)$. Substituting into the update $\hat{v}_{t+1} = \hat{v}_t + \eta (r_t - \hat{v}_t)$ yields \eqref{eq:kalman_recursion}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Regularized Online Gradient Descent Yields Exponential Memory]
\label{prop:ogd}
Consider an automated trading agent updating policy weights $\theta_t \in \R$ via Online Gradient Descent (OGD) with $L_2$ weight regularization $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ to minimize a quadratic prediction loss $\mathcal{L}(\theta_t; x_t) = \frac{1}{2} (x_t - \theta_t)^\top H (x_t - \theta_t)$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{t+1} = (1 - \gamma) \theta_t - \alpha \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}(\theta_t; x_t),
\end{equation}
where $H>0$ is scalar curvature and $\alpha>0$ is the learning step size. If the effective adaptation rate $\eta \equiv \gamma + \alpha H \in (0, 1)$ and we define gain $q \equiv \frac{\alpha H}{\gamma + \alpha H}$, the parameter recursion is mathematically identical to:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{t+1} = (1 - \eta) \theta_t + \eta q x_t.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the loss, $\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L} = -H(x_t - \theta_t)$. The parameter update becomes:
$\theta_{t+1} = (1 - \gamma)\theta_t + \alpha H (x_t - \theta_t) = (1 - (\gamma + \alpha H))\theta_t + \alpha H x_t$.
Substituting $\eta = \gamma + \alpha H$ and $q = \frac{\alpha H}{\eta}$ yields $\theta_{t+1} = (1 - \eta)\theta_t + \eta q x_t$.
\end{proof}

Propositions~\ref{prop:kalman} and~\ref{prop:ogd} demonstrate that exponential memory decay is not an ad hoc modeling assumption, but rather a common local linear reduction of standard optimal filtering and regularized gradient optimization.

\paragraph{The Joint Closed-Loop Dynamical System.}
Let cohort $i$ submit demand proportional to its state, $u_{i,t} = q_i y_{i,t}$, where $q_i \in \R$ reflects aggressive capital allocation ($q_i > 0$ represents trend-following / momentum; $q_i < 0$ represents mean-reversion). Total algorithmic demand is $u_t = \sum_{i=1}^K q_i y_{i,t}$. Each cohort updates its internal state according to:
\begin{equation}
y_{i, t+1} = (1 - \eta_i) y_{i, t} + \eta_i x_{t+1}, \qquad \eta_i \in (0, 1).
\label{eq:cohort_update}
\end{equation}

Substituting the market price formation equation \eqref{eq:micro_pricing} into the update rule \eqref{eq:cohort_update}, we obtain:
\begin{equation}
y_{i, t+1} = (1 - \eta_i) y_{i, t} + \eta_i \left( a x_t + \kappa \sum_{j=1}^K q_j y_{j, t} + w_{t+1} \right).
\end{equation}
Stacking the pricing error $x_t$ and the $K$ internal cohort states into the state vector $\mathbf{z}_t = (x_t, y_{1,t}, \dots, y_{K,t})^\top \in \R^{K+1}$, the joint economy forms an autonomous discrete-time linear dynamical system:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{t+1} = J \mathbf{z}_t + \mathbf{b} w_{t+1},
\label{eq:state_space_system}
\end{equation}
where the closed-loop system Jacobian matrix $J \in \R^{(K+1) \times (K+1)}$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
J = \begin{bmatrix}
a & \kappa q_1 & \kappa q_2 & \cdots & \kappa q_K \\
\eta_1 a & 1 - \eta_1 + \eta_1 \kappa q_1 & \eta_1 \kappa q_2 & \cdots & \eta_1 \kappa q_K \\
\eta_2 a & \eta_2 \kappa q_1 & 1 - \eta_2 + \eta_2 \kappa q_2 & \cdots & \eta_2 \kappa q_K \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
\eta_K a & \eta_K \kappa q_1 & \eta_K \kappa q_2 & \cdots & 1 - \eta_K + \eta_K \kappa q_K
\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:full_jacobian}
\end{equation}
and $\mathbf{b} = (1, \eta_1, \eta_2, \dots, \eta_K)^\top$.

\paragraph{Single-Cohort Stability Boundary.}
In the single-cohort case ($K=1$), defining total loop feedback gain $Q \equiv \kappa q_1$, the characteristic polynomial of $J$ is:
\begin{equation}
P(\lambda) = \det(\lambda I - J) = \lambda^2 - \left( a + 1 - \eta + \eta Q \right) \lambda + (1 - \eta) a.
\label{eq:char_poly}
\end{equation}
By the Jury stability criterion, all roots of $P(\lambda)$ lie strictly inside the open unit disk $\{|\lambda| < 1\}$ if and only if $|P(0)| < 1$, $P(1) > 0$, and $P(-1) > 0$. Here $|P(0)| = (1 - \eta)a < 1$ holds for all $a \in [0, 1)$ and $\eta \in (0, 1)$. Evaluating at $\lambda = 1$ and $\lambda = -1$:
\begin{equation}
P(1) = (1 - a - Q) \eta, \qquad P(-1) = (2 - \eta)(1 + a) + \eta Q.
\end{equation}
For the positive-feedback economy ($Q \ge 0$), $P(-1) > 0$ is strictly satisfied, so Schur stability is strictly equivalent to:
\begin{equation}
S \equiv a + Q < 1, \qquad \text{or equivalently} \quad \mathcal{L} \equiv \frac{Q}{1 - a} < 1,
\label{eq:stability_cond}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is total closed-loop persistence and $\mathcal{L}$ is the loop ratio. In the general signed-feedback economy ($Q \in \R$), the full Jury stability interval is $-\frac{(2-\eta)(1+a)}{\eta} < Q < 1 - a$, where the lower bound prevents period-doubling oscillatory explosion. For positive feedback with the stated interior learning and anchor conditions, the $S<1$ boundary is also sufficient under the following one-period lag. Signed-feedback stability must instead use that lagged model's full Jury inequalities. If execution features a discrete one-period lag ($x_{t+1} = a x_t + y_t + w_{t+1}, y_{t+1} = (1 - \eta) y_t + \eta Q x_t$), evaluating $P_{\text{lag}}(1) = (1 - a - Q)\eta > 0 \iff S \equiv a + Q < 1$, identically.

\subsection{Identification Boundaries: Merged Rate-Weight Equivalence and Separation Complexity}
A central question in algorithmic market surveillance is whether an econometrician observing public trade executions and prices can identify the individual algorithmic strategies active in the market.

\begin{theorem}[Merged Rate-Weight Equivalence and Minimax Separation Complexity]
\label{thm:m1_equivalence}\label{thm:m2_sample_complexity}
Let price impact $\kappa > 0$ and anchor $a \in [0, 1)$ be known. Use zero initial states and a common specified innovation/probe contract, and observe the modeled price state $x_t$. This contract is distinct from assuming that an anonymous market midpoint reveals fundamental pricing error.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Equivalence Class:} Two market economies $\mathcal{E}_A = \{(q_i^A, \eta_i^A)\}_{i=1}^{K_A}$ and $\mathcal{E}_B = \{(q_i^B, \eta_i^B)\}_{i=1}^{K_B}$ generate mathematically identical modeled price-state responses $x_t$ for all $t \ge 0$ under all public probing sequences $\{u_t^{\text{probe}}\}_{t \ge 0}$ if and only if their merged rate--weight measures coincide:
    \begin{equation}
    \sum_{i: \eta_i^A = \eta} q_i^A = \sum_{j: \eta_j^B = \eta} q_j^B \qquad \forall \eta \in (0, 1).
    \label{eq:merged_measure}
    \end{equation}
    The minimal state-space realization dimension is $1 + |\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{M})|$, where $|\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{M})|$ counts distinct rates with nonzero merged weights after signed cancellations, rather than every latent cohort. These are dynamic adaptation rates $\eta_i \in (0, 1)$ (any instantaneous cohort with $\eta = 1$ merges directly into the feedforward anchor $a$ without adding a dynamic pole).
    \item \textbf{Minimax Separation Complexity:} Consider testing whether a noiseless-state market ($w_t = 0$) consists of a single merged cohort with rate $\bar{\eta}$ versus two split cohorts with rates $\bar{\eta} \pm \delta$ and identical nonzero total feedback $Q$, splitting its weight symmetrically. Work on a fixed compact family whose transitions are uniformly Schur stable, with rates bounded away from zero and one and $\sigma_v>0$. Under deterministic probing with $\mathcal P_{\text{probe}}>0$ satisfying the power constraint $\frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} u_t^2 \le \mathcal{P}_{\text{probe}}$ and independent additive Gaussian observation noise $\tilde{x}_t = x_t + v_t$ with $v_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_v^2)$, any deterministic/open-loop probing sequence satisfying the stated average-power constraint distinguishing the two economies with worst-case probability of error at most $\alpha \in (0, 1/4)$ requires an observation budget $T$ satisfying:
    \begin{equation}
    T \ge \frac{C(\alpha, \sigma_v, \mathcal{P}_{\text{probe}},\mathcal F)}{\delta^4} = \Omega(\delta^{-4}),
    \label{eq:delta_fourth}
    \end{equation}
    where $C(\alpha, \sigma_v, \mathcal{P}_{\text{probe}},\mathcal F) > 0$ is a constant independent of $\delta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $r_i=1-\eta_i$. Zero-state cohort recursion gives
\[G(z)=\sum_i\frac{q_i\eta_i z}{z-r_i},\qquad H(z)=\frac{\kappa}{z-a-\kappa G(z)}.\]
Equality of every controlled price response is equality of $H$, which recovers $G$ because $a,\kappa$ are known. Its residue at a retained rate is $r_i\eta_i$ times that rate's merged weight. Distinct nonzero residues identify the merged measure by uniqueness of partial fractions. Conversely identical merged measures give identical $G,H$ and responses. After signed cancellations, let $D(z)$ be the product of the retained distinct $(z-r_i)$ factors. The numerator of $H$ is $\kappa D$ and its denominator has degree one more. At any retained $r_i$, the denominator is nonzero because its residue, $r_i$ and $\eta_i$ are nonzero. Thus no common factor remains and the minimal realization has the claimed degree. The cohort poles are poles of $G$ and zeros of $H$, not its closed-loop poles.

For the symmetric pair write $q=Q/\kappa$. Direct algebra gives
\[G_\delta-G_0=-\frac{qz(z-1)\delta^2}{(z-1+\bar\eta)((z-1+\bar\eta)^2-\delta^2)}.\]
All three denominator factors on $|z|=1$ have modulus at least the declared rate separation from the endpoints, say $\epsilon$. Compactness and uniform Schur stability give a finite bound $M=\sup_{\theta\in\mathcal F,|z|=1}|H_\theta(z)|$. The identity $H_\delta-H_0=H_\delta H_0(G_\delta-G_0)$ therefore gives induced $\ell_2$ gain at most $K\delta^2$, where $K=2|q|M^2/\epsilon^3$. Zero-padding the finite probe and retaining its observed response cannot increase that gain. Its Gaussian mean difference consequently has squared norm at most $K^2\delta^4\mathcal P_{probe}T$, and equal-noise KL is at most $K^2\delta^4\mathcal P_{probe}T/(2\sigma_v^2)$. Error at most $\alpha$ requires total variation at least $1-2\alpha$. Pinsker then yields
\[T\ge\frac{4\sigma_v^2(1-2\alpha)^2}{K^2\mathcal P_{probe}\delta^4}.\]
The constant depends on the specified family and resources, rather than being universal. The following theorem gives matching known-pair resource orders and explicit financial contracts.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the Identification Result]
The result concerns the stated model and observation contract. Cohorts sharing an exact rate enter the price response through their merged weight; this does not determine participant identities or wealth allocations. Nearby rates can be distinguishable with sufficient information. The $\delta^{-4}$ statement is an asymptotic lower bound on the declared uniformly stable family, with constants depending on that family; it does not establish a numerical ``160,000 times longer'' comparison to a unit rate change outside the local experiment. No matching general unknown-mixture upper bound or universal market-surveillance rate is asserted here.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional Quote and Allocation Cost of Ambiguity]
\label{thm:welfare_opacity}
Consider a fixed one-unit ticket with monetary adverse mark $Z_\theta\sim N(\mu_\theta,\sigma_\theta^2)$ and a CARA dealer of risk aversion $\gamma>0$. Let $c_\theta=\mu_\theta+\gamma\sigma_\theta^2/2$ and suppose $c_\theta\ge0$ with $\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}c_\theta<\infty$. Under competition imposing zero robust certainty equivalent, the informed quote is $s_1=c_{\theta_0}$ and the ambiguous quote is $s_2=\sup_\theta c_\theta$ for $\theta_0\in\Theta$. Thus $\Delta=s_2-s_1\ge0$, with strict inequality precisely when the worst relevant cost exceeds $c_{\theta_0}$. Cohort nonidentification alone does not establish that condition or proportionality to a parameter diameter.

Under an additional allocation-surplus criterion with inverse demand $v(V)=(V_0-V)/b$ and fixed marginal cost $c$, raising an interior quote from $s_0$ to $s_0+\Delta$ produces surplus difference
\[
\mathcal W_{\rm loss}=b(s_0-c)\Delta+\frac b2\Delta^2.
\]
The triangle formula requires the efficient baseline $s_0=c$. Treating a risk charge as a social cost requires a defended welfare criterion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Gaussian CARA certainty equivalent is $s-c_\theta$; its infimum is $s-\sup_\theta c_\theta$. The quote formulas follow from robust break-even. Equal costs throughout an observational equivalence class give zero premium. Integrating $v(V)-c$ between $V_0-b(s_0+\Delta)$ and $V_0-bs_0$ gives the stated surplus formula. Parameter coordinate rescaling can change a diameter while leaving costs and quotes unchanged.
\end{proof}

The set of relevant adverse-mark costs is an additional economic primitive; the aggregate cohort-equivalence theorem does not determine it. Likewise, a norm radius $B/\sup_\theta M_C(J_\theta)$ concerns a declared initial-state loss experiment and is not a quantity of posted depth. The empirical existence and magnitude of an ambiguity-induced liquidity or welfare effect remain open.


\subsection{Theorem 2: A Finite-Window Price-Surveillance Bound}
The following experiment isolates a statistical boundary under passive price observations. Its parameter $s$ is the scalar transition coefficient, distinct from the algebraic index $S=a+Q$ of a general multivariate market. Applying the result to a hidden multivariate market requires a valid observation-law reduction; no such reduction is inferred solely from a common unit-root boundary.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Window Abstention in a Scalar Gaussian Experiment]
\label{thm:m3_minimax_abstention}
Observe $\mathcal D_T=(x_1,\ldots,x_T)$ where $x_0=0$ and $x_t=sx_{t-1}+w_t$, $w_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)$ independently, $\sigma>0$, and $s\in(0,2)$. Let a possibly randomized rule $\Psi$ take values Stable, Unstable, or Abstain, with
\[
\sup_{s<1}P_s(\Psi=\mathrm{Unstable})\le\alpha,
\qquad \sup_{s\ge1}P_s(\Psi=\mathrm{Stable})\le\alpha,
\quad 0<\alpha<1/2.
\]
Choose $0<c_0<1$ with $c_0e^{c_0}/(2\sqrt2)<1-2\alpha$. For every integer $T\ge1$, on $\mathcal B_T=(1-c_0/T,1+c_0/T)$,
\begin{equation}
\inf_{s\in\mathcal B_T}P_s(\Psi=\mathrm{Abstain})
\ge1-2\alpha-\frac{c_0e^{c_0}}{2\sqrt2}>0.
\label{eq:abstain_bound}
\end{equation}
For every finite $T$, the minimax worst-case error of binary rules across $s\in(0,2)$ is at least $1/2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Pair any $s_0\in(1-c_0/T,1)$ with $s_1=s_0+c_0/T\in(1,1+c_0/T)$; reverse the pairing for a point on the other side, including $s_1=1$. Gaussian transition densities give
\[
D_{\rm KL}(P_{s_0}\|P_{s_1})
=\frac{(s_1-s_0)^2}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{t=1}^T E_{s_0}x_{t-1}^2.
\]
For either paired coefficient, $|s|\le1+c_0/T$ and
$E_sx_{t-1}^2=\sigma^2\sum_{j=0}^{t-2}s^{2j}\le\sigma^2(t-1)e^{2c_0}$.
Thus the KL divergence in either direction is at most $c_0^2e^{2c_0}/4$ and Pinsker's inequality bounds total variation by $c_0e^{c_0}/(2\sqrt2)$. Error control gives $P_{s_0}(\mathrm{Unstable})\le\alpha$ and $P_{s_0}(\mathrm{Stable})\le P_{s_1}(\mathrm{Stable})+\mathrm{TV}\le\alpha+\mathrm{TV}$, yielding the stated abstention bound. The reverse pairing is identical.

For binary rules, the Gaussian path densities are continuous in $s$ in total variation at each finite $T$. Let $s^-\uparrow1$ and $s^+\downarrow1$. Le Cam's two-point bound gives worst-case error at least $(1-\|P_{s^-}-P_{s^+}\|_{\rm TV})/2\to1/2$. This is a worst-case statement over arbitrarily close alternatives; it is not a 50\% error claim at every fixed separated parameter.
\end{proof}

The theorem forces a positive probability of withholding a classification under the specified uniform error guarantee. It does not require abstention on every sample path. Initial conditions, extra measurements, and observation noise can change the inference problem. In particular, a fully observed noiseless cohort recursion need not admit the common Lebesgue densities assumed by an informal reduction to a scalar price likelihood. The exact-disclosure supplement gives a constructive two-step identification result under an exact single-cohort state/demand contract. Thus observing aggregate demand as well as the true price state can change the statistical experiment and defeat a universal extension of the scalar bound.


Under uniform error control, the interval $\mathcal{B}_T = (1 - c_0/T, 1 + c_0/T)$ defines an asymptotic abstention layer where every error-controlled monitor must reserve judgment with positive probability, rather than forcing a binary classification whose worst-case minimax risk approaches $1/2$. This interval is illustrative for scalar Gaussian observations; richer state disclosures can overcome this boundary.\phantomsection\label{fig:abstention_zone}
\subsection{Financial Intermediary Solvency and Non-Normal Transient Amplification}
We now confront the fundamental divergence between price stability and financial intermediary solvency. In standard economic policy models, market stability is evaluated exclusively through the spectral radius $\rho(J) < 1$. We demonstrate that this criterion is dangerously incomplete.

\paragraph{The Non-Normal Market Jacobian.}
Consider the closed-loop Jacobian matrix $J$ from \eqref{eq:full_jacobian}. In financial markets, price impact and memory adaptation are asymmetric: order flow directly affects prices through contemporaneous impact $\kappa$, while price innovations affect memory through learning rate $\eta$. As a result, $J J^\top - J^\top J \neq 0$: the closed-loop market Jacobian $J$ is generically \textbf{non-normal} under asymmetric price-impact and learning parameters (except on a measure-zero algebraic subvariety). While normal matrices have orthogonal eigenvectors that diagonalize under unitary transformations, non-normal matrices have non-orthogonal (nearly parallel) eigenvectors. Consequently, even when all eigenvalues are strictly inside the unit circle ($|\lambda_i(J)| \le \rho(J) < 1$), matrix powers $J^t$ can undergo massive transient amplification before decaying asymptotically as $t \to \infty$ \citep{trefethen2005spectra}.

\paragraph{Optimizing Market Maker Problem and the Commitment Trap.}
To microfound the intermediary's financial vulnerability, we model a risk-averse, competitive market maker operating in continuous electronic order books \citep{ho1981optimal,grossman1988liquidity,avellaneda2008high,menkveld2013}. At each instant, the dealer quotes bid and ask prices (or an effective price-impact schedule $\kappa$ and half-spread $s_t$) to maximize expected inventory-adjusted fee revenue:
\begin{equation}
\max_{s_t, D_t} \mathbb{E}_t \left[ \sum_{k=0}^\infty \beta^k \left( s_{t+k} |q_{t+k}| - \frac{\phi}{2} I_{t+k}^2 \right) \right],
\label{eq:dealer_optimization}
\end{equation}
subject to inventory dynamics $I_{t+1} = I_t - q_t$, where $q_t$ is aggressive incoming order flow, $\phi > 0$ represents the inventory holding penalty, and $\beta \in (0, 1)$ is the discount factor. 

The market maker is subject to two institutional market design frictions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Regulatory Capital and Margin Requirements:} The dealer maintains equity capital $E_t$ subject to a maximum regulatory leverage constraint or margin haircut $\mu \in (0, 1)$ on inventory positions: $\mu |I_t| p_t \le E_t$, with equity evolving as $E_{t+1} = E_t + \Pi_t$, where $\Pi_t = s_t |q_t| + (I_t-q_t)(p_{t+1}-p_t)-f_t-g_t$ is trading P\&L. Positive $q_t$ denotes a dealer sale at the current midpoint plus signed spread; $f_t,g_t$ are monetary fees and funding charges. This follows from cash $C_{t+1}=C_t+q_tp_t+s_t|q_t|-f_t-g_t$ and inventory $I_{t+1}=I_t-q_t$, marked at $p_{t+1}$.
    \item \textbf{Execution Latency and Affirmative Quoting Commitments:} Registered market makers face regulatory affirmative obligations (spread caps $\bar{s}$ and minimum depth commitments $\underline{D}$ required by exchange rules) and high-frequency cancellation latency $\tau_{\text{cancel}} > 0$. Once resting limit orders are posted in the order book, they remain committed for the cancellation latency window.
\end{enumerate}

The dynamic objective requires a demand/fill law and a financially specified continuation state before its optimal depth can be solved. The initial available monetary capital and a declared model loss budget satisfy
\begin{equation}
A_0=E_0-\mu|I_0|p_0,\qquad 0<B\le A_0.
\label{eq:dealer_buffer}
\end{equation}
This choice alone does not ensure future margin feasibility, and dividing available buffer by an ad hoc fee-to-margin ratio $1+\phi/\mu$ lacks a unit-consistent shadow-cost derivation.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Capacity Choice and Depth-Dependent Stability]
\label{lem:endogenous_kappa}
For a static capacity illustration, fix net execution edge $r\ge0$, holding coefficient $h>0$, capital per quantity $m>0$, and bounds $L_{\min}\le L\le L_{\max}$. Maximize $rL-hL^2/2$ subject to $mL\le A$. If $\bar L=\min(L_{\max},A/m)<L_{\min}$, the quoting commitment is infeasible. Otherwise
\begin{equation}
L^*=\min\{\bar L,\max(L_{\min},r/h)\},\qquad
D^*=L^*/w,\quad \kappa^*=w/L^*\quad(L^*>0),
\label{eq:endogenous_kappa}
\end{equation}
where $w>0$ is a declared linear-book price width. Capital raises capacity weakly; nonbinding capital changes leave capacity unchanged. This is a solved one-period illustration with fixed primitives, rather than a solution of the preceding dynamic dealer objective.

For one cohort, fixed $\varphi>0$ and $0<\eta<1$, substituting $a=1-\kappa\varphi$ into the cohort transition gives Schur stability precisely when
\[
q<\varphi,\qquad
0<\kappa<\frac{4-2\eta}{(2-\eta)\varphi-\eta q}.
\]
Higher impact changes both persistence and coupling and does not necessarily increase transient magnification.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Strict concavity gives the unconstrained optimum $r/h$; projection onto the feasible interval gives $L^*$. The depth/impact relation is the declared clearing schedule. The three Jury quantities are $\kappa\eta(\varphi-q)$, $4-2\eta-\kappa[(2-\eta)\varphi-\eta q]$, and $\eta+(1-\eta)\kappa\varphi$, which yield the stated stability conditions.

For an exact counterexample, fix $\varphi=1,q=1/10,\eta=9/10$. At $\kappa=1/10$, the first column of $J$ is $(9/10,81/100)^\top$ and its squared norm is $14661/10000>1$. At $\kappa=1$, $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1/10\\0&19/100\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has squared operator norm $461/10000<1$. Both are stable. In the fixed all-state Euclidean experiment, the former has peak magnification greater than one, while the latter has peak one. Thus increasing impact can reduce transient risk in the stated model.
\end{proof}

\textbf{The Solvency Trap:} Why doesn't the optimizing dealer step away or widen spreads before non-normal amplification hits? Doing so violates exchange affirmative obligations (risking forfeiture of market-maker status and exchange fee rebates) or cedes order flow to Bertrand competitors. Furthermore, when an aggressive algorithmic feedback cascade arrives within the cancellation window $\tau_{\text{cancel}}$, resting passive limit orders are executed against at stale quotes before the dealer can cancel or widen spreads \citep{budish2015}. The following theorem concerns a declared model loss output $\ell_t=Cz_t$, $z_t=J^tz_0$, with $C$ in the same monetary budget units as $B$. Actual cash, post-trade inventory, prices, fees, funding and margin changes must justify that output mapping. It does not follow from setting inventory equal to a latent-state norm. The model loss-budget breach is $\sup_{t\ge0}|CJ^tz_0|>B$; operational solvency requires the additional accounting contract.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Loss Budget and Kreiss Bounds]
\label{thm:m5_kreiss_solvency}
Let $z_t = J^t z_0$ with $\rho(J) < 1$, balance-sheet buffer $B > 0$, and observation matrix $C$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Worst-Case Safe Radius:} For the declared criterion $\sup_t\|CJ^tz_0\|_2\le B$, the exact maximum initial-state radius is:
    \begin{equation}
    r^*_{\text{exact}} = \frac{B}{\sup_{t \ge 0} \|C J^t\|_2} \equiv \frac{B}{M_C},
    \label{eq:exact_safe_radius}
    \end{equation}
    where $M_C \equiv \sup_{t \ge 0} \|C J^t\|_2$ is the declared output gain. If $C=0$, the allowance is unbounded by this criterion; actual financial constraints still apply.
    \item \textbf{Kreiss Lower and Upper Bounds:} Under full state observation ($C = I$), the Kreiss constant of matrix $J$, defined on the resolvent outside the unit disk:
    \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{K}(J) \equiv \sup_{|z| > 1} (|z| - 1) \|(zI - J)^{-1}\|_2,
    \label{eq:kreiss_def}
    \end{equation}
    satisfies $\mathcal{K}(J) \le \sup_{t \ge 0} \|J^t\|_2 \le e \cdot n \cdot \mathcal{K}(J)$ for any $J \in \R^{n \times n}$. Consequently:
    \begin{equation}
    r_{\text{conservative}} \equiv \frac{B}{e \cdot n \cdot \mathcal{K}(J)} \le r^*_{\text{exact}} \le \frac{B}{\mathcal{K}(J)}.
    \label{eq:kreiss_bilateral_bound}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Complete Failure of Spectral Radius:} For any stable spectral radius $\rho \in (0, 1)$ and any arbitrarily small buffer $B > 0$, there exists a parameter family of non-normal matrices $J_\theta$ and an observation operator $C = [1, 0]$ exposed to the amplified coordinate such that $\rho(J_\theta) = \rho$ for all $\theta$, while the exact safe radius collapses to zero:
    \begin{equation}
    \lim_{\theta \to \infty} r^*_{\text{exact}}(J_\theta) = 0.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
Part~1 follows from the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality for operator norms: maximum loss across $\|z_0\|_2 \le r$ is $r \cdot \sup_{t \ge 0} \|C J^t\|_2 = r M_C$, yielding $r^*_{\text{exact}} = B/M_C$. Part~2 applies Spijker's (1991) optimal resolution of the Kreiss matrix conjecture, establishing $\mathcal{K}(J) \le \sup_{t \ge 0} \|J^t\|_2 \le e \cdot n \cdot \mathcal{K}(J)$ for $C=I$. Part~3 uses $J_\theta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\rho&\theta\\0&\rho\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Then $CJ_\theta=(\rho,\theta)$, so $M_C\ge\sqrt{\rho^2+\theta^2}\to\infty$. The family is defective; no eigenvector-basis argument is required. A complete proof is given in Internet Appendix~\ref{ia:kreiss_proof}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Output and disturbance geometry]
The theorem applies to a specified initial-state ball and output norm. State coordinate changes require a corresponding change in the disturbance set; arbitrary latent-state units cannot be interpreted as monetary losses. A physical execution-input experiment has a different gain, as Theorem~\ref{thm:resource_frontier} shows. The Kreiss lower radius concerns full-state observation. A small spectral radius alone supplies no universal bound, but non-normality alone does not establish severe monetary losses in a calibrated market.
\end{remark}

Internet Appendix~\ref{ia:pseudospectra} illustrates the same separation geometrically: for a constructed matrix with spectral radius $0.5$, pseudospectral contours cross the unit circle.

\subsection{Multi-Asset Generalization and Cross-Impact Kreiss Inflation}
Modern market makers operate simultaneously across multiple correlated instruments (e.g., BTC, ETH, and altcoins; or equity index components and ETFs). In multi-asset environments, cross-asset price impact creates additional non-normal coupling.

Let $\mathbf{x}_t \in \R^d$ denote the vector of pricing errors across $d$ traded assets, and let $\mathbf{u}_t \in \R^d$ denote aggregate order flows. The multi-asset price formation equation is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{x}_{t+1} = \mathbf{A} \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{K} \mathbf{u}_t + \mathbf{w}_{t+1},
\label{eq:multi_price}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A} = \diag(a_1, \dots, a_d) \in \R^{d \times d}$ collects direct price persistences, and $\mathbf{K} \in \R^{d \times d}$ is the cross-asset price impact matrix. For the following mathematical feedforward benchmark, allow $\mathbf K$ to be asymmetric. No causal instantaneous cross-asset impact matrix is calibrated here. A transactional interpretation additionally requires an execution-cost law consistent with no-price-manipulation restrictions; cross-impact symmetry under the relevant bounded-kernel assumptions is a substantive economic constraint \citep{schneiderlillo2017}.

Each asset is traded by adaptive momentum flow with learning rates $\mathbf{H} = \diag(\eta_1, \dots, \eta_d)$ and feedback gain $\mathbf{Q} \in \R^{d \times d}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{y}_{t+1} = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{H}) \mathbf{y}_t + \mathbf{H} \mathbf{x}_{t+1}, \qquad \mathbf{u}_t = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{y}_t.
\end{equation}
Stacking $\mathbf{z}_t = (\mathbf{x}_t^\top, \mathbf{y}_t^\top)^\top \in \R^{2d}$, the multi-asset closed-loop Jacobian is:
\begin{equation}
J_{\text{multi}} = \begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{A} & \mathbf{K} \mathbf{Q} \\
\mathbf{H} \mathbf{A} & \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{H} + \mathbf{H} \mathbf{K} \mathbf{Q}
\end{bmatrix} \in \R^{2d \times 2d}.
\label{eq:multi_jacobian}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Cross-Impact Kreiss Inflation and Safe Radius Bound in Directed Networks]
\label{prop:cross_kreiss}
Consider a multi-asset economy with directed block-triangular leader-satellite cross-impact ($K_{12} = 0, K_{21} > 0$), diagonal feedback matrices $\mathbf{Q} \ge 0$, anchor matrices $\mathbf{A} \ge 0$, adaptation rates $\mathbf{H} \in (0, 1)^d$, nonnegative impact matrix $\mathbf K\ge0$, stable diagonal blocks, and fixed output operator $C\ge0$. Let $J_{\text{diag}}$ denote the decoupled Jacobian where $K_{21} = 0$.
The directed coupling introduces off-diagonal block feedforward, inflating the Kreiss constant: $\mathcal{K}(J_{\text{multi}}) \ge \mathcal{K}(J_{\text{diag}})$. Consequently, the Kreiss-based conservative safe radius bound contracts: $r_{\text{conservative}}(J_{\text{multi}}) \equiv \frac{B}{e (2d) \mathcal{K}(J_{\text{multi}})} \le r_{\text{conservative}}(J_{\text{diag}})$.
For the exact safe shock radius $r^*_{\text{exact}}(J) \equiv B / M_C(J)$, because all constituent blocks of $J$ are entrywise non-negative in the momentum economy, matrix powers $J_{\text{multi}}^t$ are entrywise non-decreasing in $K_{21}$, yielding $M_C(J_{\text{multi}}) \ge M_C(J_{\text{diag}})$ and exact radius contraction $r^*_{\text{exact}}(J_{\text{multi}}) \le r^*_{\text{exact}}(J_{\text{diag}})$. A fixed-coordinate constructed coupling can increase peak gain. Its magnitude requires a declared output and shock set; no empirical percentage follows from this proposition.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Permute coordinates by asset; directed impact makes the full transition block triangular, preserving the stable diagonal spectra. Entrywise $0\le J_{diag}\le J_{multi}$ implies ordered nonnegative powers and ordered Euclidean operator norms after multiplication by fixed nonnegative $C$. For $r>1$, the nonnegative Neumann series $(rI-J)^{-1}=\sum_{t\ge0}J^t/r^{t+1}$ is likewise ordered. At any complex $z$ with $|z|=r$, its modulus is entrywise bounded by this real-radius resolvent, so the norm supremum on that circle occurs at $z=r$. Taking the Kreiss supremum proves its monotonicity. The statements concern fixed coordinates and nonnegative output; signed hedge outputs need separate analysis.
\end{proof}


Proposition~\ref{prop:cross_kreiss} concerns nonnegative downstream coupling under fixed shock/output geometry. Hedged or signed monetary outputs need separate treatment; they do not inherit a universal monotonic radius comparison, and this proof alone does not establish a fixed percentage radius contraction without an empirical multi-asset calibration (see Internet Appendix~\ref{ia:empirical_crisis_l2_8asset} for our 8-asset crisis network evaluation).

\subsection{Econometric Estimand and Moving-Average Filtering}
We compute reduced-form indices under explicit price/flow constructions. Their algebra is invariant to consistent constant price and flow rescaling, but this does not identify structural learning or physical execution impact.

A fundamental gap between theoretical models and high-frequency empirical execution concerns the definition of the state variable. In theoretical models (Section~\ref{sec:model}), $x_t \equiv p_t - p_t^*$ represents price displacement relative to an unobserved fundamental value $p_t^*$. In empirical limit order books, true fundamental value is unobservable, and econometricians routinely replace $p_t^*$ with a trailing price filter, such as an Exponential Moving Average (EMA).

Let $p_b$ denote a log recorded-price proxy sampled at discrete uniform time bars $b$ of length $\Delta$ (e.g., $\Delta = 10$~seconds). The trailing exponential moving-average anchor $m_b$ is defined by the recursive filter:
\begin{equation}
m_b = (1 - \alpha) m_{b-1} + \alpha p_b, \qquad \alpha \equiv \frac{2}{W/\Delta + 1},
\label{eq:ema_def}
\end{equation}
where the proxy is transaction close for Binance trade bars and midpoint for the recorder. Multiplication of log prices by 10,000 changes their units, not the stated constant-scaling invariants. Here $W$ is the smoothing window (e.g., $W = 300$~seconds, yielding $\alpha = 2/31 \approx 0.064516$). The empirical state variable is defined as the deviation from this trailing anchor:
\begin{equation}
x_b^{\text{EMA}} \equiv p_b - m_b = (1 - \alpha)(p_b - m_{b-1}) = \frac{1 - \alpha}{1 - (1 - \alpha) L} \Delta p_b.
\label{eq:x_ema_rep}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Mechanical Persistence under Filtered Random Walks]
\label{prop:mechanical_persistence}
Suppose the log mid-price $p_b$ follows an unpredictable martingale (random walk), such that $\mathbb{E}[p_{b+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_b] = p_b$. Then the moving-average deviation state $x_b^{\text{EMA}}$ mechanically follows an autoregressive AR(1) process:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[x_{b+1}^{\text{EMA}} \mid \mathcal{F}_b] = (1 - \alpha) x_b^{\text{EMA}},
\label{eq:mechanical_ar1}
\end{equation}
with mechanical persistence parameter $a_0 \equiv 1 - \alpha = \frac{29}{31} \approx 0.93548$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By definition, $x_{b+1}^{\text{EMA}} = p_{b+1} - m_{b+1} = (1 - \alpha)(p_{b+1} - m_b)$. Conditioning on information $\mathcal{F}_b$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[x_{b+1}^{\text{EMA}} \mid \mathcal{F}_b] = (1 - \alpha) \left( \mathbb{E}[p_{b+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_b] - m_b \right) = (1 - \alpha)(p_b - m_b) = (1 - \alpha) x_b^{\text{EMA}},
\end{equation}
which establishes \eqref{eq:mechanical_ar1} with mechanical persistence $a_0 = 1 - \alpha$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Economic Interpretation of Empirical Feedback $\hat Q$]
The EMA state measures deviation from a trailing price proxy rather than the unobserved fundamental value. In the corrected observed-flow panel, equal-weight pooled date means give $\hat Q=0.0003807$ and $\hat S=0.9203825$, hence the corresponding mean $\hat J_{11}=0.9200018$. The mechanical martingale anchor is $29/31\simeq0.9354839$ for this filter. A smaller fitted coefficient is a descriptive comparison; it does not prove market-level fundamental stability, identify bid--ask bounce or inventory replenishment as its cause, or authenticate a structural feedback mechanism.
\end{remark}

Let $f_t$ denote the sample-specific flow feature. Binance uses notional taker buy volume minus taker sell volume over the observed bar; recorder features and quote OFI use the separately declared constructions. The empirical bivariate vector autoregressive (VAR(1)) representation of joint price-flow transitions is:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} x_{t+1}^{\text{EMA}} \\ f_{t+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} J_{11} & J_{12} \\ J_{21} & J_{22} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_t^{\text{EMA}} \\ f_t \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e_{1, t+1} \\ e_{2, t+1} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \E[\mathbf{e}_t \mathbf{e}_t^\top] = \Sigma_e,
\label{eq:bivariate_var}
\end{equation}
Here all $J_{ij}$ are reduced-form coefficients for the constructed state and flow. They are not authenticated physical impact or latent learner parameters. When its denominators are nonzero, we extract dimensionless invariants $\hat{Q} \equiv \frac{\hat{J}_{12} \hat{J}_{21}}{1 - \hat{J}_{22}}$, $\hat{S} \equiv \hat{J}_{11} + \hat{Q}$, and $\hat{\mathcal{L}} \equiv \frac{\hat{Q}}{1 - \hat{J}_{11}}$, which are strictly scale-invariant.

\section{Regulatory Policy Implications and Conclusion}
\label{sec:policy_conclusion}

\subsection{Pre-Trade Controls and the Scope of the Proposed Bound}
Existing market-access rules already include pre-trade controls. SEC Rule 15c3-5 requires controls against orders exceeding appropriate credit/capital thresholds and erroneous orders; the SEC staff FAQ discusses their application to quotes and automated activity \citep{sec2010marketaccess,sec2017marketaccessfaq}. The present mathematical bound is a conditional design input. Its derivation does not establish regulatory compliance, improved welfare or an implemented exchange safety mechanism.

\subsection{An Explicit Finite-Window Quantity Gate}
An initial-state norm radius alone does not specify an executed-volume limit. Let $v_j$ be signed executed quantity, $H$ its input map and $Cz$ a financially specified monetary loss output:
\[
z_{j+1}=Jz_j+Hv_j,\qquad \ell_t=Cz_t,
\qquad \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}|v_j|\le V.
\]
Set $b_t=CJ^tz_0$ and $g_t=\max_{0\le k<t}|CJ^kH|$. Under an absolute-output budget, the sharp finite-window envelope is $|b_t|+L_{\rm other}+(g_t+c_{\rm exec})V$, where $L_{\rm other}$ is a declared monetary reserve and $c_{\rm exec}$ bounds monetary cost per executed quantity. Provided all background outputs, including $t=0$, are within budget, the cap is
\begin{equation}
\bar R=\frac{V^*}{\tau},\qquad
V^*=\min_{\substack{1\le t\le N\\g_t+c_{\rm exec}>0}}
\frac{B-L_{\rm other}-|b_t|}{g_t+c_{\rm exec}},
\label{eq:rate_gating_rule}
\end{equation}
for a window lasting $\tau$ seconds. This is quantity per second, not order-message count. Zero-gain times impose no quantity restriction if their background is feasible; an already exceeded background cannot be certified by setting a cap to zero. No Schur condition is needed for this finite-horizon calculation.

\begin{proof}
Superposition yields $\ell_t=b_t+\sum_{j<t}CJ^{t-1-j}Hv_j$. The maximum coefficient bounds the input term by $g_tV$; an adversarial single pulse at its maximizing lag attains the bound under the declared quantity ball. Costs add at most $c_{\rm exec}V$. Taking the minimum over every time gives the largest permitted cap for this linear criterion. The signed one-sided loss variant replaces $|b_t|$ with $b_t$.
\end{proof}

The Markov coefficients and this cap are unchanged by consistent invertible changes of state coordinates. In contrast, bounding the full state norm by the observable price coordinate alone ($\|z_0\|\le|x_0|$) is invalid when unobserved cohort components are nonzero, because the price component provides a lower, not upper, bound on the Euclidean state norm. Displayed top-five quantity and price impact do not determine $H$, and leveraged notional is not loss-absorbing capital.

Cohort nonidentification does not by itself make every financial functional unknown. For two reset models with identical price input responses and the same calibrated monetary price exposure, equality of Markov coefficients gives identical finite-window caps even when cohort counts differ. This is an input/output functional, not a first-use control result. Unreset hidden states, unknown exposure/input maps, nonlinear marks and changing positions require additional information.

In the one-asset positive-cohort model with $S=a+\kappa\sum_iq_i<1$, a reset price-only monetary output has executed-input peak $|\lambda|\kappa$, while its sustained-input gain is $|\lambda|\kappa/(1-S)$. Nonnegative row sums and the DC response prove this distinction. It requires the stated fixed output, input and model conditions; it does not include fundamental risk or accumulated costs. The all-state initial-shock norm and the physical execution-input risk can rank impact changes differently. The supplement gives an exact example in which the state norm falls while execution gain rises. These disturbance/output contracts must therefore be specified before interpreting a non-normality metric financially.

Each refreshed window must carry the actual state and updated capital, cost and uncertainty bounds. With $z_{t+1}=0.9z_t+v_t$ and budget one, two unit pulses in consecutive one-step windows produce output 1.9; carrying the first state reduces the second permitted pulse to 0.1. Thus window resets do not establish perpetual solvency. The detailed proof, coordinate/unit checks and exact failure examples are in the input-gate supplement. In Internet Appendix~\ref{ia:crash_free_guard} (Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_crash_free_guard}), we embed this state-carrying principle into an explicit event-sourced double-entry dealer ledger (cash $K_t$, inventory $I_t$, linear-book fills, spread revenue, costly hedge execution, and published January 2023 Binance USD$\circledS$-M Tier-2 maintenance margin) via the Real-Time Resolvent Solvency Guard ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$), establishing conditional bounded-shock forward solvency under evolving margin buffer $B_{\mathrm{rem},t} = E_t - \mathrm{MM}_t$ and worst-case norm-bounded or adversarially signed disturbances ($\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe}} = 4.03$~USDT at $t=0$, with explicit \texttt{NO\_SAFE\_ACTION} protective-halt engagement outside the domain) and achieving $0.0\%$ liquidation and $100.0\%$ model admission eligibility ($\alpha_t \ge 0.05$; $\bar{\alpha}_t = 32.2\%$--$35.5\%$ admitted depth in non-normal herding regimes) across $400$ stress episodes where eigenvalue-only guards ($\rho(J) \le 0.90$) suffer $100.0\%$ liquidation. Operational deployment in live venues still requires authenticated positions, marks, executions, fees/funding/margin rules and model/noise/input coverage; the empirical maintained-model rejections are not cured by this conditional formula.

\subsection{Market Design: Continuous Auctions vs.\ Frequent Batch Auctions}
Our findings also speak directly to the structural debate on market design initiated by \citet{budish2015}, who propose replacing continuous double auctions with Frequent Batch Auctions (FBAs) to eliminate latency arbitrage. Batch matching changes execution timing and incentives; a feedback model can also be posed across successive batches. This paper does not estimate its comparative economic effect. The finite-window quantity bound can be examined under either matching design when its input/output and accounting assumptions hold. Comparative welfare, execution feasibility and effectiveness across continuous and batch auctions remain unvalidated.

\subsection{Information Missing from Public Market Feeds}
\label{sec:missing_info}
Each result uses a declared information contract. Exact reset response functions determine a merged representation rather than participant identities. Noisy-price testing costs require the stated innovation law and exclusion of richer financial records. Exact marked equity and natural excitation supply constructive counterexamples to a universal capital barrier. The positive-class quantity certificate requires authenticated impact, margin and account assumptions; it does not require individual learning-rate identities once that model class is verified. Covariance rejection and measurement sensitivity prevent substituting public reduced-form coefficients for these operational conditions.

\subsection{Conclusion}
We establish a resource distinction within a precisely defined memory-resolution experiment: lifetime executed quantity of order $\delta^{-2}$ is necessary and sufficient for a known symmetric pair, while average power gives horizon order $\delta^{-4}$. Actual probe-trader execution cost gives matching capital order $\delta^{-4}$ for funded deterministic probes under the restricted noisy-price contract. Spectral and finite-horizon Rayleigh--H\"older duality (Theorems~\ref{thm:ia_exact_capital} and~\ref{thm:ia_nonconvex_duality}) certify an infinite-horizon lower bound of $61.44$~USDT at $\delta=0.05$, close the finite-horizon non-convex fee/margin duality gap to $0.98\%$ at $L=80$ (interval-certified lower bound $64.74$~USDT vs.\ feasible $65.38$~USDT), and establish a monotone best evaluated feasible account-capital upper bound of $64.56$~USDT (attained at active horizon $L=240$ and preserved for all $L \ge 240$ by zero-padding monotonicity $C^*(L+1) \le C^*(L)$), while moment-matched Le Cam two-point duality (Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_minimax_lecam}) proves that every measurable estimator (including biased, ridge, and James--Stein shrinkage estimators) requires $\Omega((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ reset probes for unknown two-cohort recovery ($5{,}928$ resets under the literal moment-matched pair and $6{,}173$ under closed-loop curvature refinement at $\Delta\eta=0.10$, paired with a matching $\mathcal{O}((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ cubic moment-inversion upper bound on known-midpoint families and a local perturbation/conditioning bound for parameter-blind Prony--Hankel recovery). A separate counterparty cash/margin contract gives a sharp quantity allowance, including the inventory created by the probe. Exact equity disclosure and natural excitation supply constructive counterexamples to a universal capital barrier. A fixed price-exposure output is a separate contract. More waiting alone cannot overcome a fixed lifetime quantity allowance; the result does not bound every tailored safe strategy. The empirical reconstruction shows strong timing/filter sensitivity and maintained-model rejection, rather than participant-level learning attribution. Existing simulations distinguish capacity events, defaults and effective dynamics, while the Real-Time Resolvent Solvency Guard ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_crash_free_guard}) demonstrates conditional bounded-shock forward solvency under explicit double-entry accounting across $400$ synthetic and historical-data-conditioned L2 stress episodes. Real-market financial calibration and independent specialist assessment remain necessary before publication and operational claims.

\section{Complete Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:m5_kreiss_solvency}}
\label{ia:kreiss_proof}

Throughout, $\|\cdot\|$ is the Euclidean vector norm and the induced operator (spectral) norm, and $z_t=J^tz_0$ with $\rho(J)<1$.

\paragraph{Preliminary: the gains are finite.}
By Gelfand's formula, $\|J^t\|^{1/t}\to\rho(J)<1$, so $\|J^t\|\to0$ and $M_I\equiv\sup_{t\ge0}\|J^t\|<\infty$. Hence $M_C=\sup_{t\ge0}\|CJ^t\|\le\|C\|M_I<\infty$. Taking $t=0$ gives $M_C\ge\|C\|$ and $M_I\ge\|I\|=1$.

\paragraph{Part 1 (exact safe radius).}
If $C=0$, every $z_0$ satisfies $\sup_t\|CJ^tz_0\|=0\le B$, so the criterion imposes no bound. Suppose $C\neq0$, so $M_C\ge\|C\|>0$.
\emph{Sufficiency.} If $\|z_0\|\le B/M_C$, then for every $t$,
\begin{equation}
\|CJ^tz_0\|\le\|CJ^t\|\,\|z_0\|\le M_C\cdot B/M_C=B .
\end{equation}
\emph{Necessity.} Let $r>B/M_C$. Then $B/r<M_C$, so by the definition of the supremum there is $t^*$ with $\|CJ^{t^*}\|>B/r$. The spectral norm of a finite matrix is attained: there is a unit vector $v$ with $\|CJ^{t^*}v\|=\|CJ^{t^*}\|$. The initial state $z_0=rv$ has $\|z_0\|=r$ and $\|CJ^{t^*}z_0\|=r\|CJ^{t^*}\|>B$, violating the criterion. Hence the set of radii $r$ for which every $\|z_0\|\le r$ satisfies the criterion is exactly $[0,B/M_C]$, and its maximum is $r^*_{\text{exact}}=B/M_C$.

\paragraph{Part 2 (Kreiss bounds), with $C=I$.}
\emph{Lower bound.} Fix $z\in\mathbb{C}$ with $|z|>1$. The partial sums $S_T=\sum_{t=0}^{T}z^{-(t+1)}J^t$ converge absolutely, because $\sum_t|z|^{-(t+1)}\|J^t\|\le M_I\sum_t|z|^{-(t+1)}=M_I/(|z|-1)<\infty$. Telescoping gives $(zI-J)S_T=I-z^{-(T+1)}J^{T+1}\to I$, so $zI-J$ is invertible and $(zI-J)^{-1}=\sum_{t\ge0}z^{-(t+1)}J^t$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
(|z|-1)\,\|(zI-J)^{-1}\|\le(|z|-1)\cdot\frac{M_I}{|z|-1}=M_I .
\end{equation}
Taking the supremum over $|z|>1$ yields $\mathcal{K}(J)\le M_I$.

\emph{Upper bound.} For any $n\times n$ complex matrix, \citet{spijker1991} proved $\sup_{t\ge0}\|J^t\|\le e\,n\,\mathcal{K}(J)$ in the spectral norm, with $\mathcal{K}$ defined exactly as in \eqref{eq:kreiss_def}; this resolved the LeVeque--Trefethen conjecture. Real matrices are a special case. We use this published theorem without reproving it.

\emph{Radius bounds.} Combining, $\mathcal{K}(J)\le M_I\le e\,n\,\mathcal{K}(J)$. Since $M_I\ge1$, $\mathcal{K}(J)\ge M_I/(e\,n)>0$, so all three quantities are positive and finite. Dividing $B$ through the chain and using Part~1 with $C=I$ ($r^*_{\text{exact}}=B/M_I$) gives \eqref{eq:kreiss_bilateral_bound}.

\paragraph{Part 3 (fixed spectrum, vanishing radius).}
Fix $\rho\in(0,1)$ and $B>0$. For $\theta>0$ let $J_\theta=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\rho&\theta\\0&\rho\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $C=(1,0)$. The characteristic polynomial is $(\lambda-\rho)^2$, so $\rho(J_\theta)=\rho$ for every $\theta$. The matrix is non-normal: $J_\theta J_\theta^\top-J_\theta^\top J_\theta=\operatorname{diag}(\theta^2,-\theta^2)\neq0$. Its powers are $J_\theta^t=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\rho^t&t\theta\rho^{t-1}\\0&\rho^t\end{smallmatrix}\right)\to0$, so $M_C(J_\theta)<\infty$ and Part~1 applies. Since $CJ_\theta=(\rho,\theta)$,
\begin{equation}
M_C(J_\theta)\ge\|CJ_\theta\|=\sqrt{\rho^2+\theta^2}\ge\theta,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
r^*_{\text{exact}}(J_\theta)=\frac{B}{M_C(J_\theta)}\le\frac{B}{\theta}\xrightarrow[\theta\to\infty]{}0 .
\end{equation}
This holds for every fixed $B>0$ while the spectral radius stays at $\rho$. \hfill$\square$

\section{Pseudospectral Contours and Non-Normal Resolvent Bounds}
\label{ia:pseudospectra}
To gain deeper visual and geometric intuition into why the spectral radius fails to predict dealer solvency, we examine the $\epsilon$-pseudospectrum of the closed-loop market Jacobian. For any $\epsilon > 0$, the $\epsilon$-pseudospectrum $\sigma_\epsilon(J)$ of matrix $J$ is defined as the set of complex numbers $z \in \mathbb{C}$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_\epsilon(J) \equiv \left\{ z \in \mathbb{C} : \|(zI - J)^{-1}\|_2 \ge \epsilon^{-1} \right\}.
\label{eq:pseudospectrum_def}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\sigma_\epsilon(J)$ represents the union of all eigenvalues of perturbed matrices $J + E$ across all perturbations with $\|E\|_2 \le \epsilon$.

As visualized in Figure~\ref{fig:pseudospectrum}, in a normal system, the pseudospectrum consists of concentric disks of radius $\epsilon$ centered at the eigenvalues: $\sigma_\epsilon(J) = \bigcup_i \{z : |z - \lambda_i| \le \epsilon\}$. However, for a constructed non-normal matrix, computed pseudospectral contours can stretch along the off-diagonal coupling direction, protruding significantly outside the unit circle $\{|z| = 1\}$ even when the spectral radius $\rho(J) = 0.50$ is well within the interior. 

The Kreiss constant $\mathcal{K}(J)$ measures the maximum ratio by which these pseudospectral contours protrude beyond the unit circle:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{K}(J) = \sup_{\epsilon > 0} \frac{\alpha_\epsilon(J) - 1}{\epsilon}, \qquad \alpha_\epsilon(J) \equiv \sup_{z \in \sigma_\epsilon(J)} |z|.
\end{equation}
Whenever $\mathcal{K}(J) \gg 1$, small high-frequency liquidity shocks are magnified by the non-normal resolvent, inducing large transient balance-sheet losses that exhaust dealer margin buffers before eigenvalue decay can assert itself.

\section{Computational and Empirical Foundations of the Financial Identification Frontier}
\label{ia:breakthrough_foundations}

This section provides the complete mathematical framework, computational algorithms, and empirical intervention methodology establishing the Financial Identification Frontier Theorem (FIFT) and resolving the theoretical-empirical gap identified in algorithmic market microstructure.

\subsection{Joint Financial Identification and the Tri-Region Partition}
\label{ia:frontier_program}

Consider an econometrician, exchange supervisor, or market participant seeking to distinguish between two adaptive candidate trading mechanisms parameterized by memory decay rates $\theta_0$ and $\theta_1 = \theta_0 + \delta$ over an observation horizon of $N$ discrete trading periods. The observer submits active probe orders $V = (V_1, V_2, \dots, V_N)^\top \in \R^N$, generating inventory state $I_t = \sum_{s=1}^t V_s$. 

\paragraph{Observation Contract.} We formalize the identification program under Contract $I_0$ (controlled active probing with additive Gaussian observation noise):
\begin{equation}
p_t = (h_i * V)_t + \epsilon_t, \qquad \epsilon_t \sim \text{i.i.d.} \; \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2),
\end{equation}
where $h_i$ is the closed-loop impulse response kernel under hypothesis $\mathcal{H}_i$. Because the observation noise is homoskedastic across hypotheses ($\Sigma_0 = \Sigma_1 = \sigma^2 I_N$), the Kullback--Leibler divergence is strictly quadratic in the signal difference:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{KL}(P_0^{\otimes N} \,\|\, P_1^{\otimes N}) = \frac{1}{2\sigma^2} \sum_{t=1}^N \left( (h_1 * V)_t - (h_0 * V)_t \right)^2.
\end{equation}
(In a general closed-loop state-space model with internal process innovations $w_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_w^2)$, the likelihood ratio is evaluated via the Kalman innovation sequence, where cross-hypothesis variance disparities add trace and log-determinant terms).

The observer seeks to minimize total financial capital expenditure $C(N, V)$---comprising price impact execution losses, per-trade exchange transaction fees $\phi$, and counterparty margin lockup $\mu p_0 |I_t|$:
\begin{equation}
\min_{N \in \mathbb{N}, \, V \in \R^N} \; C(N, V) \equiv \sum_{t=1}^N \left( \lambda V_t^2 + \phi |V_t| \right) + \max_{1 \le t \le N} \mu p_0 |I_t|,
\label{eq:joint_fift_program}
\end{equation}
subject to three operational and physical constraints:
\begin{align}
\text{(i) Separation:} \quad & \mathrm{KL}(P_0^{\otimes N} \,\|\, P_1^{\otimes N}) \ge \frac{1}{2} (z_\alpha + z_\beta)^2, \label{eq:stat_separation_constraint} \\
\text{(ii) Trade and Inventory Caps:} \quad & \max_{1 \le t \le N} |V_t| \le V_{\mathrm{eff}}, \quad \text{and} \quad \max_{1 \le t \le N} |I_t| \le I_{\max}, \label{eq:market_depth_constraint} \\
\text{(iii) Dealer Solvency Budget:} \quad & \max_{1 \le t \le N} \text{Loss}_t(V) \le B. \label{eq:dealer_solvency_constraint}
\end{align}
Here, $z_p = \Phi^{-1}(1 - p)$ is the standard Gaussian critical value ensuring hypothesis test error probabilities at most $(\alpha, \beta)$ (for equal targets $\alpha = \beta$, standardized separation requires $d' \ge 2 z_\alpha$, corresponding to boundary KL divergence $\frac{1}{2}(2 z_\alpha)^2 = 2 z_\alpha^2 \approx 5.41$), $V_{\mathrm{eff}} \equiv \min(V_{\max}, D_{\max}/\kappa)$ is the effective per-trade execution capacity combining resting depth $V_{\max}$ and price displacement limit $D_{\max}$, $I_{\max}$ is the regulatory inventory cap, and $B$ is the trader's cash and margin capital buffer.

\begin{proposition}[Tri-Region Partition, Observation Design, and Finite-Horizon Depth Floor]
\label{prop:tri_region_partition}
The parameter space $(\delta, B) \in (0, 1) \times \R_+$ partitions into three operational regimes under observation horizon $T$ per pulse:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Feasible Single-Pulse Region $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{feas}}$:} For any separation $\delta \ge \delta_{\min}(T)$ and capital buffer $B \ge C^*(\delta)$, the single-pulse probing program ($N_{\mathrm{pulse}} = 1$ trade, evaluated over transient price trajectory of length $T$) satisfies constraints \eqref{eq:stat_separation_constraint}--\eqref{eq:dealer_solvency_constraint} within the effective per-trade execution capacity ($A_{\mathrm{req}} \le V_{\mathrm{eff}}$). For Class~I hypothesis testing (simple rate shift $\eta_1 = \eta_0 + \delta$), $\Delta h_t = \Theta(\delta) \implies \mathrm{KL} = \Theta(\delta^2)$, yielding $C^*(\delta) = \Theta(\delta^{-2})$. For Class~II hypothesis testing (symmetric cohort splitting $\eta \in \{\eta_0 - \delta, \eta_0 + \delta\}$ with equal weights $w_1 = w_2 = q/2$), the first-order variation vanishes by symmetry ($\sum_k w_k \Delta \eta_k = 0$), so $\Delta h_t = \Theta(\delta^2) \implies \mathrm{KL} = \Theta(\delta^4)$, yielding:
\begin{equation}
C^*(\delta) = \Theta(\delta^{-4}).
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Sequential Bounded Probing Region $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{cost}}$:} For separations $\delta < \delta_{\min}(T)$, achieving statistical separation requires total executed volume $A_{\mathrm{req}}(\delta) > V_{\mathrm{eff}}$, which cannot be executed in a single period without violating resting liquidity or price displacement bounds. When individual trades are capped at $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$, each independent pulse cycle provides per-block Fisher information of order $\mathrm{KL}_1(T) = \frac{V_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 \|d_T\|_2^2}{2\sigma^2}$, where $d_T = (\Delta h_1, \dots, \Delta h_T)^\top$ is the transient signal difference kernel. Accumulating target divergence $\mathrm{KL} \ge \frac{1}{2}(z_\alpha + z_\beta)^2 \approx 5.4119$ requires:
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{pulses}}(T, \delta) = \left\lceil \frac{4\sigma^2 z_\alpha^2}{V_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 \|d_T\|_2^2} \right\rceil.
\end{equation}
Cumulative transaction fees and execution costs scale strictly as $C^*_{\mathrm{seq}}(\delta) = \Theta(\delta^{-4})$ (for Class~II) and $\Theta(\delta^{-2})$ (for Class~I), expanding the required observation horizon to $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$ or $\Theta(\delta^{-2})$. Under the simplified benchmark cost model ($C_{\mathrm{bench}} = \frac{1}{2}\kappa V^2 + \phi P^* |V| = 15.875$ USDT/pulse at $V_{\mathrm{eff}} = 6.25$ BTC), required capital at $\delta = 0.05$ is $4{,}305.25$ USDT under the restricted single-tap design ($T=3$, $N=271$ pulses), but collapses to $193.62$ USDT under the capacity-saturating multi-observation transient design ($T=20$, $N=12$ pulses, $V=6.25$ BTC), or $182.02$ USDT under the exact volume-optimized design ($N=12$, $V^* = 6.057$ BTC), reaching $177.75$ USDT at horizon $T=100$ ($N=11$ pulses; $175.35$ USDT at $V^*=6.207$ BTC). Under the physically executable round-trip unwind model ($C_{\mathrm{exec}} = \kappa V^2 + 2\phi P^* |V| = 31.75$ USDT/pulse), required capital is $8{,}607.38$ USDT ($T=3$), $384.12$ USDT ($T=20$ capacity-saturating), and $360.77$ USDT ($T=20$ volume-optimized). \emph{These figures are costs within the isolated-pulse design class, not minimum capital requirements.} The per-pulse formula charges initial margin once ($3.13$ USDT) and does not track the ledger; twelve buys that are never unwound carry $75$ BTC of inventory and $37.5$ USDT of margin. Section~\ref{ia:account_capital} replaces the formula by an explicit account ledger and shows that a non-pulse probe reaches the same $5\%$ error with about $65$ USDT of account capital.
\item \textbf{Not Feasible with Tested Isolated Pulses ($T \le 20$) ($\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{pulse-infeas}}$):} Identification within the isolated-pulse design class is infeasible if:
(a) The capital budget is insufficient to finance sequential impact and margin costs ($B < C^*_{\mathrm{seq}}(\delta)$); or
(b) The peak inventory required along the trajectory exceeds the regulatory limit ($\max_{1 \le t \le N} |I_t| > I_{\max}$); or
(c) The surveillance horizon is strictly bounded by a session deadline $T_{\mathrm{session}}$, in which case the exact non-asymptotic finite-session resolution floor $\delta_{\min}(T_{\mathrm{session}})$ under cycle length $T_{\mathrm{cycle}}$ is defined as the unique positive root $\delta > 0$ of the implicit signal-to-noise equation:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{eff}} \|d_{T_{\mathrm{cycle}}}(\delta)\|_2 = \frac{2 \sigma z_\alpha}{\sqrt{\lfloor T_{\mathrm{session}} / T_{\mathrm{cycle}} \rfloor}}.
\label{eq:delta_min_implicit_root}
\end{equation}
In the small-separation limit ($\delta \to 0$), substituting the normalized directional memory curvature vector $\tilde{d}_{T_{\mathrm{cycle}}} \equiv \lim_{\delta \to 0} \frac{d_{T_{\mathrm{cycle}}}(\delta)}{\delta^2}$ yields the explicit asymptotic fourth-root approximation:
\begin{align}
\delta_{\min}^{\mathrm{asymp}}(T_{\mathrm{session}}) &= \left(\frac{4 \sigma^2 z_\alpha^2}{V_{\mathrm{eff}}^2 \|\tilde{d}_{T_{\mathrm{cycle}}}\|_2^2 \lfloor T_{\mathrm{session}} / T_{\mathrm{cycle}} \rfloor}\right)^{1/4} \nonumber \\
&= \sqrt{\frac{2 \sigma z_\alpha}{V_{\mathrm{eff}} \|\tilde{d}_{T_{\mathrm{cycle}}}\|_2 \sqrt{\lfloor T_{\mathrm{session}} / T_{\mathrm{cycle}} \rfloor}}} = \Theta(T_{\mathrm{session}}^{-1/4}).
\label{eq:delta_min_def}
\end{align}
Under the calibrated Binance USD$\circledS$-M order book depth ($V_{\max} = 50.0$ BTC, $\kappa = 0.8, q = 0.2, \sigma = 0.01$) and price displacement limit $D_{\max} = 5.0$ USDT ($V_{\mathrm{eff}} = 6.25$ BTC), the operational single-pulse resolution floor evaluates to $\delta_{\min}(3) = 0.2028$ (where $\|\tilde{d}_3\|_2 = \kappa^2 q = 0.128$, so exact root \eqref{eq:delta_min_implicit_root} and asymptotic formula \eqref{eq:delta_min_def} coincide identically), shifting down to $\delta_{\min}(4) = 0.1376$, $\delta_{\min}(10) = 0.0973$, $\delta_{\min}(20) = 0.0914$, and $\delta_{\min}(100) = 0.0905$ under extended transient price observation. Crucially, as proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_exact_capital}, this infeasibility boundary applies strictly to the tested isolated-pulse protocol ($T \le 20$). The certified deterministic lower bound below which no flat trading sequence can achieve $5\%$ error is $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{det}}(0.05) = 61.44$ USDT ($61.14$ USDT fee-free spectral infimum plus $0.31$ USDT minimal fee bound; adaptive expected-loss lower bound $30.09$ USDT, Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_adaptive_capital}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Account-Level Capital and the Gap to the Lower Bound}
\label{ia:account_capital}

Theorem~2.9 is an order statement: required capital is $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$ and the isolated third-observation pulse attains that order. It does not identify the constant. At the calibrated parameters its explicit lower bound evaluates to $4.9\times10^{-4}$ USDT, roughly five orders of magnitude below any design we can execute, so the bound is correct but numerically uninformative. To measure what a trader needs under the calibrated linear-impact environment, \texttt{exp13\_account\_capital.py} runs each design through an explicit synthetic account ledger parameterized at reference price $P^* = 100$ USDT and Binance USD$\circledS$-M fee/margin rules (not live exchange executions): fills at the average linear-book price, taker fees $\phi=4$ bp, marks to the prevailing price, and $0.5\%$ maintenance margin. Initial capital is $C_0=\max_t(\text{margin}_t-\text{equity}_t)$, maximised over both hypotheses, and the testing error uses the full price path including unwind legs.

\input{../breakthrough_experiments/tables/table_account_capital.tex}

Three conclusions follow from Table~\ref{iatab:account_capital}. First, twelve buys that are never unwound fail the $5\%$ target (KL $4.95<5.41$) once the test uses the joint price path rather than adding twelve independent per-pulse divergences, and they end with $75$ BTC of inventory. Second, if every unwind leg is itself treated as an informative probe, five or six buy/sell round trips meet the target with $158$--$189$ USDT and zero terminal inventory, so the pulse-class figure is attainable in a real account. Third, the pulse class is not optimal: a generalized-eigenvector candidate probe over a 120-step window (maximizing KL divergence per unit fee-free executable round-trip cost, whose quadratic form is positive definite with minimum eigenvalue $0.12$, so no round-trip arbitrage exists) needs $65.05$ USDT ($64.88$ USDT at $L=160$, and $64.59$ USDT after fee-aware refinement), with maximum trade $1.25$ BTC and maximum price displacement $2.23$ USDT, well inside the $5$ USDT limit.

We formulate the terminal-cost and pathwise-account capital minimization problems over all feasible flat, fee-paying trading policies $v = (v_0, \dots, v_{L-1})^\top \in \R^L$ with zero terminal inventory ($\sum_{t=0}^{L-1} v_t = 0$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{v \in \R^L} \; & C_{\mathrm{term}}(v) \equiv \max_{i \in \{0,1\}} L_i(v) + \sum_{t=0}^{L-1} \phi P^* |v_t| + \max_{0 \le t \le L-1} \mu P^* |I_t|, \\
& \text{where } L_i(v) \equiv \sum_{t=0}^{L-1} v_t \left( p_{i,t} - \frac{1}{2}\kappa v_t - P^* \right) = \frac{1}{2} v^\top T_{h_i} v, \\
\text{s.t.} \; & \|(H_1 - H_0) v\|_2 \ge 2 \sigma z_{1-\alpha}, \quad \sum_{t=0}^{L-1} v_t = 0, \quad C_{\mathrm{account}}(v) \equiv \max_{i \in \{0,1\}} \max_{0 \le t \le L-1} \left( \mu P^* |I_t| - E^{\mathrm{mtm}}_{i,t}(v) \right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:general_capital_optimization}
\end{equation}
Here $L_i(v) = \frac{1}{2} v^\top T_{h_i} v$ is the exact Toeplitz round-trip execution loss including cross-period transient impact $v_t(p_{i,t} - P^* - \kappa v_t)$ (whereas a direct-only quadratic proxy $\sum_{t=0}^{L-1} \frac{1}{2}\kappa v_t^2$ omits cross-time price impact and overstates the two-trade round trip $v = (+1, -1)^\top$ at $0.8000$~USDT vs.\ actual Toeplitz loss $L_0(v) = 0.3488$~USDT and pathwise account capital $C_{\mathrm{account}}(v) = 0.4288$~USDT). Because any flat sequence $v \in \mathcal{V}_L$ can be extended to horizon $L+1$ by appending $v_L = 0$ without altering $L_i(v)$, $\|v\|_1$, $\max_t |I_t|$, or $\|(H_1 - H_0)v\|_2$, the true minimum capital over all admissible flat policies of length at most $L$ is non-increasing in $L$: $C^*(L+1) \le C^*(L)$. The evaluated sequences yield a monotone best feasible account-capital upper bound $\overline{C}_0^{\mathrm{feas}}(\le L)$ of $65.38$~USDT ($L=80$), $64.70$~USDT ($L=120$), $64.60$~USDT ($L=160$), and $64.56$~USDT ($L \in \{240, 320, 480\}$). While the infinite-horizon spectral lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{det}}(0.05) = 61.44$~USDT (Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_exact_capital}) leaves a $5.07\%$ gap relative to the best evaluated finite-horizon feasible probe ($64.56$~USDT, attained at active horizon $L=240$), Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_nonconvex_duality} below resolves the mathematical origin of this gap by deriving both a continuous-domain interval-certified mode-weighted Rayleigh--H\"older dual lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,cert}}(L)$ and a discrete $360 \times 2{,}000$ grid estimate $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{RH,grid}}(L)$ over the flat subspace $\mathcal{V}_L = \{v \in \mathbb{R}^L : \mathbf{1}^\top v = 0\}$. At $L=80$, $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,cert}}(80) = 64.7423$~USDT ($\hat{C}_{\mathrm{RH,grid}}(80) = 64.7431$~USDT) against feasible $\overline{C}_0^{\mathrm{feas}}(\le 80) = 65.38$~USDT, closing the finite-horizon non-convex duality gap to $0.98\%$ ($2.63\%$ at $L=120$).

The certified capital lower bounds, the information-theoretic value of adaptivity, and the finite-horizon Rayleigh--H\"older duality convergence are established by Theorems~\ref{thm:ia_exact_capital}, \ref{thm:ia_adaptive_capital}, and \ref{thm:ia_nonconvex_duality} and Tables~\ref{iatab:capital_frontier_exact} and \ref{iatab:nonconvex_duality} below.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral Dissipation Lower Bound and Feasible Identification Probes]
\label{thm:ia_exact_capital}
Consider the linear market microstructure with impact parameter $\kappa > 0$, anchor weight $a \in [0, 1)$, and symmetric adaptive cohorts separating by memory gap $\delta > 0$ under two hypotheses $H_0$ and $H_1$, with closed-loop transfer functions $H_0(z)$ and $H_1(z)$. Let $\widehat{H}_i(z) \equiv z H_i(z) = \sum_{j=0}^\infty h_{i,j} z^{-j}$ (with $h_{i,0} = \kappa$) denote the impact-indexed closed-loop transfer function mapping input order flow to first-affected price displacements. Let $\Delta \widehat{H}(e^{i\omega}) \equiv \widehat{H}_1(e^{i\omega}) - \widehat{H}_0(e^{i\omega}) = e^{i\omega} (H_1(e^{i\omega}) - H_0(e^{i\omega}))$, satisfying $|\Delta \widehat{H}(e^{i\omega})| = |\Delta H(e^{i\omega})|$. Define the real spectral dissipation kernel:
\begin{equation}
g_i(\omega) \equiv \operatorname{Re} \widehat{H}_i(e^{i\omega}) - \frac{\kappa}{2} > 0 \qquad \forall \omega \in [-\pi, \pi].
\label{eq:ia_spectral_kernel}
\end{equation}
(Strict positivity $g_i(\omega) > 0$ holds because $h_{i,0} = \kappa$ and $h_{i,j} = \sum_\ell w_\ell \lambda_\ell^j$ with $w_\ell \ge 0, \lambda_\ell \in [0, 1)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:positive_price_kernel}, ensuring $\operatorname{Re}\widehat{H}_i(e^{i\omega}) - \frac{\kappa}{2} = \frac{1}{2}\sum_\ell w_\ell \frac{1-\lambda_\ell^2}{1-2\lambda_\ell\cos\omega+\lambda_\ell^2} > 0$; in contrast, unshifted $H_0(z) = z^{-1}\widehat{H}_0(z)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re} H_0(-1) - \frac{\kappa}{2} = -0.9195 < 0$ at $\omega = \pi$, failing positivity without impact indexing.)
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Toeplitz Energy Identity:} For any finite-horizon flat round-trip probe $v \in \mathbb{R}^{L+1}$ ($\sum_{t=0}^L v_t = 0$), the fee-free trading loss under hypothesis $H_i$ is identical to the Toeplitz quadratic form:
\begin{equation}
L_i(v) \equiv \sum_{t=0}^L v_t \left( p_t - \frac{\kappa}{2} v_t - P^* \right) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi g_i(\omega) \left| V(e^{i\omega}) \right|^2 d\omega,
\end{equation}
where $V(e^{i\omega}) = \sum_{t=0}^L v_t e^{-i\omega t}$.
\item \textbf{Certified Deterministic Lower Bound:} Under iid Gaussian innovation noise with variance $\sigma^2$, the output price divergence satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\|dx\|_2^2 \equiv \|(H_1 - H_0) v\|_2^2 \le \left( \sup_{\omega \in [-\pi, \pi]} \frac{|\Delta \widehat{H}(e^{i\omega})|^2}{g_i(\omega)} \right) L_i(v).
\end{equation}
Consequently, every deterministic round-trip probe guaranteeing worst-case classification error $\alpha \le 0.05$ under Neyman--Pearson testing ($\|dx\|_2 \ge 2\sigma z_{1-\alpha}$) requires execution loss bounded below by:
\begin{equation}
L_i(v) \ge 4 \sigma^2 z_{1-\alpha}^2 \inf_{\omega \in [-\pi, \pi]} \frac{g_i(\omega)}{|\Delta \widehat{H}(e^{i\omega})|^2} \equiv C^*_{\mathrm{det,quad}}(\delta).
\end{equation}
At $\delta = 0.05$, $C^*_{\mathrm{det,quad}}(0.05) = 61.14$ USDT (with minimizing angular frequency $\omega^* = 0.2526$ rad). Adding the minimal unavoidable taker fee under effective capacity $V_{\mathrm{eff}}$ ($\phi P^* \|v\|_2^2 / V_{\mathrm{eff}} \ge 0.3056$ USDT) establishes the certified necessary lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{det}}(0.05) \ge 61.44$ USDT.
\item \textbf{Feasible Upper Bound and Bounded Gap:} A concrete 160-step executable trading sequence $v^{(L)}$ ($L=160$, respecting $|v_t| \le 6.25$ BTC, $D_{\max} \le 5.0$ USDT, $0.5\%$ maintenance margin, and $4$ bp taker fees) achieves target error $\le 0.05$ with initial capital $C_0 = 64.59$ USDT ($65.05$ USDT at $L=120$; $64.56$ USDT under $L_1$-regularized optimization at active horizon $L=240$, preserved for all $L \ge 240$ by zero-padding). Across evaluated memory separations $\delta \in [0.02, 0.15]$, the quadratic lower bound exhibits asymptotic scaling $\delta^4 \times C^*_{\mathrm{det,quad}}(\delta) = (3.82 \pm 0.15) \times 10^{-4}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Information-Theoretic Lower Bound for Adaptive and Sequential Probing]
\label{thm:ia_adaptive_capital}
Let $(v_t)_{t=1}^\tau$ be any causal, non-anticipating sequential probing policy adapted to the price filtration $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}^p$, with stopping time $\tau$ almost surely finite under $H_0$ and $H_1$, satisfying terminal flatness $\sum_{t=1}^\tau v_t = 0$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Information-Theoretic Expected Loss Lower Bound:} By Wald's identity and the data processing inequality for Kullback--Leibler divergence:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_0[L_0(v)] \ge 2 \sigma^2 \operatorname{kl}(\alpha, 1-\beta) \inf_{\omega \in [-\pi, \pi]} \frac{g_0(\omega)}{|\Delta \widehat{H}(e^{i\omega})|^2} \equiv \mathbb{E}_0[C^*_{\mathrm{adapt,quad}}(\delta)],
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{kl}(\alpha, 1-\beta) = \alpha \log \frac{\alpha}{1-\beta} + (1-\alpha) \log \frac{1-\alpha}{\beta}$. At $\delta = 0.05$, $\mathbb{E}_0[C^*_{\mathrm{adapt,quad}}] = 29.94$ USDT, and adding minimal fees yields necessary expected loss bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{adapt}}(0.05) \ge 30.09$ USDT.
\item \textbf{Dynamic Information-Energy Savings Ceiling (Attained Fee-Free Quadratic Infimum vs.\ Fee-Constrained Feasible Upper Bound):} Bounding a ratio $A / D$ from below requires pairing a lower bound $A \ge \underline{A}$ on the adaptive numerator with an \emph{upper bound} $D \le \overline{D}$ (or attained infimum) on the deterministic denominator. We therefore separate two exact regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Unconstrained Fee-Free Quadratic Loss $L_0(v)$:} At any finite horizon $L \ge 2$, the deterministic quadratic infimum $\inf_{v \in \mathcal{V}_L} L_0(v) = \underline{C}_{\mathrm{quad},0}(L) = \frac{4\sigma^2 z_{1-\alpha}^2}{\lambda_{\max}(M, G_0)}$ is \textbf{attained} by the top generalized eigenvector $v^*_{\mathrm{eig}}(L) = E u_{\max}$, and as $L \to \infty$, $\inf_{L \ge 2} \underline{C}_{\mathrm{quad},0}(L) = C^*_{\mathrm{det,quad}}(\delta) = 61.14$~USDT ($61.19$~USDT at $L=480$). Because the deterministic denominator equals $C^*_{\mathrm{det,quad}}(\delta)$, dividing the adaptive lower bound $\mathbb{E}_0[L_0(v^\pi)] \ge \mathbb{E}_0[C^*_{\mathrm{adapt,quad}}(\delta)]$ by the attained deterministic quadratic infimum yields:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\inf_{\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{adapt}}} \mathbb{E}_0[L_0(v^\pi)]}{\inf_{v \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{det}}} L_0(v)} = \frac{\inf_{\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{adapt}}} \mathbb{E}_0[L_0(v^\pi)]}{C^*_{\mathrm{det,quad}}(\delta)} \ge \frac{\operatorname{kl}(\alpha, 1-\beta)}{2 z_{1-\alpha}^2} = \frac{\operatorname{kl}(0.05, 0.95)}{2 \times (1.6449)^2} \approx 0.4897,
\label{eq:ia_adaptive_inequality}
\end{equation}
establishing that dynamic adaptation can reduce expected fee-free quadratic loss by at most $1 - 0.4897 = 51.03\%$ in the infinite-horizon limit ($29.94 / 61.19 = 0.4893$, or at most $51.07\%$ against the finite-horizon eigenvector at $L=480$), and cannot alter the fundamental $\Theta(\delta^{-4})$ scaling.
\item \emph{Fee-and-Margin Constrained Account Problem:} When $L_1$ taker fees and maintenance margin are included, the exact deterministic minimum $C^*_{\mathrm{acc}}(\delta) \in [61.44, 64.56]$~USDT is bracketed rather than known in closed form; pairing the adaptive lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{adapt}}(0.05) = 30.09$~USDT with the certified feasible deterministic upper bound $\overline{C}_0^{\mathrm{feas}}(\le 240) = 64.56$~USDT ($64.50$~USDT terminal loss plus fee) yields a certified ratio $\ge 30.09 / 64.56 \approx 0.4661$ (at most $53.39\%$ reduction). Inequality~\eqref{eq:ia_adaptive_inequality} bounds expected loss under the martingale stopping model; it does not guarantee a saving in initial pathwise solvency capital.
\end{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sequential SPRT Performance and Solvency Capital:} Across $N=20{,}000$ sequential SPRT Monte Carlo trials at $\delta = 0.05$, empirical mean loss is $45.96$ USDT under $H_0$ ($45.58$ USDT under $H_1$), strictly respecting the theoretical expected-loss bounds ($30.09 \le 45.96 \le 61.44$ USDT). However, because stopping times $\tau$ vary across sample paths, realized losses and pathwise required account capital $C_0 = \max_{t \le \tau}(\text{margin}_t - \text{equity}_t)$ exhibit substantial tail risk: under $H_0$, the 95th percentile loss is $105.99$ USDT ($p95$ account capital $C_0 = 106.53$ USDT), the 99th percentile loss is $155.46$ USDT ($p99$ account capital $C_0 = 155.99$ USDT), and the maximum simulated loss is $268.52$ USDT (max $C_0 = 269.05$ USDT). Initial capital required for pathwise solvency at $99\%$ confidence is $B_{0.01} = 155.99$ USDT ($106.53$ USDT at $95\%$ confidence); funding an adaptive trader with only the mean loss ($B = 45.96$ USDT) results in a $37.73\%$ insolvency rate before the test terminates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Horizon Rayleigh--H\"older Duality and Multi-Horizon Capital Convergence]
\label{thm:ia_nonconvex_duality}
For any finite round-trip horizon $L \ge 2$ ($L+1$ active trading steps $t=0, \dots, L$ followed by passive tail relaxation), let $E \in \mathbb{R}^{(L+1) \times L}$ denote the zero-sum first-difference basis ($E_{t,t}=1$, $E_{t+1,t}=-1$) spanning $\mathcal{V}_L = \{v = E u : u \in \mathbb{R}^L, \mathbf{1}^\top v = 0\}$, and let $G_i = E^\top Q_i E \succ 0$ ($i \in \{0, 1\}$) and $M = E^\top A_{\mathrm{diff}} E \succeq 0$ denote the reduced quadratic execution-loss and Fisher-information matrices. Let $\{(\lambda_j, e_j)\}_{j=1}^L$ be the $G_i$-orthonormal generalized eigenpairs ($M e_j = \lambda_j G_i e_j$, $e_j^\top G_i e_k = \delta_{jk}$) ordered as $0 \le \lambda_1 \le \cdots \le \lambda_{L-2} < \lambda_{L-1} \le \lambda_L$, and define $c_{\mathrm{req}} \equiv 4\sigma^2 z_{1-\alpha}^2$ and $c_{\mathrm{fee}} \equiv \phi P^*$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{2D Spectral-Residual Decomposition and Continuous Interval-Arithmetic Enclosure (\nolinkurl{exp16_nonconvex_duality_closing.py}):} Every flat probe $v = E u \in \mathcal{V}_L$ satisfying $u^\top M u \ge c_{\mathrm{req}}$ decomposes uniquely into its top-2 spectral doublet component and orthogonal residual:
\begin{equation}
u = r d(\theta) + u_\perp, \qquad d(\theta) = \cos\theta\, e_{L-1} + \sin\theta\, e_L \;\;(\theta \in [0, \pi)), \qquad u_\perp = \sum_{j=1}^{L-2} c_j e_j.
\end{equation}
Define the directional Rayleigh quotient $\lambda(\theta) \equiv \lambda_{L-1}\cos^2\theta + \lambda_L\sin^2\theta \in [\lambda_{L-1}, \lambda_L]$ and baseline quadratic loss $L_0(\theta) \equiv c_{\mathrm{req}}/\lambda(\theta)$. Because every residual mode satisfies $\lambda_j \le \lambda_{L-2} < \lambda_{L-1} \le \lambda(\theta)$, the excess quadratic loss $\Delta_2 \equiv u^\top G_i u - L_0(\theta) = r^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{L-2} c_j^2 - L_0(\theta)$ obeys:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta_2 &\ge \sum_{j=1}^{L-2} \left(1 - \frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda(\theta)}\right) c_j^2 \ge 0, \\
\|u_\perp\|_{G_i}^2 &\le \frac{\lambda(\theta)}{\lambda(\theta) - \lambda_{L-2}}\Delta_2, \qquad r^2 \ge L_0(\theta)\bigl(1 - \gamma(\theta)\Delta_2\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma(\theta) \equiv \frac{\lambda_{L-2}\lambda(\theta)}{(\lambda(\theta)-\lambda_{L-2})c_{\mathrm{req}}}$. For any dual sign vector $s \in \{-1, 0, 1\}^{L+1}$ with $\tilde{g}(s) \equiv c_{\mathrm{fee}} V^\top E^\top s$ ($V = [e_1, \dots, e_L]$), Hölder's inequality $c_{\mathrm{fee}}\|E u\|_1 \ge c_{\mathrm{fee}} s^\top E(r d(\theta) + u_\perp)$ and Cauchy--Schwarz yield:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_i(v) + c_{\mathrm{fee}}\|v\|_1 &\ge L_0(\theta) + \Delta_2 \\
&\quad + \left[ c_{\mathrm{fee}}\bigl(s^\top E d(\theta)\bigr)\sqrt{L_0(\theta)}\sqrt{\max(0, 1 - \gamma(\theta)\Delta_2)} - W_\perp(\theta; s)\sqrt{\Delta_2} \right]_+,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ia_rh_2d_surface}
\end{equation}
where $W_\perp(\theta; s) \equiv \bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{L-2} \frac{\tilde{g}_j(s)^2}{1 - \lambda_j/\lambda(\theta)}\bigr)^{1/2}$. Partition $[0, \pi)$ into $n_\theta = 1{,}800$ sub-intervals $I_m = [\theta_a, \theta_b]$ ($h_\theta = \frac{\pi}{2 n_\theta}$) with midpoint sign $s_m = \operatorname{sign}(E d(\theta_{\mathrm{mid}}))$. Because $f_m(\theta) \equiv s_m^\top E d(\theta)$ satisfies $f_m''(\theta) = -f_m(\theta)$, $f_m$ is concave wherever non-negative on $I_m$, attaining its exact minimum $\underline{\ell}_m = \max\bigl(0,\, \cos(h_\theta)\|E d(\theta_{\mathrm{mid}})\|_1 - \sin(h_\theta)|s_m^\top E d'(\theta_{\mathrm{mid}})|\bigr)$ at an endpoint. Moreover, since $c_{\mathrm{fee}}\|v\|_1 \ge 0$, any $\Delta_2 \ge \Delta_{2,\max}(I_m) \equiv \overline{C}_{\mathrm{ref}} - L_{0,\min}(I_m)$ satisfies $L_i(v) + c_{\mathrm{fee}}\|v\|_1 \ge \overline{C}_{\mathrm{ref}}$. Partitioning $[0, \Delta_{2,\max}(I_m)]$ into $n_{\Delta_2} = 4{,}000$ sub-intervals $J_{m,k} = [a_k, b_k]$ ($7.2 \times 10^6$ continuous 2D boxes) and evaluating the monotone box lower bound:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\underline{C}_{m,k} &= L_{0,\min}(I_m) + a_k + F_{\mathrm{dom},\min}(I_m)\sqrt{\max(0, 1 - \gamma_{\max}(I_m) b_k)} \\
&\quad - W_{\perp,\max}(I_m)\sqrt{b_k} - \delta_{\mathrm{fp}}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
($\delta_{\mathrm{fp}} = 10^{-9}$ IEEE-754 safety margin) certifies the global lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,cert}}(L) = \max\!\bigl(\underline{C}_{L_2}(L),\, \min_{m,k} \underline{C}_{m,k}\bigr)$ across all $v \in \mathcal{V}_L$ (with enclosure gap $\hat{C}_{\mathrm{RH,grid}}(L) - \underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,cert}}(L) < 10^{-3}$~USDT). Crucially, eliminating the $\Delta_2$ grid altogether via the exact concave-secant envelope $\sqrt{1 - \gamma\Delta_2} \ge 1 - \Delta_2/\Delta_{2,\max}$ and closed-form quadratic-square-root minimum $\min_{\Delta_2 \ge 0}(\alpha \Delta_2 - W_{\perp,\max}\sqrt{\Delta_2}) = -W_{\perp,\max}^2/(4\alpha)$ (kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{analytical_quadratic_linear_envelope_bound}) yields the closed-form $\Delta_2$-free lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,semi}}(L)$, and further replacing the $\theta$ partition with the exact $2\times 2$ singular-value minimum $\sigma_{\min}(A_2)$ of $A_2 = [E e_{L-1}, E e_L]$ and smooth dual multiplier $s(\theta) = E d(\theta)/\max_t \|(A_2)_{t,:}\|_2$ (kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{rayleigh_holder_closed_form_analytic_lower_bound}) yields the purely analytical 0-grid lower bound $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,0grid}}(L)$:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Tight Finite-Horizon Duality Certificate (Analytical 0-Grid, Full-Domain 1,800-Interval Outward-Rounded \texttt{Decimal} Audit, Weyl Eigenvalue Enclosure, and Double-Entry Ledger Reconciliation):} At $\delta = 0.05$ and $L=80$, the purely analytical 0-grid formula gives $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,0grid}}(80) = 63.9206$~USDT ($63.48$~USDT at $L=120$ with $0$ grid points in both $\theta$ and $\Delta_2$), the closed-form $\Delta_2$-free bound gives $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,semi}}(80) = 64.7383$~USDT ($0$ grid points in $\Delta_2$, within $\$0.004$ of the 2D box audit), and the 2D box audit gives $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,cert}}(80) = 64.7423$~USDT ($\hat{C}_{\mathrm{RH,grid}}(80) = 64.7431$~USDT; worst box $m^* = 955, k^* = 89$, $\theta \in [1.6668, 1.6685]$, $\Delta_2 \in [0.0616, 0.0623]$) against feasible $\overline{C}_0^{\mathrm{feas}}(\le 80) = 65.38$~USDT (certified finite-horizon duality gap $0.98\%$), and $\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,cert}}(120) = 63.0368$~USDT ($\underline{C}_{\mathrm{RH,semi}}(120) = 63.0319$~USDT) against feasible $\overline{C}_0^{\mathrm{feas}}(\le 120) = 64.70$~USDT (gap $2.63\%$). \label{ia:unknown_mixture_inversion}

Theorem~\ref{thm:m1_equivalence} establishes that algorithmic cohorts sharing an adaptation rate merge into an observationally equivalent composite weight. A critical identification challenge in general unknown-cohort markets is separating the latent memory poles $r_i = 1 - \eta_i$ from the closed-loop system dynamics without conditioning on the true cohort count $K$.

Consider the closed-loop pricing equation with $K$ hidden adaptive cohorts expressed in price displacement $x_t \equiv p_t - P^*$:
\begin{equation}
x_t = a x_{t-1} + \kappa \sum_{i=1}^K q_i y_{i,t-1} + \kappa v_t, \qquad y_{i,t} = (1 - \eta_i) y_{i,t-1} + \eta_i x_t.
\label{eq:closed_loop_pricing_displacement}
\end{equation}
In the frequency domain, the transfer function from probe input $v_t$ to price displacement $x_t$ is:
\begin{equation}
H(z) = \frac{\kappa}{z - a - \kappa G(z)}, \qquad G(z) = \sum_{i=1}^K \frac{q_i \eta_i z}{z - (1 - \eta_i)}.
\label{eq:closed_loop_H_transfer}
\end{equation}
As proved in Theorem~\ref{thm:m1_equivalence}, the latent memory rates $r_i = 1 - \eta_i$ are the poles of the open-loop feedback kernel $G(z)$. Because $H(z) = \frac{\kappa D_G(z)}{(z - a)D_G(z) - \kappa N_G(z)}$, the roots of $D_G(z) = 0$ (the true memory poles) are \emph{transmission zeros} of the closed-loop transfer function $H(z)$, while the poles of $H(z)$ represent closed-loop system eigenvalues.

Consequently, extracting poles directly from the closed-loop price impulse response $h_t$ via naive Matrix Pencil or Prony methods targets the closed-loop eigenvalues ($\lambda_1 = 0.4256, \lambda_2 = 0.8224$ for $K=1, \eta = 0.30$), failing to identify the hidden memory pole $r = 1 - \eta = 0.70$.

To recover the true structural parameters without prior knowledge of $K$, we implement a blind closed-loop inversion algorithm:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Impulse Decoupling:} Extract the strictly proper transfer component $g_t \equiv h_{t+1} - a h_t$ from the empirical price response.
\item \textbf{Minimal State-Space Realization:} Form the block Hankel matrix $\mathcal{H}_{i,j} = g_{i+j-1}$ and execute Kung's Eigenrealization Algorithm (ERA) via truncated singular value decomposition to obtain a balanced realization $(A, B, C, D)$.
\item \textbf{Transmission Zero Inversion:} Compute the transmission zeros of $(A, B, C, D)$, which invert the feedback loop under known $(\kappa, a)$ and isolate the roots of $D_G(z) = 0$.
\item \textbf{Direct Closed-Loop Feedback Inversion:} Given recovered memory rates $\hat{\eta}_k$, invert the physical pricing relation $u_t = \frac{x_t - a x_{t-1}}{\kappa} = \sum_{k=1}^K w_k y_{k,t-1}$, where internal memory filters evolve as $y_{k,t} = (1 - \hat{\eta}_k) y_{k,t-1} + \hat{\eta}_k x_t$. Projecting unobserved algorithmic order flow $u_t$ onto the reconstructed filter basis $\{y_{k,t-1}\}$ resolves the structural cohort weights $w_k$ without confounding from closed-loop pole shifts.
\end{enumerate}

Evaluating this estimator on synthetic markets gives exact recovery only for a single cohort or noiseless, well-separated cohorts:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Noiseless Benchmark ($K=1, \eta = 0.30, q = 0.20$):} The estimator blindly selects $\hat{K} = 1$, identifies the true memory pole $\hat{r} = 0.7000$ (numerical error $< 10^{-6}$), and recovers feedback weight $\hat{w} = 0.200000$ with $0.000\%$ error (resolving the previous $0.415$ bias from naive Hankel fitting).
\item \textbf{Pure Dealer Edge Case ($K=0$):} In an economy with zero algorithmic learning, singular values truncate to order 0, identifying 0 transmission zeros and recovering strictly $0.0$ feedback weight.
\item \textbf{Multi-Cohort Recovery ($K=2$, $\eta \in \{0.45, 0.25\}$):} In the noiseless regime with well-separated rates, the estimator recovers poles $[0.5500, 0.7500]$ and both weights without model-order knowledge. This is the only multi-cohort case that succeeds.
\item \textbf{Per-Cohort Failure Under Noise or Close Rates:} Scoring each weight separately (tolerance $0.01$), rather than only their sum, shows that recovery fails outside that case. For $K=2$ at $\sigma=10^{-3}$ per-cohort recovery is $0/50$ for well-separated rates; for the $\delta=0.05$ pair $\eta\in\{0.35,0.45\}$ ($\Delta\eta=0.10$) it fails even without noise; for $K=3$ it fails even without noise. In the \texttt{exp2} grid ($K\in\{2,3,5\}$, $\sigma\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},5\times10^{-3}\}$) the per-cohort recovery rate is at most $0.07$ in every cell, while the earlier sum-of-weights metric looked accurate. A unit probe of $6.25$ BTC against price noise $\sigma=0.01$ gives effective impulse-response noise of about $0.0016$, inside the failure regime.
\item \textbf{Information-Theoretic Root Cause:} The difficulty of multi-cohort weight recovery is quantified by the local Fisher information geometry established in Proposition~\ref{prop:ia_cohort_information} and Table~\ref{iatab:cohort_information}. For close rates ($\Delta\eta \le 0.10$), the Fisher information matrix condition number grows as $\Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$, requiring between $40{,}374$ and $2.6\times 10^6$ independent reset probes for any regular, asymptotically unbiased estimator to achieve standard error $\le 0.01 / 1.96$. This establishes $N^* = 40{,}374$ as a rigorous CRB-based local sample-size benchmark for unbiased estimation under the 121-period impulse observation contract; under ordinary surveillance budgets ($N \le 50$), regular unbiased estimators cannot resolve close individual cohort weights. In the noiseless limit, Maximum Likelihood resolves the $\Delta\eta=0.10$ pair and $K=3$ ($\Delta\eta=0.20$) where the ERA estimator fails (max error $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ and $1.1 \times 10^{-4}$), and at the critical noise level ML achieves the Cram\'er--Rao lower bound ($\text{RMSE}/\text{CRB} \in [0.91, 1.16]$ across $n_{\mathrm{mc}} = 100$ trials per cell).
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Cram\'er--Rao Local Information Benchmark for Multi-Cohort Weight Recovery]
\label{prop:ia_cohort_information}
Let $\theta = (w_1, \dots, w_K, \eta_1, \dots, \eta_K)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{2K}$ denote the structural weights and adaptation rates of a $K$-cohort adaptive feedback economy with closed-loop impulse response $h_t(\theta)$ ($t=0, \dots, T$). Suppose the econometrician observes $h_t$ with iid Gaussian estimation noise of standard deviation $\sigma_h = \sigma_p / (V_{\mathrm{probe}} \sqrt{N})$, generated by $N$ independent reset probes of size $V_{\mathrm{probe}}$ against innovation noise $\sigma_p$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Fisher Information Matrix and Cram\'er--Rao Bound:} The Fisher information matrix is $F(\theta) = \frac{1}{\sigma_h^2} J^\top J$, where $J_{tj} = \frac{\partial h_t(\theta)}{\partial \theta_j}$. For any regular, asymptotically unbiased estimator $\hat{w}_k$, the variance obeys the Cram\'er--Rao lower bound:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}(\hat{w}_k) \ge [F^{-1}(\theta)]_{kk} = \sigma_h^2 [J^\top J]^{-1}_{kk}.
\end{equation}
To achieve target standard error $\mathrm{SE}(\hat{w}_k) \le \mathrm{tol} / 1.96$ (corresponding to $95\%$ confidence interval width $\pm \mathrm{tol}$, with $\mathrm{tol} = 0.01$), the required number of independent reset cycles is:
\begin{equation}
N^* \ge \left( \frac{1.96}{\mathrm{tol}} \right)^2 \left( \frac{\sigma_p}{V_{\mathrm{probe}}} \right)^2 [F_{\mathrm{unit}}^{-1}(\theta)]_{kk},
\end{equation}
where $F_{\mathrm{unit}} = J^\top J$.
\item \textbf{High-Order Inversion Scaling:} For $K=2$ cohorts with rate separation $\Delta\eta = |\eta_1 - \eta_2|$, the joint Fisher information matrix inversion satisfies $[F_{\mathrm{unit}}^{-1}]_{kk} = \Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ (kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{cramer_rao_two_cohort_inverse_sixth_scaling}), yielding:
\begin{equation}
N^*(\Delta\eta) = \Theta\left( (\Delta\eta)^{-6} \right).
\end{equation}
\item \textbf{Critical Noise Floor and Attainability:} For the benchmark $\delta=0.05$ pair ($\eta \in \{0.35, 0.45\}$, $\Delta\eta = 0.10$), the critical closed-loop noise floor below which $95\%$ confidence is achievable with a single probe is $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}} = 7.96 \times 10^{-6}$, requiring $N^* = 40{,}374$ reset probes. The operational single-probe noise $\sigma_h(1) = 0.01 / 6.25 = 1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ exceeds $\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ by a factor of $200.9\times$. For $\Delta\eta = 0.05$, $N^* = 2{,}620{,}320$ probes ($\sigma_h(1)/\sigma_{\mathrm{crit}} = 1{,}618.7\times$). When observations satisfy $\sigma_h \le \sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}$ for well-conditioned separations ($\Delta\eta \ge 0.10$ for $K=2$, $\Delta\eta \ge 0.20$ for $K=3$), Maximum Likelihood achieves the Cram\'er--Rao bound ($\operatorname{RMSE}/\mathrm{CRB} \in [0.91, 1.16]$, empirical $95\%$ CI coverage $87\%$--$99\%$, and $100\%$ blind BIC selection over $K \in \{0,1,2,3,4\}$ across $n_{\mathrm{mc}} = 100$ trials), resolving the transmission-zero inversion defect.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

While Proposition~\ref{prop:ia_cohort_information} establishes the $\Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ sample-complexity barrier for regular, asymptotically unbiased estimators, a natural econometric question is whether biased, ridge-regularized ($L_2$ Tikhonov), James--Stein shrinkage, or Bayesian MAP estimators can escape this scaling. Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_minimax_lecam} resolves this globally and non-asymptotically via an exact moment-matched Le Cam two-point construction paired with a matching non-asymptotic sub-Gaussian cubic moment-inversion upper bound.

\begin{theorem}[Global Non-Asymptotic Le Cam Two-Point Minimax Lower Bound for Arbitrary Biased and Shrinkage Estimators]
\label{thm:ia_minimax_lecam}
Fix total cohort weight $q = 0.20$, mean adaptation rate $\bar{\eta} = 0.40$, target weight separation $\tau = 0.01$, and admissible cohort rate spacing $\Delta\eta \in (0, \min(\bar{\eta}/p_+, (1-\bar{\eta})/p_+)) = (0, 8/11)$ (so every cohort adaptation rate lies strictly inside the open stability interval $\eta_{i,k} \in (0, 1)$, whereas $\Delta\eta = 8/11 \approx 0.7273$ reaches the boundary $\eta_{0,1} = 0$). Define $p_- \equiv \frac{1}{2} - \frac{\tau}{q} = 0.45$ and $p_+ \equiv \frac{1}{2} + \frac{\tau}{q} = 0.55$, and construct the unrescaled two-cohort parameter pair:
\begin{equation}
\theta_0 = (q p_-,\, q p_+,\, \bar{\eta} - p_+ \Delta\eta,\, \bar{\eta} + p_- \Delta\eta), \qquad \theta_1 = (q p_+,\, q p_-,\, \bar{\eta} - p_- \Delta\eta,\, \bar{\eta} + p_+ \Delta\eta).
\label{eq:ia_lecam_pair}
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact Moment-Matching and Cubic Divergence Identity:} Both parameter vectors in \eqref{eq:ia_lecam_pair} have identical cohort spacing $\eta_{i,2} - \eta_{i,1} = \Delta\eta$, exact $\ell_\infty$ weight separation $\|w(\theta_1) - w(\theta_0)\|_\infty = 2\tau = 0.02$, and identical first three open-loop rate moments $M_m(\theta_0) = M_m(\theta_1)$ for $m \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ ($M_m(\theta) \equiv \sum_{k=1}^2 w_k \eta_k^m$), while their third moment differs by the exact cubic polynomial identity (kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{lecam_two_point_moment_matched_cubic_gap}):
\begin{equation}
M_3(\theta_1) - M_3(\theta_0) = 4 \tau p_- p_+ (\Delta\eta)^3 = 0.0099 \, (\Delta\eta)^3.
\end{equation}
Consequently, the closed-loop impulse-response distance scales as $\|h(\theta_1) - h(\theta_0)\|_2 = \Theta(\tau (\Delta\eta)^3)$.
\item \textbf{Universal Non-Asymptotic Minimax Lower Bound and Matching $\Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ Upper Bound:} For any measurable estimator $\hat{w} : \mathbb{R}^{(T+1) \times N} \to \mathbb{R}^2$ (including arbitrary biased, ridge/Tikhonov, James--Stein shrinkage, or Bayesian MAP estimators), the worst-case failure probability and $\ell_\infty$ mean-squared error obey Le Cam's two-point testing inequality:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\sup_{\theta \in \Theta(\Delta\eta)} \mathbb{P}_\theta\!\left( \|\hat{w} - w(\theta)\|_\infty \ge \tau \right) &\ge 1 - \Phi\!\left( \frac{\sqrt{N}\, \|h(\theta_1) - h(\theta_0)\|_2}{2 \sigma_h} \right), \\
\sup_{\theta \in \Theta(\Delta\eta)} \mathbb{E}_\theta\!\left[ \|\hat{w} - w(\theta)\|_\infty^2 \right] &\ge \tau^2 \left[ 1 - \Phi\!\left( \frac{\sqrt{N}\, \|h(\theta_1) - h(\theta_0)\|_2}{2 \sigma_h} \right) \right].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
To achieve worst-case failure probability $\le \delta_{\mathrm{fail}} = 0.05$, every measurable estimator requires at least $N \ge N^*_{\mathrm{LeCam}}(\Delta\eta) \equiv \frac{4\sigma_h^2 (z_{1-\delta_{\mathrm{fail}}})^2}{\|h(\theta_1) - h(\theta_0)\|_2^2} = \Omega((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ independent reset probes (empirical log-log slope $-6.064$ for the literal pair \eqref{eq:ia_lecam_pair} and $-6.021$ under closed-loop curvature refinement). Conversely, we pair this unconditional global lower bound with two constructive $\mathcal{O}((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ upper-bound analyses (\texttt{P0-3}, implemented as \nolinkurl{blind_multilag_prony_estimator} and \nolinkurl{analytical_prony_hankel_conditioning_bound} in \nolinkurl{exp17_minimax_lecam_bound.py}): (i)~on the known-midpoint family, projecting the empirical impulse response $\bar{h}_N$ onto the normalized third-moment functional $u_3 \equiv \nabla_{M_3} h / \|\nabla_{M_3} h\|_2$ and inverting the cubic moment identity via the linear estimator $\hat{w}_{\mathrm{mom}}$ satisfies the exact global Lipschitz error bound $\|\hat{w}_{\mathrm{mom}} - w(\theta)\|_\infty \le |\hat{M}_3 - M_3(\theta)| / (2 p_- p_+ (\Delta\eta)^3)$ (kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{moment_estimator_cubic_inversion_stability}), yielding $N^*_{\mathrm{upper}}(\Delta\eta; 1-\delta_{\mathrm{fail}}) = \frac{2\sigma_h^2 \ln(2/\delta_{\mathrm{fail}})}{\tau^2 p_-^2 p_+^2 \|\nabla_{M_3} h\|_2^2 (\Delta\eta)^6} = \mathcal{O}(\tau^{-2}(\Delta\eta)^{-6}\ln(1/\delta_{\mathrm{fail}}))$ ($109.2$ resets at $\Delta\eta = 0.30$ and $87{,}892.3$ resets at $\Delta\eta = 0.10$, empirical log-log slope $-6.070$); and (ii)~on the separated non-degenerate sub-class $\Theta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\Delta\eta) \equiv \{\theta : w_k \ge w_{\min} > 0, \eta_k \in [\eta_{\min}, \eta_{\max}] \subset (0, 1), |\eta_2 - \eta_1| = \Delta\eta\}$, without prior knowledge of $\bar{\eta}$, $\Delta\eta$, $q$, or $p_\pm$, applying triangular Toeplitz deconvolution $\bar{h}_N \mapsto \hat{g}$ ($H(z) = \kappa / (z - a - \kappa G(z))$), solving the overdetermined multi-lag Hankel Prony polynomial for the latent poles $\hat{r}_k = 1 - \hat{\eta}_k$, and solving the resulting Vandermonde system for $(\hat{w}_1, \hat{w}_2)$ achieves noiseless recovery error $2.73 \times 10^{-15}$ ($\Delta\eta = 0.30$) to $8.76 \times 10^{-15}$ ($\Delta\eta = 0.10$). On $\Theta_{\mathrm{sep}}(\Delta\eta)$, the $2 \times 2$ Prony--Hankel matrix $H_0 = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} g_1 & g_2 \\ g_2 & g_3 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has exact positive determinant $\det(H_0) = g_1 g_3 - g_2^2 = w_1 w_2 \eta_1 \eta_2 (\Delta\eta)^2 \ge w_{\min}^2 \eta_{\min}^2 (\Delta\eta)^2 > 0$ (kernel-checked in Lean~4 inside \nolinkurl{prony_blind_four_moment_uniform_upper_bound}); combining this exact Hankel determinant lower bound with first-order perturbation/conditioning analysis of the nonlinear Prony root-and-Vandermonde map yields a local analytical conditioning diagnostic $N^*_{\mathrm{Prony,unif}}(\Delta\eta)$ with exact log-log slope $-6.000000 = \mathcal{O}((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ and a local Jacobian upper bound $N^*_{\mathrm{Prony,blind}}(\Delta\eta)$ with log-log slope $-6.055$ (achieving $1.2\% \le 5\%$ empirical Monte Carlo failure rate at $\Delta\eta = 0.30$, $N = 13{,}200$). The diagnostic labelled ``unif does not establish a uniform finite-sample guarantee for the parameter-blind nonlinear estimator: estimated-pole perturbations and admissibility require additional control. Together, the unconditional global Le Cam lower bound, the global known-midpoint cubic-moment upper bound, and the local Prony--Hankel perturbation bound characterize the $\Theta((\Delta\eta)^{-6})$ scaling (derived directly from $\Delta M_3 = \frac{99}{10000}(\Delta\eta)^3$ and kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{minimax_sample_complexity_theta_d_eta_minus_six}).
\item \textbf{Literal Analytic Pair vs.\ Rate-Refined Pair and Shrinkage Benchmark:} At $\Delta\eta = 0.10$ ($\delta = 0.05$), the literal moment-matched pair \eqref{eq:ia_lecam_pair} has $\|\Delta h_{\mathrm{an}}\|_2 = 6.837 \times 10^{-5}$ and requires $N^*_{\mathrm{LeCam,an}}(95\%) = 5{,}927.6$ ($\lceil N^* \rceil = 5{,}928$) resets, while closed-loop curvature refinement (which adjusts $\eta_1$ by $\mathcal{O}((\Delta\eta)^2)$ to reduce $\|\Delta h_{\mathrm{ref}}\|_2$ to $6.700 \times 10^{-5}$, introducing small non-zero open-loop gaps $\Delta M_1 = -8.60\times 10^{-6}$ and $\Delta M_2 = -6.88\times 10^{-6}$) yields $N^*_{\mathrm{LeCam,ref}}(95\%) = 6{,}172.2$ ($\lceil N^* \rceil = 6{,}173$; $8{,}764$ at $97.5\%$). At ordinary surveillance budget $N = 50$, the estimator-independent minimax lower bound on worst-case failure probability is $44.00\%$ (literal pair) and $44.12\%$ (refined pair), and empirical worst-case failure rates across $1{,}000$ Monte Carlo trials are $94.1\%$ (Unregularized ML), $87.0\%$ (Ridge/Tikhonov), $91.3\%$ (James--Stein $\gamma=0.35$), and $79.5\%$ (Heavy Prior Shrinkage $\gamma=0.75$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Causal Identification via Multi-Agent Policy-Freeze Intervention}
\label{ia:causal_multi_agent}

A fundamental critique of observational market microstructure literature is that empirical price-volume correlations cannot identify whether observed return predictability and tail volatility originate from algorithmic machine learning adaptation, ordinary trade execution (such as TWAP/VWAP order splitting), liquidity provision, or human behavioral feedback.

To establish causal attribution, we deploy a paired microstructure simulation with an event-sourced double-entry dealer ledger. We execute 100 independent random seeds sharing identical precomputed streams of fundamental price shocks and noise flow across four strictly controlled market conditions:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Condition $P_0$ (Baseline Noise Traders):} Market participants execute independent zero-feedback order flow.
\item \textbf{Condition $P_2^{\mathrm{frozen}}$ (Counterfactual Policy Freeze):} Algorithmic agents run a fixed non-adaptive policy without dynamic online state adaptation ($\eta = 0$).
\item \textbf{Condition $P_2^{\mathrm{active}}$ (Active Adaptive Learning):} Agents run identical initial policies but update internal memory dynamically from price changes.
\item \textbf{Condition $P_1$ (Persistent Flow Benchmark):} Non-learning execution algorithms split parent orders via an autoregressive process with $\rho = 0.85$, matching empirical order flow persistence.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Information-Disclosure Value Frontier and Regulatory Substitutability}
\label{ia:disclosure_frontier}

Because active market probing imposes significant execution costs and risks inventory lockup, we formalize the regulatory disclosure frontier. We evaluate the minimum capital required to achieve statistical identification under six observation contracts:
\begin{itemize}
\item $I_0$ (Public Tape Only): The observer observes noisy transaction prices $p_t$.
\item $I_1$ (Public Prices and Aggregated Flow): The observer observes $p_t$ and executed flow $Q_t$.
\item $I_2$ (Prices and Coarse Balance Sheet Disclosures): One independent noisy report of dealer marked equity per reset ($\sigma_E = 0.50$, Proposition 2.8).
\item $I_3$ (Prices and Audited Equity Marks): Exact, infinite-precision marked equity reported at session close (Proposition 2.8); we also report the case of reports rounded to $0.01$ USDT.
\item $I_4$ (Algorithmic Update Timestamps): Exact disclosure of the time intervals during which algorithms modify parameters. Timestamps do not enter any observable likelihood in our model, so we assign them no information value.
\item $I_5$ (Full Supervisory Audit Registry): Continuous regulatory access to internal algorithm state logs.
\end{itemize}

Under that model $I_2$ needs $267$ resets ($4{,}241.75$ USDT) and saves only $1.5\%$ against the single-tap benchmark, and nothing against the $T=20$ price design. Algorithmic update timestamps ($I_4$) do not enter the likelihood of any observable in our model, so they add no information and no saving is established without an external structural link between parameter-update times and price innovations (for example, a two-pulse design based on an unmodelled $7.32\times$ information multiplier suffers a $24.4\%$ price-only testing error). The audited-equity rows ($I_3$) assume a noiseless, infinite-precision report and a known accounting map from inventory to equity. The equity difference between hypotheses is $\Delta E=\kappa^2 q\delta^2V^2$, which is $8\times10^{-7}$ USDT at $V=0.05$ BTC; if reports are rounded to $0.01$ USDT, a guaranteed change requires $V\ge5.59$ BTC and $15.52$ USDT, so the Binance and CME-micro figures hold only under infinite precision. The flow contract ($I_1$) depends on an assumed flow-noise level $\sigma_q=0.05$ and gives no saving at $\delta=0.05$. Finally, supervisory audit registry access ($I_5$) requires zero trading volume ($V=0$) and zero capital ($C=0.00$), achieving passive verification without active market intervention.

\subsection{Coordinate-Weighted Output Sensitivity and Multi-Asset Cross-Impact}
\label{ia:nonnormal_decoupling}

We now examine output-weighted amplification in the closed-loop price system. The state used in \texttt{exp12\_nonnormal\_balance\_sheet.py} is $z_t=(x_t,\,y_t)^\top$, where $x_t$ is the price deviation and $y_t$ the algorithmic memory, evolving as
\begin{equation}
z_{t+1} = J z_t, \qquad J = \begin{pmatrix} a & \kappa q \\ \eta a & 1-\eta+\eta\kappa q \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
with $a=0.5$, $\kappa=0.8$, $q=0.2$, $\eta=0.3$. The closed-loop system is spectrally stable ($\rho(J)=0.8224<1$) and $J$ is non-normal. Here the second coordinate $y_t$ is algorithmic memory rather than physical dealer inventory. \emph{The state $z_t$ contains no dealer inventory, cash or equity.}

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate-Weighted Output Sensitivity]
\label{prop:balance_sheet_decoupling}
Let $C_{\mathrm{price}} = (1, 0)$ and let $C_w \equiv (1, \lambda/\mathrm{MMR}) = (1,160)$ with $\lambda=0.8$ and $\mathrm{MMR}=0.005$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $M_{C_{\mathrm{price}}} \equiv \sup_{t \ge 0} \|C_{\mathrm{price}} J^t\|_2 = 1.0$, so the price output has no transient amplification.
\item $M_{C_w} \equiv \sup_{t \ge 0} \|C_w J^t\|_2 = \|C_w\|_2 = 160.003$, attained at $t=0$, and it decays monotonically thereafter ($122.319$, $98.322$, $80.793$ at $t=1,2,3$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The factor $160$ is the static coordinate weight $\lambda/\mathrm{MMR}$, not dynamic transient growth. Because the state $z_t$ contains no physical inventory, $C_w z_t$ represents a coordinate-weighted state functional rather than an account margin calculation: for example $z=(0,0.005)$ gives $C_w z=0.8$ with zero holdings. Explicit dealer margin and liquidation must instead be evaluated on a double-entry cash-and-inventory ledger, as we carry out in Section~\ref{ia:crash_free_guard}.

\subsection{Observable-Data Bridge and Spurious Feedback Orthogonalization}
\label{ia:observable_bridge}

Finally, we address the identification challenge in empirical market microstructure using observational trade records. Suppose an empirical researcher seeks to test whether signed order flow $q_t$ adapts to past price movements via the structural memory model:
\begin{equation}
q_t = \gamma y_t + u_t, \qquad y_t = (1 - \eta) y_{t-1} + \eta \Delta p_t,
\end{equation}
where $u_t$ represents exogenous execution order flow (e.g., algorithmic TWAP/VWAP order splitting). When execution flow exhibits temporal persistence:
\begin{equation}
u_t = \rho u_{t-1} + \epsilon_t, \qquad \rho = 0.80,
\end{equation}
and price changes reflect market impact $\Delta p_t = \kappa q_{t-1} + \xi_{t-1}$, lagged price movements are mechanically correlated with past execution flow $u_{t-1}$. Because $u_t$ is autocorrelated with $u_{t-1}$, raw returns are endogenous with respect to the disturbance $u_t$. Consequently, standard naive OLS regression of $q_t$ on lagged return $r_{t-1}$ produces a severely inflated estimate $\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{OLS}} = 0.702$ ($t = 18.64$, $p < 10^{-15}$), generating a false discovery rate of 100\% at the nominal 5\% level in markets where traders follow strictly zero feedback ($\gamma = 0$).

To eliminate this confounder, we apply the Cochrane--Orcutt quasi-differencing transformation:
\begin{equation}
q_t - \rho q_{t-1} = \gamma (y_t - \rho y_{t-1}) + \epsilon_t.
\end{equation}
In the no-learning baseline ($\gamma = 0$), regressing the orthogonalized flow innovation $q_t^\perp \equiv q_t - \rho q_{t-1}$ onto quasi-differenced returns purges the serial correlation in the disturbance, yielding $\hat{\beta}^\perp = -0.0002$ ($t = -0.01$, $p = 0.98$) and restoring empirical size control (7.5\% empirical false-positive rate across 200 trials, within standard binomial error of nominal 5.0\%).

Crucially, in an economy with genuine positive feedback ($\gamma = 0.20$), regressing flow on raw single-period returns $r_{t-1}$ produces an attenuated regression slope $\hat{\beta}_{\mathrm{RF}} = 0.0208$, reflecting the exponential memory half-life ($y_t$ scales by $\approx \eta$). In contrast, when the econometrician projects the quasi-differenced flow innovation onto the filtered memory state $\hat{y}_t$, the structural feedback parameter is accurately recovered: $\hat{\gamma} = 0.1965$ (bias $-0.0035$). Furthermore, three specification sensitivity tests (\texttt{TC-E4}--\texttt{TC-E6} in \nolinkurl{independent_oracle_and_falsification.py}) confirm that: (i)~under a 3-cohort vs.\ 2-cohort state-space specification (\texttt{TC-E4}), the aligned impact estimate shifts by only $0.0014\%$ ($\hat{\kappa}_{2\text{-cohort}} = 0.79981$ vs.\ $\hat{\kappa}_{3\text{-cohort}} = 0.79982$) while BIC favors the parsimonious 2-cohort specification ($-22{,}044.4 < -22{,}036.1$); (ii)~parent-child order-splitting aggregation (\texttt{TC-E5}) across $100$~ms, $500$~ms ($2\times$), and $1{,}000$~ms ($5\times$) windows preserves strictly positive aligned impact ($\hat{\kappa}_{\mathrm{aligned}} \in [0.779, 0.971] > 0$); and (iii)~comparing event clock ($50$-trade buckets), volume clock ($25$-BTC buckets), and calendar clock ($1$-second bars) (\texttt{TC-E6}) confirms that the legacy misalignment sign reversal ($\hat{\kappa}_{\mathrm{aligned}} > +0.78$ vs.\ $\hat{\kappa}_{\mathrm{misaligned}} < -0.14$) holds invariant across all three observation clocks. This establishes a rigorous econometric bridge between reduced-form lag regressions and structural multi-agent feedback identification.

\subsection{Conditional Bounded-Shock Forward Solvency via Real-Time Resolvent Guarding}
\label{ia:crash_free_guard}

While Section~\ref{ia:nonnormal_decoupling} demonstrated that a state vector containing only price and algorithmic memory cannot directly represent dealer margin, a central normative question for exchange design is whether we can couple the multi-cohort AI state-space dynamics to an \emph{explicit double-entry dealer balance-sheet ledger} (tracking physical inventory $I_t$, cash $K_t$, linear-book execution fills, costly inventory-rebalancing spreads and fees, mark-to-market equity $E_t = K_t + I_t p_t$, and Binance USD$\circledS$-M Tier-2 maintenance margin $\mathrm{MM}_t = \max(0, 0.005|I_t|p_t - 50)$) and construct a real-time exchange admission controller that eliminates forced dealer liquidation within a declared bounded-shock domain across both super-critical ($\rho(J) > 1$) and spectrally stable but non-normal ($\rho(J) < 1$, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C}} \gg 1$) AI herding regimes. Theorem~\ref{thm:ia_crash_free_guard} establishes this conditional bounded-shock forward-solvency guarantee (along with an explicit out-of-domain falsification certificate), and Table~\ref{iatab:crash_free_ai_guard} and Figure~\ref{iafig:three_breakthrough_frontiers} report its empirical validation and 6-channel controller ablation across $400$ stress episodes, including $100$ real extreme L2 order-book shock windows replayed from January 2023 Binance BTCUSDT (\texttt{empirical\_pipeline/data/1-09-1-20.csv}).

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Bounded-Shock Forward Solvency of the Real-Time Resolvent Guard]
\label{thm:ia_crash_free_guard}
Consider a venue dealer with complete evolving account state $(K_t, I_t, p_t, z_t)$ at step $t$, initialized at $E_0 = 10{,}000$~USDT, structural inventory exposure $I_0 = -2{,}000$~units at reference price $P^* = 100$~USDT ($20\times$ notional leverage), cash $K_0 = E_0 - I_0 P^* = 210{,}000$~USDT, and initial Tier-2 maintenance-margin buffer $B_{\mathrm{rem},0} \equiv E_0 - \mathrm{MM}_0 = 9{,}050$~USDT (with target safety reserve $R_{\mathrm{safe}} = 950$~USDT, giving initial safe drawdown budget $B_{\mathrm{safe},0} = 8{,}100$~USDT). At any step $t$, let $E_t = K_t + I_t p_t$ denote mark-to-market equity, $\mathrm{MM}_t = \max(0, 0.005|I_t|p_t - 50)$ denote Tier-2 maintenance margin (margin rate $\mu = 0.005$), $B_{\mathrm{rem},t} \equiv E_t - \mathrm{MM}_t$ denote the current remaining margin buffer, $R_{\mathrm{safe},t} \equiv \min(R_{\mathrm{safe}}, 0.5\max(0, B_{\mathrm{rem},t}))$, $I_{t+1}(\alpha_t) \equiv I_t + \Delta I^{\mathrm{hedge}}_t(\alpha_t)$ denote post-hedge inventory, $C_{\mathrm{hedge},t}(\alpha_t) \equiv |\Delta I^{\mathrm{hedge}}_t(\alpha_t)|\bigl(\tfrac{s}{2} + \phi p_t\bigr)$ denote the step hedging execution cost ($\Delta I^{\mathrm{hedge}}_t(\alpha_t) = -0.06(1-\alpha_t)I_t$ when hedging is enabled, and $0$ under pure operator throttling), $\Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t) \equiv 12\alpha_t s(\alpha_t)\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t}$ denote the worst-case passive maker fill quantity absorbed at quoted depth $\alpha_t$ (which equals $0$ at $\alpha_t = 0$), $|I_{t+1}^{\mathrm{post\text{-}fill}}(\alpha_t)| \equiv |I_{t+1}(\alpha_t)| + \Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t)$ denote the worst-case post-hedge and post-fill inventory bound, and $C_{\mathrm{fill},t}(\alpha_t) \equiv \Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t)\max(0,\, \mu p_t - 0.02)$ denote the net post-fill maintenance-margin increment after deducting the $0.02$~USDT/unit passive maker half-spread revenue (at model values $\mu = 0.005, p_t = 100$~USDT, $\mu p_t - 0.02 = 0.48$~USDT/unit $> 0$). Let $J(\alpha) = A_0 + \alpha B F$ denote the closed-loop transition matrix when algorithmic feedback flow is scaled by admission factor $\alpha \in [0, 1]$, and define the price-output transient gain $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(\alpha) \equiv \max_{0 \le k \le H} \|c_{\mathrm{price}}^\top J(\alpha)^k\|_2$ and cumulative convolution gain $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{cum}}(\alpha) \equiv \sum_{k=0}^H \|c_{\mathrm{price}}^\top J(\alpha)^k\|_2$. Under a strictly \textbf{non-anticipative timing contract} (\texttt{TC-G3}, kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{non_anticipative_guard_action_invariance}), at the start of step $t$---prior to observing the exogenous disturbance realization $u_t$ and with zero post-shock override---the \textbf{Real-Time Resolvent Solvency Guard} ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$, implemented via \nolinkurl{select_non_anticipative_alpha} directly invoking \nolinkurl{evaluate_guard_inequalities_ia39}) evaluates the 41-point admission grid $\mathcal{A}_{41} = \{0, 0.025, \dots, 1.0\}$ using only the pre-shock state $(K_t, I_t, p_t, z_t)$ and declared shock envelope $(\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t}, \bar{u}_{\mathrm{cum},t})$, selecting the maximal $\alpha_t \in \mathcal{A}_{41}$ satisfying:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\rho(J(\alpha_t)) &\le 0.90, \\
|I_{t+1}^{\mathrm{post\text{-}fill}}(\alpha_t)| (1 + 0.005) \Bigl( \mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(\alpha_t)\|z_t\|_2 &+ \mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(\alpha_t)\, s(\alpha_t)\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t} \Bigr) \\
&\quad + C_{\mathrm{hedge},t}(\alpha_t) + C_{\mathrm{fill},t}(\alpha_t) + R_{\mathrm{safe},t} \le B_{\mathrm{rem},t}, \\
|I_{t+1}^{\mathrm{post\text{-}fill}}(\alpha_t)| (1 + 0.005) \Bigl( \mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(\alpha_t)\|z_t\|_2 &+ s(\alpha_t)\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{cum}}(\alpha_t)\bar{u}_{\mathrm{cum},t} \Bigr) \\
&\quad + C_{\mathrm{hedge},t}(\alpha_t) + C_{\mathrm{fill},t}(\alpha_t) + R_{\mathrm{safe},t} \le B_{\mathrm{rem},t},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule}
\end{equation}
where the intake shock-scaling factor is $s(\alpha) \equiv 1$ under pure operator throttling and $s(\alpha) \equiv 0.15 + 0.85\alpha$ when exchange intake shock gating is co-activated, so that the cumulative shock term $|I_{t+1}^{\mathrm{post\text{-}fill}}(0)|(1+0.005)s(0)\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{cum}}(0)\bar{u}_{\mathrm{cum},t} > 0$ remains strictly positive at $\alpha_t = 0$ (\texttt{TC-G1}, kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{resolvent_guard_cumulative_positive_at_zero_alpha}), the Tier-2 maintenance-margin factor $(1 + \mu) = 1.005$ and post-fill margin increment $C_{\mathrm{fill},t}(\alpha_t)$ enter both inequalities and the acceptance decision consistently (\texttt{TC-G2}, kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{resolvent_guard_maintenance_margin_tier2_consistency} and \nolinkurl{resolvent_guard_post_hedge_and_fill_margin_accounting}), and since $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(\alpha) \le \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{cum}}(\alpha)$, whenever $\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t} \le \bar{u}_{\mathrm{cum},t}$ the cumulative inequality strictly dominates the one-step inequality. If no candidate $\alpha \in \mathcal{A}_{41}$ satisfies \eqref{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule} at pre-shock state $(K_t, I_t, p_t, z_t)$, the controller sets $\alpha_t = 0$, explicitly flags \texttt{status = NO\_SAFE\_ACTION} (\texttt{pre\_infeasible = True}), and engages a protective quoting halt (\texttt{protective\_halt\_engaged = True}) that suspends active quoting and sets passive maker fill intake to zero ($\Delta I^{\mathrm{fill}}_t = 0$), never claiming a solvency guarantee on an infeasible state. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Norm-Bounded Worst-Case Sufficiency vs.\ Signed Pointwise Safety, Post-Hedge/Post-Fill Accounting, Multi-Step Recursive Feasibility, and Four-Category State Audit (\texttt{P0-4}):} At state $(K_t, I_t, p_t, z_t)$ with $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(0) = 1$, $C_{\mathrm{hedge},t}(0) = 0$, and $\Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(0) = C_{\mathrm{fill},t}(0) = 0$ at $\alpha_t = 0$, setting $\alpha_t = 0$ and $s(0)=1$ in \eqref{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule} yields the algebraically identical state-dependent norm-bounded admissibility thresholds:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},t} &\equiv \frac{B_{\mathrm{rem},t} - R_{\mathrm{safe},t} - |I_t|(1+0.005)\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(0)\|z_t\|_2}{|I_t|(1 + 0.005)}, \\
\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,liq},t} &\equiv \frac{B_{\mathrm{rem},t} - |I_t|(1+0.005)\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(0)\|z_t\|_2}{|I_t|(1 + 0.005)},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
which at initial state $z_0 = 0, |I_0| = 2{,}000, B_{\mathrm{rem},0} = 9{,}050$~USDT equal $\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},0} = \frac{8{,}100}{2{,}010} = \frac{270}{67} \approx 4.0299$~USDT and $\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,liq},0} = \frac{9{,}050}{2{,}010} = \frac{905}{201} \approx 4.5025$~USDT (for example, an adversarial move $d = 4.05$~USDT consumes $2{,}010 \times 4.05 = 8{,}140.5$~USDT plus $R_{\mathrm{safe}} = 950$~USDT $= 9{,}090.5 > 9{,}050$~USDT, violating \eqref{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule} and leaving $E_1 - \mathrm{MM}_1 = 909.5 < 950$~USDT). For any admitted depth $\alpha_t > 0$, passive maker order-flow absorption in the simulator (\texttt{flow\_absorbed = -12.0 * g\_star * shock\_scale * shocks[t, 0]}) equals $\Delta I^{\mathrm{fill}}_t = -12\alpha_t s(\alpha_t) w_{1,t}$ identically, matching the guard's certified bound $|\Delta I^{\mathrm{fill}}_t| \le \Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t) = 12\alpha_t s(\alpha_t)\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t}$ 1-for-1 (for example, at $\alpha_t = 0.35$ and $\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t} = 0.05$, both the guard and the simulator evaluate to $12 \times 0.35 \times 0.4475 \times 0.05 = 0.093975$~units), post-step inventory satisfies $|I_{t+1}| \le |I_{t+1}(\alpha_t)| + \Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t)$, and because at model values $\mu p_t = 0.005 \times 100 = 0.50$~USDT exceeds the maker half-spread $0.02$~USDT by $0.48$~USDT/unit ($0.02 \le \mu p_t$), both the post-fill price-move exposure $(1+\mu)\Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t)|\Delta p_{t+1}|$ and the net post-fill maintenance-margin surcharge $C_{\mathrm{fill},t}(\alpha_t) = \Delta I_{\max,t}^{\mathrm{fill}}(\alpha_t)(\mu p_t - 0.02)$ are included directly in the acceptance decision of \eqref{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule} (verified in \nolinkurl{evaluate_guard_inequalities_ia39} and kernel-checked in Lean~4 under the true premise $\text{half\_spread} \le \mu p_{\mathrm{mid}}$ as \nolinkurl{resolvent_guard_post_hedge_and_fill_margin_accounting}). The condition $\|B u_t\|_2 \le \bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},t}$ is a \textbf{sufficient condition} for worst-case single-step safety over the entire Euclidean disturbance ball $\mathcal{B}(\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},t})$ (and necessary and sufficient for \emph{adversarially signed} disturbances $-\operatorname{sign}(I_t)\Delta p_{t+1} \le \bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},t}$), \textbf{not} a direction-independent ``if and only if'' condition on $\|B u_t\|_2$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Signed-Shock Counterexample (\texttt{CE-15}) and Four-Category State Audit (\texttt{P0-4}):} Because single-step mark-to-market equity changes as $E_1 - E_0 = I_0 \Delta p_1$ at zero fill, shock direction relative to inventory $I_0 = -2{,}000$ determines solvency. Consider two disturbances of identical magnitude $|\Delta p_1| = 10$~USDT $> \bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},0} = 4.03$~USDT: a downward jump $\Delta p_1 = -10$~USDT ($p_1 = 90$~USDT) \emph{benefits} the short dealer, raising equity to $E_1 = 210{,}000 - 2{,}000(90) = 30{,}000$~USDT against $\mathrm{MM}_1 = 850$~USDT ($B_{\mathrm{rem},1} = 29{,}150$~USDT $\ge 950$~USDT, \textbf{Safe}), whereas an upward jump $\Delta p_1 = +10$~USDT ($p_1 = 110$~USDT) yields $E_1 = -10{,}000$~USDT $< \mathrm{MM}_1 = 1{,}050$~USDT (\textbf{Insolvent}). In \texttt{p04\_four\_category\_state\_audit}, we verify all four state categories: (1)~ordinary feasible states (\texttt{status = FEASIBLE}, $\alpha^*_0 = 0.45$, where guard certified max fill and simulator actual fill both equal $0.143775$~units and $0.093975$~units at the $\alpha=0.35$ witness), (2)~exact margin-boundary states ($\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step}} = 270/67$, $\mathrm{LHS} = B_{\mathrm{rem},0} = 9{,}050.0$~USDT, \texttt{status = FEASIBLE} at $\alpha^*_0 = 0.0$), (3)~pre-shock infeasible states where all 41 candidates fail \eqref{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule} ($B_{\mathrm{rem},t} = 550$~USDT, $\|z_t\|_2 = 0.866$, returning \texttt{pre\_infeasible = True}, $\alpha_t = 0.0$, \texttt{status = NO\_SAFE\_ACTION}, \texttt{protective\_halt\_engaged = True}, and zero passive fill intake $\Delta I^{\mathrm{fill}}_t = 0.0$), and (4)~out-of-domain realized shocks ($u_0 = (+10, 0, 0)^\top$ and $(+100, 0, 0)^\top$, flagging \texttt{status = NO\_SAFE\_ACTION}).
\item \emph{Multi-Step Recursive Feasibility:} Forward solvency across $t = 0, \dots, T-1$ is guaranteed conditionally on the recursive admissibility of the evolving state trajectory: at each step $t$ where \eqref{eq:ia_kreiss_guard_rule} admits a feasible $\alpha_t \in \mathcal{A}_{41}$, the single-step algebraic buffer bound $B_{\mathrm{rem},t} - (\mathrm{loss}_t + R_{\mathrm{safe},t}) \ge 0$ holds (kernel-checked in Lean~4 as \nolinkurl{kreiss_resolvent_guard_solvency_invariance}), and continuing solvency at step $t+1$ requires that the updated buffer $B_{\mathrm{rem},t+1} = E_{t+1} - \mathrm{MM}_{t+1}$ and state $z_{t+1}$ keep $\bar{u}_{\mathrm{adm,safe},t+1} \ge s(0)\bar{u}_{\mathrm{step},t+1}$.
\end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Structural Failure of Eigenvalue-Only Guards under Non-Normal AI Herding:} An eigenvalue-only risk monitor that enforces $\rho(J(\alpha_t)) \le 0.90$ (or $\rho(J(\alpha_t)) \le 0.95$; \texttt{TC-G4} verifies that both thresholds admit $\alpha_t \equiv 1$ in all non-normal regimes since $\rho(J) < 0.85$, yielding identical $100.0\%$ liquidation in Regimes~II and~IV and $0.0\%$ in Regimes~I and~III) without bounding the resolvent/transient gain $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}}(\alpha_t)$ suffers $100.0\%$ dealer liquidation in both synthetic non-normal cascades (Regime~II, $\rho(J) = 0.780 < 0.90$, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{C},\mathrm{price}} = 40.79$) and empirical January 2023 Binance BTCUSDT L2 shock replays (Regime~IV).
\item \textbf{Model Admission Eligibility ($\alpha_t \ge 0.05$) vs.\ Admitted Liquidity Depth:} Across all $400$ bounded-domain episodes spanning Regimes~I--IV, $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{Kreiss}}$ achieves $0.0\%$ liquidation and $100.0\%$ model admission eligibility ($\alpha_t \ge 0.05$, measuring controller non-halt status rather than observed exchange bid/ask quoting), while throttling admitted liquidity depth share $\bar{\alpha}_t$ from $100.0\%$ in Regime~I (benign, $0.0\%$ throttled flow) to $32.2\%$ in Regime~II ($67.8\%$ throttled flow), $37.5\%$ in Regime~III ($62.5\%$ throttled flow), and $35.5\%$ in Regime~IV ($64.5\%$ throttled flow). In contrast, a reactive circuit breaker ($\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{halt}}$) suffers $49.0\%$ liquidation in Regime~II and $39.0\%$ liquidation in Regime~IV while collapsing model admission eligibility to $6.3\%$--$8.3\%$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{2. Exact 10-Level L2 Book Walking (\nolinkurl{exp20_exact_l2_book_walking.py}).}
In continuous double auctions, large predatory or herding sweeps walk past the top of book ($\ell = 0$), invalidating static linear midpoint-slope approximations during liquidity droughts. Using all $10$ bid and ask price and volume levels (\texttt{bid\_p0..9}, \texttt{bid\_v0..9}, \texttt{ask\_p0..9}, \texttt{ask\_v0..9}) in the authenticated January 9--20, 2023 Binance BTCUSDT perpetual dataset (\texttt{1-09-1-20.csv}), we compute exact piecewise-linear order-book walking slippage:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Delta p_{\mathrm{L2}}(t, q) &= \sum_{\ell=0}^{9} \frac{\min\!\big(\max(q - V_{\ell-1,t},\,0),\, v_{\ell,t}\big)}{q}\bigl(p_{\ell,t} - m_t\bigr) \\

\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ia_l2_book_walking}
\end{equation}
where $V_{\ell,t} = \sum_{j=0}^\ell v_{j,t}$ is cumulative book depth through level $\ell$. As shown in Table~\ref{iatab:exact_l2_book_walking} and Panel~B of Figure~\ref{iafig:empirical_crisis_l2_8asset}, 90th-percentile 10-level L2 book-walking slippage exceeds the static midpoint-slope approximation by $2.75\times$--$3.51\times$ across order sizes $|q| \in \{1, 5, 10, 20, 50\}$ BTC.
