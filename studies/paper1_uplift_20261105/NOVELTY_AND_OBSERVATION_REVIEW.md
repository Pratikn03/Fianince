# Paper 1: novelty and observation review

Review date: 5 November 2026. This is an AI-assisted critical comparison, not an independent referee report or an assertion of exhaustive priority. Current paper: *What Prices Cannot Tell a Market Supervisor: The Cost of Identifying Trader Groups and the Value of Class Disclosure*. Read together with the current claim ledger and build receipt.

The defensible contribution is a model-specific connection between a latent trader-population inverse problem, financially paid experiments, and an executable class intervention. The mathematical ingredients and the general principle that decisions need less information than identification have substantial prior art. Stronger market or welfare claims require evidence not supplied by public position series.

## Recommended contribution statement

“In a positive linear feedback market with specified primitives and observation laws, we characterize the merged population response revealed by public prices and connect the difficulty of resolving nearby populations to executed quantity and funded probing. An explicit two-group construction shows that close public responses can require different class interventions. Under a verified tagged-flow experiment, a response allowance and a fixed action menu, we derive a conservative decision rule and evaluate the net value of acquiring information.”

This statement excludes an unproved all-population dominant-weight construction, universal strategic robustness, general equilibrium invariance and real-market welfare superiority. It does not assert that each component is individually novel. The combination still needs the authors' and readers' judgment about economic significance.

## Observation contracts: what is and is not validated

| Surplus, implementation cost, breach loss and acquisition bill | Explicit synthetic accounting | Net values are surplus units under assumed utility. They are not social welfare estimates, dollars or regulatory budget estimates. |

Binance documentation distinguishes the top-position ratio (positions of users in the top 20 percent by margin balance) from the top-account ratio (each qualifying account counted once). The all-account series overlaps the top subset. These are ratios with changing denominators and potentially changing membership, not disjoint signed flow streams or verified algorithm classes. See the [official market-data documentation](https://developers.binance.info/en/docs/catalog/core-trading-derivatives-trading-usd-s-m-futures/api/rest-api/market-data).

CFTC COT describes Tuesday open-interest positions generally released Friday. Classification follows predominant business purpose reported on Form 40 and reviewed by staff; the agency says it does not know the specific reasons for positions. Categories can change or gain/lose members. Consequently the public series cannot be interpreted as contemporaneous tagged executed flows or pure trading strategies. See the [official CFTC description](https://www.cftc.gov/es/node/128971).

The original stock–flow bridge is an accounting model. Applying it needs adjustments for valuation, transfers, liquidation, reporting lag and membership. Public prices minus an estimated fundamental proxy also introduce estimation error and potential endogeneity. Neither correlation nor a fitted persistence coefficient identifies an algorithm's update rule.

## New falsification evidence

`flow_falsification.py` retains six fixed synthetic cases with 20,000 replications per world. At the selected illustrative calibration, downward second-flow biases of 0.003 and 0.006 invalidate mean coverage but do not produce unsafe halts in this run. Bias 0.010 produces unsafe halts in the G world in all replications. Raising the action response to 0.030 while allowing only 0.005 produces unsafe halts in the R world in all replications. Swapping second-response labels makes the rule abstain in both worlds. These are targeted failures of the maintained contracts, not population error estimates or independent market validation.

The 60-case valid-contract grid finds 14 positive estimated acquisition values. The selected largest gain is 2.122094 surplus units; its grid-adjusted simultaneous Hoeffding interval is [1.135735, 3.108452]. This interval describes Monte Carlo error under the fixed synthetic model, not uncertainty in economic assumptions. Negative cases remain in the output. No superiority claim follows outside the declared menu and parameter grid.

## Publication judgment and remaining work

The two-group mechanism now has a more concrete information channel and economic account. The pricing/evasion overclaims have been replaced by conditional statements. The older conclusion and institutional-disclosure discussion have also been aligned with these limits: cancellation is a possible mechanism rather than an identified explanation, and an algorithm identifier does not automatically establish homogeneous dynamics. The unconfirmed all-authors approval statement has been removed. The closest research substantially limits claims of originality at the level of general identification rates, control without identification and financial exploration.

A complete proof-to-manuscript certification is still absent: audit each of the 36 numbered Paper 1 statements and its subparts against the actual typed declarations; do not reuse the old 23-statement integrated-paper registry as if it covered this source. Prioritize the passive-data divergence rate, stationary IV central limit argument, adaptive stopping hypotheses, spectral process-noise extension and finite-horizon Rayleigh–Hölder result. The all-K dominant-weight construction remains outside the demonstrated core. Reconciliation of the finance working papers with their final published versions and independent review by Pratik, Prashant and Sujan remain important. No statement here attests that those human reviews have occurred.
