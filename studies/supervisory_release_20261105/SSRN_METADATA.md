## Abstract field

What can prices reveal about trader groups, and when is additional disclosure worth its cost? In a linear feedback market, prices identify a merged distribution of trading responses; equal-memory groups remain inseparable, while nearby distinct groups can require sharply increasing data. We translate identification difficulty into sample, execution and capital requirements under explicit observation contracts. A two-group supervisory example separates an aggregate prudential constraint from a targeted halt decision: nearly indistinguishable price responses can imply different feasible actions. General-population extensions require a separately verified dominant-group gap. We replace an unrestricted strategic-response claim with a bounded-response condition and connect the example to information fees, intervention losses and decision errors. A reproducible synthetic accounting study exhibits both positive and negative values of paid information and failures under bias or underestimated responses. Existing position-report studies are descriptive diagnostics; they do not identify learning memory, validate the intervention model, or establish welfare gains. Proof review, the observation-to-market bridge and the precise incremental contribution remain explicit research obligations.
